\section{Finite three-dimensional driven packing and the modal normalization audit}
\label{hs:three-section}

This section replaces the unproved finite-extrusion step by a different
construction: finite inclusions with conical \emph{point} tips.  Small
positive contrast supplies a convenient uniform scattering inverse; it
does not remove the point singularity.  The material bounds and frequency
remain fixed.  The modal question is separated at the end.

\begin{theorem}[PROVED: finite three-dimensional driven point localization]
\label{hs:three-packing}
Fix $k>0$ and an upper bound $\varepsilon_+>1$.  There is a number
$\varepsilon_h\in(1,\varepsilon_+]$ that can be kept fixed in the
following construction.  Given any nonempty open patch $\mathcal P$ in
a plane, any finite $N$, and any $\ell,M>0$, there exist a compactly
supported binary dielectric $\varepsilon_r\in\{1,\varepsilon_h\}$
with $2N$ disjoint smooth inclusions and one unit-incoming-power
Maxwell illumination at frequency $k$ such that there are $N$
pairwise disjoint three-dimensional balls $Q_j$, of diameter less
than $\ell$, satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\overline Q_j,\,\text{one-sided}}|E|>M,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\text{a collar of }\partial Q_j,\,\text{one-sided}}|E|
 <\tfrac12\max_{\overline Q_j,\,\text{one-sided}}|E|.
 \label{hs:three-peak}
\end{equation}
The centers of the balls lie in $\mathcal P$, and the orthogonal
projections of the balls and of the material support are contained
in $\mathcal P$.  In particular the selected superlevel components
are localized in all three spatial dimensions.  The maximizing
points after rounding need not lie exactly in the original plane.
There is no invariant infinite direction, no internal impressed
source, and no conducting boundary in the physical device.
\end{theorem}

The prescribed patch is therefore a projection constraint, while the
localized neighborhoods themselves are three-dimensional.  This exact
quantifier avoids silently replacing a point by an infinitely long edge.
As before, no minimum energy fraction in an individual peak is required.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: finite-device rounding continuity]
\label{hs:three-rounding}
Let $\chi_\rho,\chi$ be supported in the common comparison ball,
$0\leq\chi_\rho,\chi\leq d$, with $\chi_\rho\to\chi$ almost
everywhere.  For one fixed incident field, their total electric
fields satisfy $E_\rho\to E$ in $L^2(B_L)^3$.
On a fixed compact observation set avoiding all vertices and joining
curves, and near which the coefficients are unchanged, convergence
is uniform on each closed material side.  This includes fixed
sphere collars intersecting smooth conical interfaces.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Subtract the volume equations:
\begin{equation}
 E_\rho-E=(I-\mathcal K_k\chi_\rho)^{-1}
                  \mathcal K_k((\chi_\rho-\chi)E).
 \label{hs:three-rounding-identity}
\end{equation}
Dominated convergence makes the last multiplication tend to zero
in $L^2$, and Lemma~\ref{hs:three-volume} bounds both operators
uniformly.  This proves the first claim.

For the second, the field difference solves the same homogeneous
Maxwell equation in every fixed observation neighborhood.
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-decomposition} produces
$E_\rho-E=\nabla v_\rho+W_\rho$ there, with
$\|v_\rho\|_{H^1}+\|W_\rho\|_{H^2}\to0$ on smaller balls.
The potential satisfies
$\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon_r\nabla v_\rho)
=-\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon_r W_\rho)$.
Across a fixed smooth interface this is the scalar positive
transmission problem with continuous potential and conormal jump
$-[\varepsilon_r W_\rho\cdot n]$.
Its local piecewise $H^3$ estimate is bounded by
$C(\|v_\rho\|_{H^1}+\|W_\rho\|_{H^2})$.
To see the required regularity, flatten the fixed smooth interface,
apply tangential difference quotients twice to the scalar weak
equation, and use ellipticity and cutoffs to estimate them.
The equation then determines second normal derivatives; differentiating
it once determines third normal derivatives.  The coefficient and
flattening derivatives are bounded on this fixed neighborhood.
The two material-side estimates include the displayed conormal
data, whose trace has the required $H^{3/2}$ regularity from
$W_\rho\in H^2$.  Thus
$\nabla v_\rho+W_\rho\to0$ piecewise in $H^2$.
Since $H^2$ embeds in $C^{0,\gamma}$ in three dimensions,
uniform one-sided convergence follows.  A finite cover treats the
observation set.  No estimate uniform at the shrinking tip is used.
The same local argument gives bounded one-sided electric fields
for each fixed smooth rounded geometry.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
The rounded coefficients converge almost everywhere with fixed
support and the same positive material bound.  Apply
Lemmas~\ref{ms:fredholm}--\ref{ms:normalization} and choose the
square-root scale converging to the sharp field's scale.
The volume representation supplies local $L^2$ convergence also
on any larger fixed ball, by the same static and compact estimates.
For local regularity write $U_\rho=E_\rho-E$.  Its equation is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}U_\rho
 =k_\rho^2\varepsilon_\rho U_\rho
 +(k_\rho^2\varepsilon_\rho-k_*^2\varepsilon)E.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side tends to zero in local $L^2$ and $U_\rho$
does likewise.  Cutoff testing gives
$\operatorname{curl}U_\rho\to0$ in smaller interior $L^2$.
The constant-coefficient curl/div estimate for
$\operatorname{curl}U_\rho$ then gives convergence in interior
$H^1$.  The fixed regularized curl lifting gives a difference
$W_\rho-W\to0$ in $H^2$, and the corresponding mean-fixed
potentials converge in $H^1$.
On a neighborhood disjoint from the changing tips the coefficients
are identical.  The scalar equation for the potential difference
is $\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon\nabla(v_\rho-v))
=-\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon(W_\rho-W))$.
The piecewise $H^3$ scalar transmission estimate across a fixed
smooth positive interface, used in
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-rounding}, gives convergence of the potentials
in smaller piecewise $H^3$ sets.  Hence the electric fields converge
piecewise in $H^2$, and uniformly by the three-dimensional Sobolev
embedding.  At ordinary interior points the same conclusion follows
from the homogeneous interior estimates.
\end{proof}
