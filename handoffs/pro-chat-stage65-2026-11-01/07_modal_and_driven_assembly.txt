\section{Analytic excitation of conical points by one genuine resonance}
\label{hs:modal-next-section}

This section proves a local bridge that the driven-field construction
does not provide: a single analytically continued Maxwell resonance
retains a nonzero electric singular coefficient at every member of a
finite family of newly inserted conical points.  The contrast of the
insertions may depend on that finite family; the bounds
$1\leq\varepsilon_r\leq\varepsilon_+$ do not change.  A resonance here
has a complex frequency and satisfies the analytically continued
outgoing condition.  It is not a real-frequency driven solution.

\subsection{The angular branch through a homogeneous medium}

Fix an axis $e\in S^2$, a number $t\in(1/\sqrt3,1)$, and put
\begin{equation}
 a(\widehat x)=\mathbf1_{\{|\widehat x\cdot e|>t\}},\qquad
 q_\eta(\widehat x)=1+\eta a(\widehat x).
 \label{hs:modal-angular-material}
\end{equation}
The physical permittivity near a selected point is
$\varepsilon_b q_\eta$, where $1<\varepsilon_b<\varepsilon_+$ is the
permittivity of its initially homogeneous host.  The small parameter
$\eta$ is real and positive for the physical construction.  Its
complex extension below is only an analytic perturbation parameter.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: an analytic angular eigenbranch]
\label{hs:modal-angular-branch}
For sufficiently small complex $\eta$ there are holomorphic functions
$\nu_\eta$, $\lambda_\eta$, and
$\phi_\eta\in H^1(S^2)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 -\operatorname{div}_{S^2}(q_\eta\nabla_{S^2}\phi_\eta)
       =\nu_\eta q_\eta\phi_\eta,\qquad
 \nu_\eta=\lambda_\eta(\lambda_\eta+1),\qquad
 \int_{S^2}q_\eta\phi_\eta^2\,dS=1.
 \label{hs:modal-angular-branch-equation}
\end{equation}
The functions $\phi_\eta$ are axisymmetric about $e$ and odd under
$\widehat x\cdot e\mapsto-\widehat x\cdot e$.  In particular their
weighted means vanish.  With the continuous choice of sign,
\begin{equation}
 \phi_0=\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\,\widehat x\cdot e,
 \quad \nu_0=2,\quad \lambda_0=1,
 \quad \nu'_0=-3t(1-t^2),\quad
 \lambda'_0=-t(1-t^2).
 \label{hs:modal-angular-derivative}
\end{equation}
For sufficiently small real $\eta>0$, all these quantities are real
and $0<\lambda_\eta<1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in the closed subspace of axisymmetric odd functions in
$H^1(S^2)$.  At $\eta=0$, the eigenvalue two is simple in this
subspace: the spherical harmonics in it have degrees
$l=1,3,5,\ldots$, each with azimuthal index zero.  The stiffness and
mass forms have a common domain and depend affinely on $\eta$.
On this odd subspace the stiffness form is coercive at zero, and
remains invertible for small complex $\eta$ by a Neumann series.
Composing its inverse with the mass form gives a holomorphic compact
operator on $H^1$, compactness following from
$H^1(S^2)\hookrightarrow L^2(S^2)$.
A small contour about its simple eigenvalue $1/2$ defines a
holomorphic rank-one spectral projection.  Apply that projection to
$\phi_0$ and divide by the analytic square root of its nonzero
bilinear mass.  This constructs the displayed normalized eigenpair;
the analytic square root of $1+4\nu_\eta$ selects
$\lambda_\eta=(-1+\sqrt{1+4\nu_\eta})/2$ near one.
Because $q_\eta$ is axisymmetric and even, the restricted weak
eigenproblem is also the full-sphere weak eigenproblem.  Orthogonal
angular symmetry sectors make the other test functions vanish.

Differentiating the normalized eigenproblem at zero gives
\begin{equation}
 \nu'_0=\int_{S^2}a
       \bigl(|\nabla_{S^2}\phi_0|^2-2\phi_0^2\bigr)\,dS
       =3\int_t^1(1-3s^2)\,ds=-3t(1-t^2).
\end{equation}
Differentiation of $\nu=\lambda(\lambda+1)$ gives
$\lambda'_0=\nu'_0/3$.  Reality and the strict inequality follow
by conjugation uniqueness and this negative derivative.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A coefficient formula regular at the exponent one}

The apparent difficulty at $\eta=0$ is the integral
$\int_0^r s^{-\lambda_\eta}\,ds$ in the earlier coefficient
formula.  Its singular denominator is removable after one subtracts
the value of the regular curl lifting at the vertex.  The following
argument carries out that subtraction before taking the limit.

\begin{theorem}[PROVED: analytic coefficient continuation from a smooth field]
\label{hs:modal-coefficient-continuation}
Let $E_\eta$ be holomorphic with values in $L^2(B_{3R})^3$ for
$\eta$ in a complex neighborhood of zero.  Suppose that, in
$B_{3R}$, it belongs to $H_{\mathrm{loc}}(\operatorname{curl})$
and is a weak Maxwell electric field satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E_\eta
   =k_\eta^2\varepsilon_b q_\eta E_\eta,
 \qquad k_\eta\ne0,
 \label{hs:modal-local-maxwell}
\end{equation}
where $k_\eta$ is holomorphic and $q_\eta$ is the radially constant
coefficient in \eqref{hs:modal-angular-material}.  Then its intrinsic
conical coefficient for small real $\eta>0$ extends to a holomorphic
scalar $c_\eta$ near zero, with the exact value
\begin{equation}
 c_0=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}\,E_0(0)\cdot e.
 \label{hs:modal-coefficient-limit}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $E_0(0)\cdot e\ne0$, then $c_\eta\ne0$ for all
sufficiently small real positive $\eta$, and the continued electric
field is unbounded at ordinary points approaching the vertex.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $0<\gamma<1/2$ and decrease the parameter neighborhood so that
$\operatorname{Re}\lambda_\eta<1+\gamma/2$ and
$\operatorname{Re}\lambda_\eta>1/2$.  The local regular
decomposition of Lemma~\ref{hs:three-decomposition} also applies at
complex nonzero $k_\eta$: its cutoff estimates use only bounded
$|k_\eta|$, bounded $q_\eta$, and a positive lower bound for
$|k_\eta|$.  More explicitly, cutoff testing of
\eqref{hs:modal-local-maxwell} controls $\operatorname{curl}E_\eta$
in interior $L^2$.  Its divergence is zero and its curl is
$k_\eta^2\varepsilon_bq_\eta E_\eta$, so the constant-coefficient
curl/div identity controls it in a smaller interior $H^1$ space.
Apply one fixed regularized Poincare lifting from $H^1$ to $H^2$,
and fix the mean of the remaining scalar potential.  The result is
\begin{equation}
 E_\eta=\nabla v_\eta+W_\eta,
 \qquad v_\eta\in H^1(B_R),\quad W_\eta\in H^2(B_R)^3,
 \label{hs:modal-decomposition}
\end{equation}
holomorphically in those spaces, using slightly larger balls for
the estimates if necessary.  Holomorphy of the interior derivatives
follows from their distributional holomorphy and locally uniform
bounds; equivalently, Cauchy's integral formula in distributions
and the same local estimates gives the formula in the stronger
spaces.  Thus $W_\eta$ is holomorphic in $C^{0,\gamma}$ on the
final smaller closed ball.  From now on $R$ denotes that final
radius.

Set $w_\eta=W_\eta(0)$ and
$\widetilde W_\eta(x)=W_\eta(x)-w_\eta$.  The uniform estimate
$|\widetilde W_\eta(r\widehat x)|\leq C r^\gamma$ holds on a
smaller closed parameter disk.  Define
\begin{align}
 U_\eta(r)&=\int_{S^2}q_\eta v_\eta(r\widehat x)\phi_\eta\,dS,
 & A_{0,\eta}&=\int_{S^2}q_\eta(w_\eta\cdot\widehat x)
                                      \phi_\eta\,dS,\\
 \widetilde A_\eta(r)&=\int_{S^2}q_\eta
          (\widetilde W_\eta\cdot\widehat x)\phi_\eta\,dS,
 &\widetilde B_\eta(r)&=\int_{S^2}q_\eta
           \widetilde W_{\eta,t}\cdot\nabla_{S^2}\phi_\eta\,dS.
 \label{hs:modal-subtracted-projections}
\end{align}
All angular pairings here are bilinear, without complex conjugation,
as appropriate for holomorphic dependence.  The last two quantities
are $O(r^\gamma)$ uniformly.  For the constant vector $w_\eta$,
the eigenproblem gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \int_{S^2}q_\eta w_{\eta,t}\cdot\nabla_{S^2}\phi_\eta\,dS
       =\lambda_\eta(\lambda_\eta+1)A_{0,\eta}.
 \label{hs:modal-constant-identity}
\end{equation}
Indeed $w_{\eta,t}=\nabla_{S^2}(w_\eta\cdot\widehat x)$.

Taking the divergence of \eqref{hs:modal-local-maxwell} gives
$\operatorname{div}(q_\eta E_\eta)=0$.  Its angular projection is
the same radial equation as \eqref{hs:three-radial}.  A particular
solution valid on the entire small complex parameter disk is
\begin{align}
 p_\eta(r)&=-rA_{0,\eta}+\widetilde p_\eta(r),\notag\\
 \widetilde p_\eta(r)&=\frac{1}{2\lambda_\eta+1}
 \left[r^{\lambda_\eta}\int_0^r s^{-\lambda_\eta}
       \{\widetilde B_\eta(s)
                 -(\lambda_\eta+1)\widetilde A_\eta(s)\}\,ds
 \right.\notag\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{22mm}\left.
 -r^{-\lambda_\eta-1}\int_0^r s^{\lambda_\eta+1}
       \{\widetilde B_\eta(s)
                      +\lambda_\eta\widetilde A_\eta(s)\}\,ds
 \right].
 \label{hs:modal-regular-particular}
\end{align}
We use $r^{\lambda}=\exp(\lambda\log r)$ for $r>0$.
The integrals converge uniformly on compact parameter disks, because
their respective small-$s$ powers have real parts greater than $-1$.
They are holomorphic in $\eta$ by dominated integration, also after
parameter differentiation on a smaller disk.  Direct estimates give
\begin{equation}
 |\widetilde p_\eta(r)|\leq C r^{1+\gamma},\qquad
 |\widetilde p_\eta'(r)|\leq C r^\gamma.
 \label{hs:modal-regular-particular-bound}
\end{equation}
The first term $-rA_{0,\eta}$ is an exact particular solution for
the constant-vector forcing by \eqref{hs:modal-constant-identity}.
For $\lambda_\eta\ne1$ it is also exactly the result of performing
the two constant-vector integrals in \eqref{hs:three-exact}; thus
no additional homogeneous term has been inserted by this
regularization.

Variation of constants now gives
\begin{equation}
 U_\eta(r)=c_\eta r^{\lambda_\eta}
          +d_\eta r^{-\lambda_\eta-1}+p_\eta(r).
\end{equation}
Finite $H^1$ energy of $v_\eta$ excludes $d_\eta$: the radial
derivative of the second term would have squared integral with
weight $r^2$ proportional to
$\int_0^R r^{-2\operatorname{Re}\lambda_\eta-2}\,dr$.
The remaining coefficient has the annular formula
\begin{equation}
 c_\eta=\frac{2}{R}\int_{R/2}^R
          r^{-\lambda_\eta}\{U_\eta(r)-p_\eta(r)\}\,dr.
 \label{hs:modal-holomorphic-coefficient}
\end{equation}
The projection of $v_\eta$ on this annulus is a bounded linear
operation in $L^2$, and all other factors were shown holomorphic.
This proves holomorphy of $c_\eta$.  For positive real $\eta$,
the exponent is below one, so this is exactly the intrinsic
coefficient of Lemma~\ref{hs:three-coefficient}.

At zero the medium is homogeneous, and the Maxwell field $E_0$ is
smooth near the origin by the interior Helmholtz equation.  As
$\nabla v_0=E_0-W_0\in C^{0,\gamma}$,
\begin{equation}
 v_0(r\widehat x)=v_0(0)
        +r\{E_0(0)-w_0\}\cdot\widehat x+O(r^{1+\gamma}).
\end{equation}
The zero angular mean removes $v_0(0)$.  Adding $rA_{0,0}$ to its
projection and using the bound on $\widetilde p_0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 c_0=\int_{S^2}(E_0(0)\cdot\widehat x)\phi_0\,dS
     =\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}\,E_0(0)\cdot e.
\end{equation}
The nonvanishing and unboundedness conclusions follow from
continuity and Lemma~\ref{hs:three-coefficient}.  In particular,
closeness of fields in $L^2$ alone is not being used to infer
nonvanishing of a singular coefficient; its explicit continuous
extension has been established.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[PROVED: all finitely many coefficients of one continued mode]
\label{hs:modal-all-coefficients}
Suppose a compact dielectric has a Maxwell resonance $k_0\ne0$
with eigenfield $E_0$.  Let $p_1,\ldots,p_N$ have disjoint
neighborhoods of constant permittivity $\varepsilon_b$, and choose
axes $e_j$ such that $E_0(p_j)\cdot e_j\ne0$.
In each neighborhood insert the angular material
$\varepsilon_b q_\eta$ of
\eqref{hs:modal-angular-material}, with that neighborhood's axis,
and close the insertions outside its local conical ball.
If the same resonance has a nonzero local-$L^2$ holomorphic
continuation $E_\eta,k_\eta$ for this simultaneous material
perturbation, with $E_\eta\vert_{\eta=0}=E_0$, then there is
$\eta_N>0$ such that every $0<\eta<\eta_N$ has
\begin{equation}
 c_j(E_\eta)\ne0\quad\hbox{for all }1\leq j\leq N.
 \label{hs:modal-all-coefficients-equation}
\end{equation}
These are coefficients of one eigenfield at one resonance, not of
a sum of independent modes or separately selected illuminations.
The physical material bound is respected by also requiring
$\varepsilon_b(1+\eta)\leq\varepsilon_+$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Translate and rotate Theorem~\ref{hs:modal-coefficient-continuation}
at each $p_j$ and take the minimum of the finitely many positive
nonvanishing radii in parameter space.  Scaling a chosen eigenfield
by any holomorphic nonzero normalization factor multiplies all its
coefficients by that same factor and does not affect the result.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact remaining global hypotheses}

\begin{proposition}[PROVED: transfer from a simple resonance to rounded modal peaks]
\label{hs:modal-transfer}
Assume a finite homogeneous dielectric sphere with permittivity
$\varepsilon_b\in(1,\varepsilon_+)$ has a simple resonance in the
axisymmetric TM sector, whose axial electric component is nonzero
on an open interval of its symmetry axis.  Assume furthermore:
\begin{enumerate}
\item This sector's eigenpair has a holomorphic continuation under
the finite axisymmetric cone insertions in
Corollary~\ref{hs:modal-all-coefficients}.
\item An explicitly specified genuine QNM normalization is finite,
nonzero and continuous along that branch and under the rounded
approximations below.
\item Rounding the conical vertices while retaining the same
material values gives a nearby resonance and normalized eigenfield
converging locally in $L^2$, and uniformly one-sided on fixed
smooth-interface collars away from the vertices.
\end{enumerate}
Then, for every finite $N$ and every $M,\ell>0$, one normalized
resonance of a finite smooth dielectric has $N$ disjoint
three-dimensional peak balls of diameter less than $\ell$, each
with one-sided maximum larger than $M$ and a collar maximum smaller
than half its peak.  Their centers may be placed on a line in a
prescribed open plane patch, and their projections may be contained
in that patch, provided that the chosen nonzero axial interval is
positioned to meet the patch.  If the symmetry axis lies in that
plane and the rounding remains axisymmetric, a maximizing point
in each ball can itself be selected in the plane patch.  The
surrounding resonator need not project into the patch.  A maximum
off the symmetry axis may belong to a rotational ring inside its
small ball; no nondegeneracy or uniqueness of a maximum is asserted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose $N$ distinct axial centers in a sufficiently short nonzero
subinterval and choose disjoint balls and insertions as small as
necessary.  Axisymmetric insertions preserve the indicated sector.
Choose one small positive contrast from
Corollary~\ref{hs:modal-all-coefficients}.  Its one normalized sharp
resonance is unbounded at every tip and bounded on each chosen
fixed collar.  Select an ordinary high-field point inside each ball
whose value exceeds twice the collar bound and the target $M$, with
strict margins.  The hypothesized normalized rounding convergence
preserves all these finitely many strict inequalities at one
sufficiently small rounding scale.  Smooth-interface regularity
makes the one-sided maxima on the closed balls finite and attained;
each peak lies inside its ball because of the collar inequality.
This is the same final compactness step as the driven construction,
but it is applied to a homogeneous resonance branch with its stated
QNM normalization.

For the last placement assertion, an axisymmetric vector field
obeys $E(\mathcal R x)=\mathcal R E(x)$ under rotations about its
axis, and therefore $|E|$ is invariant.  The same is true for its
one-sided material traces.  A ball centered on that axis is
rotation invariant.  Rotate any maximizing point into the specified
plane through the axis; it stays in the same ball and retains its
maximal value.  The projection constraint puts this representative
in the prescribed patch.
\end{proof}

This proposition is a proved implication with explicit global
hypotheses.  Lemmas~\ref{ms:sphere-simple}, \ref{ms:continuation},
\ref{ms:normalization}, and~\ref{ms:rounding} supply those hypotheses
for the finite-device construction in Theorem~\ref{ms:modal-packing}.
Theorem~\ref{hs:modal-coefficient-continuation} and
Corollary~\ref{hs:modal-all-coefficients} establish its local
excitation bridge.  No resonance is inferred from a driven field,
and no radiating mode is normalized by a divergent all-space positive
energy integral.  A real dilation fixes the real part of the
nonzero resonance frequency; its negative imaginary part remains
part of the QNM statement.

\section{Spectral construction and normalization of the modal branch}
\label{ms:section}

This section supplies the global hypotheses used by
Proposition~\ref{hs:modal-transfer}.  Frequencies in this section are
complex vacuum wavenumbers.  An outgoing resonance means the
analytic continuation of the vacuum outgoing Green function from
$\operatorname{Im}k>0$; no decay at real spatial infinity is imposed
when $\operatorname{Im}k<0$.  The material is still real, positive,
scalar, nondispersive, and equal to vacuum outside a bounded set.
The normalization below is a reciprocal Green-function residue norm.
It is not a positive all-space energy integral.

\subsection{A compact analytic pencil and continuity under rounding}

Let $B$ be a ball containing all material changes, let
$X=L^2(B;\C^3)$, and extend its elements by zero when applying
whole-space operators.  Write $\chi=\varepsilon_r-1$.  With
$\Phi_k(x)=e^{ik|x|}/(4\pi|x|)$, put
\begin{equation}
 K(k)f=\left.(k^2I+\nabla\nabla)\Phi_k*f\right|_B,
 \qquad L_\chi(k)=I-K(k)\chi.
 \label{ms:pencil}
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $\chi$ is included in the last expression.
The static longitudinal Fourier projection, compressed to $B$, is
denoted by $P$.  It satisfies $P=P^*$ and $0\leq P\leq I$.
The distributional identity
\begin{equation}
 K(k)=-P+R(k),\qquad
 R(k)=k^2\Phi_k I+\nabla\nabla(\Phi_k-\Phi_0),\qquad R(0)=0
 \label{ms:compact-split}
\end{equation}
holds as an identity of bounded operators on $X$.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: analytic Fredholm reduction with a fixed material bound]
\label{ms:fredholm}
For every measurable $0\leq\chi\leq C$ supported in $B$,
\begin{equation}
 L_\chi(k)=A_\chi F_\chi(k),\quad
 A_\chi=I+P\chi,\quad
 F_\chi(k)=I-A_\chi^{-1}R(k)\chi
 \label{ms:factorization}
\end{equation}
has $A_\chi$ invertible with $\|A_\chi^{-1}\|\leq1+C$.
The operator $R(k)$ is an entire Hilbert--Schmidt family, and
$F_\chi$ is an analytic identity-minus-compact pencil with
$F_\chi(0)=I$.  Its noninvertible values are isolated, of finite
algebraic multiplicity.  At $k\ne0$, its kernel is exactly the
restriction to $B$ of the finite local-energy Maxwell resonances
for this material, with exterior field given by \eqref{ms:pencil}.

If $0\leq\chi_j\leq C$ and $\chi_j\to\chi$ almost everywhere
in this same ball, then $F_{\chi_j}(k)\to F_\chi(k)$ in operator
norm, locally uniformly in $k$, as do all $k$ derivatives.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Fourier symbol of $\nabla\nabla\Phi_0$ is
$-\xi\xi^T/|\xi|^2$, including its distributional local term.
Taylor expansion of $e^{ikr}$ shows that each entry of the
remaining kernel in \eqref{ms:compact-split} is $O(1/r)$ as
$r\downarrow0$, uniformly for $k$ in compact subsets of $\C$.
The constant term $ik/(4\pi)$ in $\Phi_k-\Phi_0$ has zero Hessian.
The squared kernel is locally integrable in three dimensions;
on bounded $B\times B$ it is integrable.  Its parameter power
series and derivatives have the same local majorants on compact
parameter sets.  This proves the entire Hilbert--Schmidt assertion.

Put $s=\sqrt\chi$.  The positive selfadjoint operator $I+sPs$
has inverse of norm at most one, and direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
 A_\chi^{-1}=I-Ps(I+sPs)^{-1}s.
 \label{ms:static-inverse}
\end{equation}
This proves the stated bound and factorization.  Analytic Fredholm
theory applies, with the invertible value at zero fixing its
nontrivial alternative.  This application needs no small contrast,
smooth boundary, or uniform feature size.

For completeness, the volume representation is an exact Maxwell
equation.  In distributions,
$(\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}-k^2)K(k)f=k^2f$.
Consequently a kernel vector extended by $E=K(k)(\chi E)$
satisfies $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E
=k^2(1+\chi)E$.  Its curl is locally square integrable, by
the first-derivative Newton-potential estimate; equivalently this
follows from local cutoff testing.  Set $H=(ik)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}E$
in units with vacuum impedance one.  This gives the two first-order
Maxwell equations and the outgoing continued representation.
Conversely, subtracting this volume field from any such resonance
leaves an entire vacuum Maxwell field regular at the origin and
outgoing outside a ball.  In each vector spherical harmonic its
regular radial function and outgoing radial function have nonzero
Wronskian for $k\ne0$, so all coefficients vanish.  Thus the
restriction is a kernel vector.  A zero restriction gives the zero
global field.

Almost-everywhere convergence and the common bound give strong
convergence of the multiplication operators.  The resolvent identity
and \eqref{ms:static-inverse} give strong convergence of $A_{\chi_j}^{-1}$
with a uniform bound.  Dominated convergence of the integral kernels
gives $R(k)\chi_j\to R(k)\chi$ in Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
A uniformly bounded strongly convergent operator sequence converges
in norm after composition on the right with a fixed compact operator:
approximate that compact operator by finite-rank operators.
Apply this observation to $A_{\chi_j}^{-1}R(k)\chi$ and add the
Hilbert--Schmidt difference.  Compactness in the parameter $k$ makes
the convergence locally uniform; Cauchy's formula gives the same
claim for derivatives.
\end{proof}

All these arguments also hold on a fixed closed symmetry sector
preserved by $P$, $R(k)$, and the material multiplication.  We use
the axisymmetric electric sector: $E$ has only meridional components
and $H$ only the azimuthal component in cylindrical coordinates.
Rotationally invariant scalar material preserves this sector.
It is closed in $L^2$ and orthogonal to the axisymmetric magnetic
sector, both for the Hermitian pairing and for the unconjugated
bilinear pairing.  At the coordinate axis these statements are
understood in the distributional and almost-everywhere sense.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: persistence of a simple resonance]
\label{ms:continuation}
Suppose the restriction of $F_\chi$ to such a fixed sector has an
isolated characteristic value $k_*$ of algebraic multiplicity one.
An analytic $L^\infty$ material perturbation has a locally
holomorphic continued wavenumber $k_\eta$ and nonzero eigenfield
$E_\eta\in X$.  Their values at zero are $k_*$ and a prescribed
nonzero eigenfield.  If instead bounded nonnegative materials
$\chi_j$ converge almost everywhere to $\chi$, and preserve this
sector, they have unique nearby simple characteristic values
$k_j\to k_*$ and eigenfields $E_j\to E$ in $X$, after one fixed
nonzero linear functional fixes their scale.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose closed complements of the one-dimensional kernel and
cokernel of $F_\chi(k_*)$.  The block between those complements
is invertible, and remains so in a small operator-norm neighborhood.
Block elimination reduces the kernel equation to a single scalar
analytic function $f(k)$, multiplied by invertible analytic block
operators.  Algebraic multiplicity one is exactly
$f(k_*)=0$, $f'(k_*)\ne0$.
For an analytic material perturbation, $A_\chi^{-1}$ continues
analytically by a Neumann series near the base material, so every
block is jointly analytic.  The scalar analytic implicit-function
theorem supplies $k_\eta$; back substitution supplies $E_\eta$.
For almost-everywhere material convergence, Lemma~\ref{ms:fredholm}
gives uniform convergence of the blocks and scalar functions on a
small closed disk.  Rouch\'e's theorem gives exactly one zero,
counting multiplicity.  It tends to $k_*$ and is simple.  Back
substitution and a linear functional nonzero on $E$ give the stated
field convergence.  This reduction must use $F$ for the
almost-everywhere convergence claim: the leading multiplication
in $L$ need not converge in operator norm.  Multiplication by $A$
is analytic and invertible and does not alter a characteristic
value's multiplicity or its right kernel.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A simple sphere resonance without a genericity assumption}

Choose an algebraic real refractive index
$1<n<\sqrt{\varepsilon_+}$, leaving a positive margin for the
subsequent insertions.  Rational numbers already provide this
choice for every $\varepsilon_+>1$.  In a sphere of radius $a$ and
permittivity $n^2$, write $z=ka$ and
$\psi_l(z)=zj_l(z)$, $\xi_l(z)=zh_l^{(1)}(z)$.
Matching the tangential electric and magnetic fields of the regular
interior electric multipole and the outgoing exterior multipole
gives, for $l\geq1$, the determinant
\begin{equation}
 D_l(z)=n\psi_l(nz)\xi_l'(z)-\xi_l(z)\psi_l'(nz).
 \label{ms:mie-denominator}
\end{equation}
Primes mean derivatives with respect to the displayed argument.
For example the interior tangential electric component is
proportional to $\psi_l'(nz)/(nz)$, and its tangential magnetic
component to $n\psi_l(nz)/(nz)$; their exterior analogues are
$\xi_l'(z)/z$ and $\xi_l(z)/z$.  Eliminating their two amplitudes
gives \eqref{ms:mie-denominator}.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: simplicity and separation of electric sphere poles]
\label{ms:sphere-simple}
For algebraic $n>1$, every nonzero zero of $D_l$ is simple, and
$D_l$ and $D_j$ have no common nonzero zero if $l\ne j$.
In the axisymmetric electric sector each such zero is therefore a
simple Maxwell characteristic value.  There are $l=1$ poles
\begin{equation}
 z_m=\frac{\pi(m+1/2)}{n}
       -\frac{i}{2n}\log\frac{n+1}{n-1}+O(m^{-1}),
 \qquad m\longrightarrow\infty.
 \label{ms:pole-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Their real parts are positive, imaginary parts negative, and
$\operatorname{Re}z_m/(-2\operatorname{Im}z_m)\to\infty$.
The $l=1,m_{\rm az}=0$ interior electric field has a nonzero axial
component at the center and on a sufficiently short axial interval.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The exact finite spherical-Hankel expansions\cite{dlmfExplicit} are
\begin{align}
 \xi_l(z)&=(-i)^{l+1}e^{iz}p_l(z),&
 z h_l^{(2)}(z)&=i^{l+1}e^{-iz}\widetilde p_l(z),\\
 p_l(z)&=\sum_{s=0}^l\frac{i^s(l+s)!}{2^s s!(l-s)!z^s},&
 \widetilde p_l(z)&=\sum_{s=0}^l\frac{(-i)^s(l+s)!}{2^s s!(l-s)!z^s}.
 \label{ms:hankel-polynomials}
\end{align}
Using $\psi_l=(\xi_l+zh_l^{(2)})/2$, the determinant, up to a
nonzero factor independent of its zero condition, is
\begin{equation}
 C_{l,+}(z)e^{inz}+C_{l,-}(z)e^{-inz},
 \label{ms:exp-polynomial}
\end{equation}
where, with $p=p_l$, $\widetilde p=\widetilde p_l$,
\begin{align}
 C_{l,+}&=(-i)^{l+1}\{i(n-1)p(z)p(nz)
                  +np(nz)p'(z)-p(z)p'(nz)\},\\
 C_{l,-}&=i^{l+1}\{i(n+1)p(z)\widetilde p(nz)
             +n\widetilde p(nz)p'(z)-p(z)\widetilde p'(nz)\}.
 \label{ms:rational-coefficients}
\end{align}
These are rational functions with algebraic coefficients.  They
cannot vanish together at $z\ne0$: the two corresponding equations
would annihilate both independent interior Riccati--Hankel
solutions.  Their nonzero Wronskian would then force both
$\xi_l(z)$ and $\xi_l'(z)$ to vanish, contradicting uniqueness
for their second-order radial equation.  Thus at any zero of $D_l$
both coefficients are nonzero and the equation is equivalently
\begin{equation}
 e^{2inz}=R_l(z):=-C_{l,-}(z)/C_{l,+}(z).
 \label{ms:rational-pole}
\end{equation}
The expansion $p_l(z)=1+i l(l+1)/(2z)+O(z^{-2})$ gives
\begin{equation}
 R_l(z)=(-1)^l\frac{n+1}{n-1}
       \left(1-\frac{i l(l+1)}{nz}+O(z^{-2})\right).
 \label{ms:rational-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
For different parity the leading constants differ; for the same
parity and distinct $l$ the coefficients of $1/z$ differ.  Hence
$R_l-R_j$ is never identically zero when $l\ne j$.

The Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem implies that $e^\alpha$ is
transcendental for every nonzero algebraic complex
$\alpha$\cite{bezivinRobba1989}.  If $D_l$ and $D_j$ had a common
nonzero zero $z$, the nontrivial rational equation
$R_l(z)=R_j(z)$ would make $z$ algebraic.  Equation
\eqref{ms:rational-pole} would make $e^{2inz}$ algebraic, a
contradiction.  If $D_l$ had a multiple nonzero zero, differentiation
of \eqref{ms:rational-pole} would give
$R_l'(z)=2inR_l(z)$.  This too is a nontrivial rational equation:
$R_l'/R_l\to0$ at infinity, whereas $2in\ne0$.
It again makes $z$ algebraic and yields the same contradiction.
No division by a zero coefficient was used at a pole.

Separation of variables decomposes the axisymmetric electric
sector into the degrees $l=1,2,\ldots$, with one azimuthal mode
at each degree.  Regularity at the origin and the outgoing condition
leave one radial amplitude on each side of the sphere.  Locally in
$k\ne0$, solving the inhomogeneous radial equations and matching
them reduces each block, by analytic invertible elimination, to
its two-by-two tangential matching matrix.  Its determinant is
\eqref{ms:mie-denominator} times a nonzero analytic factor.
Thus a simple zero is a one-dimensional simple characteristic
value of that block.  Lemma~\ref{ms:fredholm} excludes accumulation
at a finite nonzero $k$; the other angular blocks are invertible
near the selected zero.  The proved separation across $l$ gives
algebraic multiplicity one in the whole selected sector.
The familiar degeneracy across the other azimuthal indices in the
full sphere is irrelevant to this invariant-sector statement.

For $l=1$ let $r_\infty=-(n+1)/(n-1)$ and choose the centers
$z_m^0$ equal to the two leading terms in
\eqref{ms:pole-asymptotic}.  They obey $e^{2inz_m^0}=r_\infty$.
On the circle $|z-z_m^0|=C/m$, the modulus of
$e^{2inz}-r_\infty$ is at least $cC/m$ for large $m$, with
$c>0$ independent of $m$.  The rational correction
$R_1(z)-r_\infty$ is at most $C_0/m$ there.  Taking $C$ large
enough, Rouch\'e's theorem gives exactly one zero in this disk.
This proves \eqref{ms:pole-asymptotic}.  Finally
$\psi_1(u)=u^2/3+O(u^4)$.  The regular electric dipole vector
spherical wave therefore tends to a nonzero constant axial vector
at the center, for a nonzero radial amplitude.  The amplitude
cannot vanish without making the whole matched eigenfield zero.
Analyticity in the homogeneous core preserves the nonzero component
on a short axial interval.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A genuine reciprocal mode norm}

The bilinear pairing in the remainder of this section has no
complex conjugation.  Reciprocity gives $K(k)^T=K(k)$ on $X$.
For a simple characteristic value in the selected sector, define
\begin{equation}
 d(E,k,\chi)=-\int_B \chi E\cdot K'(k)(\chi E)\,dx,
 \qquad \mathcal N(E)=-\frac{k}{2}d(E,k,\chi).
 \label{ms:residue-norm}
\end{equation}
The derivative kernel is a bounded compact operator; these are
finite integrals of locally square-integrable fields.  Their values
are independent of which containing ball is used.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: nonzero residue norm and its stability]
\label{ms:normalization}
At a simple nonzero Maxwell pole in the selected sector,
$\mathcal N(E)\ne0$.  Scaling $E$ by $a\in\C$ scales
$\mathcal N$ by $a^2$.  The reciprocal electric Green operator for
$\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}-k^2\varepsilon_r$,
restricted to this sector, has residue
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname*{Res}_{k=k_*}G(k;x,y)
       =-\frac{E(x)\otimes E(y)}{2k_*\mathcal N(E)}.
 \label{ms:green-residue}
\end{equation}
The norm is continuous along the almost-everywhere material and
eigenfield convergence of Lemma~\ref{ms:continuation}, and
holomorphic along its analytic branches.  A local nonzero analytic
choice of scale makes $\mathcal N=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The functional $\ell_E(v)=\int_B\chi E\cdot v$ is nonzero:
if $\chi E=0$, the volume equation makes $E=0$.  It lies in the
left kernel of $L_\chi(k_*)$, since
$\ell_E L_\chi=\langle\chi E,\cdot\rangle-
\langle K(k_*)\chi E,\chi\,\cdot\rangle=0$.
It is a nonzero functional also on the chosen sector, because that
sector is closed under complex conjugation and multiplication by
the real rotationally invariant $\chi$.  Its derivative pairing
$\ell_E L_\chi'(k_*)E$ is exactly $d$.
The scalar Schur reduction in Lemma~\ref{ms:continuation} shows
that this pairing is nonzero at a simple characteristic value;
otherwise the scalar zero would have multiplicity at least two.
The residue of $L_\chi^{-1}$ is therefore
$E\otimes\ell_E/d$.

The vacuum electric resolvent is $G_0(k)=k^{-2}K(k)$, with its
natural restriction when the source is supported in $B$.
The full electric field for a compact source is
$L_\chi(k)^{-1}G_0(k)f$ in $B$.  At the pole, reciprocity and
$K(k_*)\chi E=E$ give
\begin{equation}
 \ell_E G_0(k_*)f=k_*^{-2}\int E(y)\cdot f(y)\,dy.
\end{equation}
The same identity for sources outside $B$ follows directly from
the global volume representation and the symmetric kernel.
Multiplying the two residue factors yields
$E\otimes E/(k_*^2d)$, which is
\eqref{ms:green-residue}.  This is a genuine pole normalization
of a quasinormal mode, consistent with reciprocal Green-residue
normalizations\cite{muljarov2016}; the sign reflects the displayed
choice of Maxwell operator.  For a degenerate full-space pole the
formula describes the selected symmetry block, not a claim that
the entire Green residue has rank one.

Under rounding, $\chi_j E_j\to\chi E$ in $L^2$: split the
difference into the uniformly bounded multiplier times $E_j-E$
and dominated convergence applied to $(\chi_j-\chi)E$.
Operator-norm continuity of $K'(k)$ proves convergence of $d$
and $\mathcal N$.  Holomorphy and quadratic scaling follow from
the formulas.  A nonzero analytic square root of $\mathcal N$
on a sufficiently small parameter disk normalizes the field.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: normalized resonance continuity away from rounded tips]
\label{ms:rounding}
Fix the finite axisymmetric conical construction and a simple
sector pole with $\mathcal N=1$.  Round all its vertices
axisymmetrically within balls of radius $\rho$, leaving the material
values and all other interfaces fixed.  For sufficiently small
$\rho$, there is a simple sector pole $k_\rho\to k_*$ and
eigenfield with $\mathcal N(E_\rho)=1$ converging to $E$ locally
in $L^2$.  On any fixed compact set away from the vertices,
including either side of a fixed smooth interface, the fields
converge uniformly one-sided.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The rounded coefficients converge almost everywhere with fixed
support and the same positive material bound.  Apply
Lemmas~\ref{ms:fredholm}--\ref{ms:normalization} and choose the
square-root scale converging to the sharp field's scale.
The volume representation supplies local $L^2$ convergence also
on any larger fixed ball, by the same static and compact estimates.
For local regularity write $U_\rho=E_\rho-E$.  Its equation is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}U_\rho
 =k_\rho^2\varepsilon_\rho U_\rho
 +(k_\rho^2\varepsilon_\rho-k_*^2\varepsilon)E.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side tends to zero in local $L^2$ and $U_\rho$
does likewise.  Cutoff testing gives
$\operatorname{curl}U_\rho\to0$ in smaller interior $L^2$.
The constant-coefficient curl/div estimate for
$\operatorname{curl}U_\rho$ then gives convergence in interior
$H^1$.  The fixed regularized curl lifting gives a difference
$W_\rho-W\to0$ in $H^2$, and the corresponding mean-fixed
potentials converge in $H^1$.
On a neighborhood disjoint from the changing tips the coefficients
are identical.  The scalar equation for the potential difference
is $\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon\nabla(v_\rho-v))
=-\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon(W_\rho-W))$.
The piecewise $H^3$ scalar transmission estimate across a fixed
smooth positive interface, used in
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-rounding}, gives convergence of the potentials
in smaller piecewise $H^3$ sets.  Hence the electric fields converge
piecewise in $H^2$, and uniformly by the three-dimensional Sobolev
embedding.  At ordinary interior points the same conclusion follows
from the homogeneous interior estimates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Closed modal hotspot statement}

\begin{theorem}[PROVED: arbitrarily many peaks in one normalized finite-device mode]
\label{ms:modal-packing}
Fix $\varepsilon_+>1$, a real target wavenumber $k_0>0$, a
nonempty open patch $\mathcal P$ of a plane, a positive integer
$N$, and numbers $\ell,M,Q_0>0$.  There is a finite smooth
piecewise-constant dielectric with vacuum exterior and
$1\leq\varepsilon_r\leq\varepsilon_+$, and one nonzero outgoing
Maxwell quasinormal mode $(E,H,k_*)$, such that
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Re}k_*=k_0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Im}k_*<0,\qquad
 \frac{\operatorname{Re}k_*}{-2\operatorname{Im}k_*}>Q_0,
 \qquad \mathcal N(E)=1.
 \label{ms:modal-parameters}
\end{equation}
There are $N$ disjoint three-dimensional observation balls of
diameter less than $\ell$, centered in $\mathcal P$ and with
orthogonal projections contained in $\mathcal P$.  In each ball
the one-sided electric-field maximum exceeds $M$ and is more than
twice the maximum on a fixed collar of its boundary.  A point
attaining each maximum can be chosen in $\mathcal P$.
Thus each peak's half-amplitude connected region stays in its
small ball.  No uniform support-size bound, minimum feature size,
nondegeneracy of the maximum, or positive energy fraction per peak
is asserted.  The insertion contrast may depend on $N$; the common
material bounds do not.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take an algebraic index $n$ strictly between one and
$\sqrt{\varepsilon_+}$.  Lemma~\ref{ms:sphere-simple} gives a
simple axisymmetric electric dipole pole with quality factor
strictly greater than $Q_0$.  Scale the sphere initially to make
the real part of its wavenumber $k_0$.  Put its symmetry axis in
the target plane and its center in the open patch.  Choose $N$
distinct points on a sufficiently short axial interval where the
background axial component is nonzero, with ample clearance from
the patch boundary.  Choose disjoint arbitrarily small conical
neighborhoods and close their material insertions smoothly away
from their vertices, inside the homogeneous sphere.

Axisymmetry preserves the electric sector.  Lemma~\ref{ms:continuation}
gives one holomorphic pole and eigenfield under simultaneous
positive cone contrast $\eta$.  Theorem~\ref{hs:modal-coefficient-continuation}
and Corollary~\ref{hs:modal-all-coefficients} give nonzero singular
coefficients at all $N$ vertices for one sufficiently small
$\eta>0$.  Choose it also so that $n^2(1+\eta)<\varepsilon_+$,
the pole remains simple, its imaginary part remains negative, and
its quality factor remains strictly above $Q_0$.
Lemma~\ref{ms:normalization} fixes its genuine norm to one.
Lemma~\ref{ms:rounding} supplies every rounding and normalization
hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{hs:modal-transfer}.  Its high-point
and collar argument now proves all the simultaneous strict peak
inequalities for sufficiently small rounding radius.  The same
axisymmetric rotation argument puts an actual maximizing point
of each ball in the specified plane.

There is one final harmless adjustment because the real pole
frequency has shifted.  About a point in the interior of the patch,
dilate space by $s=\operatorname{Re}k_*/k_0>0$.  Maxwell scaling
sends the wavenumber to $k_*/s$, keeps the quality factor and
material values unchanged, and tends to the identity as the
insertion contrast tends to zero.  A normalized field scales as
$E_s(x)=s^{-3/2}E(x/s)$: indeed \eqref{ms:residue-norm} and
the scaled kernel give $\mathcal N(E(x/s))=s^3\mathcal N(E)$.
Choose all observation-ball diameters with strict margins below
$\ell$, their projections with strict interior clearance, and
their pre-scaling peak targets larger than $s^{3/2}M$ for every
$s$ in a fixed small neighborhood of one.  These choices can be
made before the final rounding.  The dilation therefore preserves
every desired inequality and sets the exact real frequency.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[PROVED: vanishing residue-normalized localization ratios]
\label{ms:modal-volume}
For one fixed sharp modal construction with $N$ tips, its rounded
normalized modes have uniformly finite nonzero residue norm, and
the maximum in each selected ball tends to infinity.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|\mathcal N(E_\rho)|}
 {\displaystyle\max_{x\text{ in ball }j}
        \varepsilon_\rho(x)|E_\rho(x)|^2}\longrightarrow0
 \quad (1\leq j\leq N).
\end{equation}
These are positive intensity ratios formed from the genuine
reciprocal norm.  A directional complex mode volume uses
$\mathcal N/[\varepsilon(u\cdot E)^2]$ instead; selecting a
Cartesian component of size at least $|E|/\sqrt3$ gives the same
vanishing magnitude along a subsequence with a fixed component.
No divergent all-space positive energy is substituted for
$\mathcal N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every finite amplitude threshold choose ordinary high points
of the fixed sharp eigenfield at all tips, and then use the uniform
convergence at those fixed points from Lemma~\ref{ms:rounding}.
This proves divergence of every maximum as $\rho\downarrow0$.
The normalized numerator is one, and the positive permittivities
have a common lower bound.  There are only three Cartesian
components, so the indicated subsequence exists for a directional
volume.  The finite-frequency dilation has a finite nonzero limit
and leaves the vanishing-ratio conclusion unchanged.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite three-dimensional driven packing and the modal normalization audit}
\label{hs:three-section}

This section replaces the unproved finite-extrusion step by a different
construction: finite inclusions with conical \emph{point} tips.  Small
positive contrast supplies a convenient uniform scattering inverse; it
does not remove the point singularity.  The material bounds and frequency
remain fixed.  The modal question is separated at the end.

\begin{theorem}[PROVED: finite three-dimensional driven point localization]
\label{hs:three-packing}
Fix $k>0$ and an upper bound $\varepsilon_+>1$.  There is a number
$\varepsilon_h\in(1,\varepsilon_+]$ that can be kept fixed in the
following construction.  Given any nonempty open patch $\mathcal P$ in
a plane, any finite $N$, and any $\ell,M>0$, there exist a compactly
supported binary dielectric $\varepsilon_r\in\{1,\varepsilon_h\}$
with $2N$ disjoint smooth inclusions and one unit-incoming-power
Maxwell illumination at frequency $k$ such that there are $N$
pairwise disjoint three-dimensional balls $Q_j$, of diameter less
than $\ell$, satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\overline Q_j,\,\text{one-sided}}|E|>M,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\text{a collar of }\partial Q_j,\,\text{one-sided}}|E|
 <\tfrac12\max_{\overline Q_j,\,\text{one-sided}}|E|.
 \label{hs:three-peak}
\end{equation}
The centers of the balls lie in $\mathcal P$, and the orthogonal
projections of the balls and of the material support are contained
in $\mathcal P$.  In particular the selected superlevel components
are localized in all three spatial dimensions.  The maximizing
points after rounding need not lie exactly in the original plane.
There is no invariant infinite direction, no internal impressed
source, and no conducting boundary in the physical device.
\end{theorem}

The prescribed patch is therefore a projection constraint, while the
localized neighborhoods themselves are three-dimensional.  This exact
quantifier avoids silently replacing a point by an infinitely long edge.
As before, no minimum energy fraction in an individual peak is required.

\subsection{A genuine conical point exponent}
\label{hs:three-angular-subsection}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: strict positive contrast gives a conical exponent below one]
\label{hs:three-angular}
Choose $t\in(1/\sqrt3,1)$ and set, on the unit sphere,
\begin{equation}
 q(\widehat x)=1+d\,\mathbf1_{\{|\widehat x\cdot e_3|>t\}},
 \qquad d>0.
 \label{hs:three-caps}
\end{equation}
There are a real $\phi\in H^1(S^2)$ and $\lambda\in(0,1)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 -\operatorname{div}_{S^2}(q\nabla_{S^2}\phi)
   =\nu q\phi,\qquad
 \nu=\lambda(\lambda+1),\qquad
 \int_{S^2}q\phi^2=1,\quad \int_{S^2}q\phi=0.
 \label{hs:three-angular-eigenproblem}
\end{equation}
The electrostatic field $E_0=\nabla(r^\lambda\phi)$ is curl free,
satisfies $\operatorname{div}(qE_0)=0$ weakly in a ball, and obeys
\begin{equation}
 \int_{B_R}q|E_0|^2\,dx=\lambda R^{2\lambda+1}.
 \label{hs:three-static-energy}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The weighted Rayleigh quotient on the subspace orthogonal to constants
has a positive minimum by the compact embedding
$H^1(S^2)\hookrightarrow L^2(S^2)$ and the sphere Poincare inequality.
Use the trial function $z=\widehat x\cdot e_3$, whose weighted mean
vanishes by reflection symmetry.  Since
$|\nabla_{S^2}z|^2=1-z^2$,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{S^2}q(|\nabla_{S^2}z|^2-2z^2)
 =d\int_{|z|>t}(1-3z^2)<0.
 \label{hs:three-trial}
\end{equation}
Explicitly, the trial quotient is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q(z)=\frac{2+d(2-3t+t^3)}{1+d(1-t^3)},\qquad
 \mathcal Q(z)-2=-\frac{3dt(1-t^2)}{1+d(1-t^3)}<0.
 \label{hs:three-trial-explicit}
\end{equation}
Thus the first positive eigenvalue $\nu$ is less than two; its
positive indicial root satisfies $0<\lambda<1$.  Separation in
spherical coordinates proves the weak electrostatic equation.
The interface conditions are exactly those in the angular weak
problem.  The flux through a small sphere is of order
$r^{\lambda+1}$, so there is no point source at the origin.
Finally $\int q|\nabla_{S^2}\phi|^2=\nu$ gives
$(\lambda^2+\nu)\int_0^Rr^{2\lambda}\,dr
=\lambda R^{2\lambda+1}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact Maxwell coefficient functional at the conical point}
\label{hs:three-functional-subsection}

We use one standard regularity theorem in a precise local form.  On a
ball there is a bounded linear regularized Poincare lifting
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R:H^1(B)^3\cap\ker\operatorname{div}\longrightarrow H^2(B)^3,
 \qquad \operatorname{curl}\mathcal R f=f.
 \label{hs:three-lifting}
\end{equation}
This is the regularized curl-potential operator of
Costabel and McIntosh~\cite{costabelMcIntosh2010}; their homotopy
identity and order-minus-one mapping theorem apply to a ball.
Only this established local Sobolev mapping is imported.  It is not
a scattering, corner-excitation, or rounding theorem.

Use normalized units $\operatorname{curl}E=ikH$,
$\operatorname{curl}H=-ikqE$ in the local arguments; the magnetic
field here is the physical magnetic field multiplied by $Z_0$.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: local regular decomposition with controlled remainder]
\label{hs:three-decomposition}
Suppose $E,H$ solve the source-free Maxwell system with
$1\leq q\leq\varepsilon_h$ on $B_{3R}$ and have finite local energy.
On $B_R$ one can write
\begin{equation}
 E=\nabla v+W,\qquad v\in H^1(B_R),\quad W\in H^2(B_R)^3,
 \quad
 \|v\|_{H^1}+\|W\|_{H^2}\leq C\|E\|_{L^2(B_{3R})},
 \label{hs:three-decomposition-bound}
\end{equation}
after fixing the additive constant of $v$.  The maps can be chosen
linear.  The constant depends on the displayed radii, frequency and
material bounds, not on derivatives of $q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Testing $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E=k^2qE$ against
$\chi^2E$, for a compactly supported cutoff, and applying Young's
inequality gives
$\|\operatorname{curl}E\|_{L^2(B_{5R/2})}
\leq C\|E\|_{L^2(B_{3R})}$.
Since $\operatorname{div}H=0$ and
$\operatorname{curl}H=-ikqE$, the Fourier curl/div identity applied
to a cutoff of $H$ gives
$\|H\|_{H^1(B_{2R})}\leq C\|E\|_{L^2(B_{3R})}$.
This is the same constant-permeability interior mechanism as
Lemma~\ref{ch:magneticHone}.  Apply \eqref{hs:three-lifting} on
$B_{2R}$ to $\operatorname{curl}E=ikH$, which is divergence free,
and restrict the resulting $W$ to $B_R$.
The curl-free difference $E-W$ is a gradient on the simply connected
ball.  Its potential with zero mean satisfies the stated bound by
the ordinary Poincare inequality.  All operations are bounded and
linear.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: intrinsic conical singular coefficient]
\label{hs:three-coefficient}
In the conical material of Lemma~\ref{hs:three-angular}, every local
Maxwell solution as above has a linear coefficient $c(E)$, continuous
for the $L^2(B_{3R})$ topology on its solution space.  If $c(E)\ne0$,
then $|E|$ is unbounded at ordinary points approaching the conical
vertex from the homogeneous regions.  Its local energy remains finite.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use $E=\nabla v+W$ from Lemma~\ref{hs:three-decomposition}.  Since
$H^2(B_R)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\gamma}(\overline B_R)$ for
$0<\gamma<1/2$, $W$ is bounded.  Define
\begin{equation}
 U(r)=\int_{S^2}qv(r,\widehat x)\phi,\qquad
 A(r)=\int_{S^2}qW_r(r,\widehat x)\phi,\qquad
 B(r)=\int_{S^2}qW_t(r,\widehat x)\cdot\nabla_{S^2}\phi.
 \label{hs:three-projections}
\end{equation}
The subscripts denote radial and tangential components.  Projecting
$\operatorname{div}(q(\nabla v+W))=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 (r^2U')'-\lambda(\lambda+1)U=-(r^2A)'+rB.
 \label{hs:three-radial}
\end{equation}
The exact solution with finite $H^1$ energy is
\begin{align}
 U(r)&=c(E)r^\lambda+p(r),\notag\\
 p(r)&=\frac{1}{2\lambda+1}\left[
 r^\lambda\int_0^rs^{-\lambda}\{B(s)-(\lambda+1)A(s)\}\,ds
 \right.\notag\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{36mm}\left.
 -r^{-\lambda-1}\int_0^rs^{\lambda+1}\{B(s)+\lambda A(s)\}\,ds
 \right].
 \label{hs:three-exact}
\end{align}
To verify this formula, variation of constants for the two homogeneous
solutions $r^\lambda,r^{-\lambda-1}$ first gives integrals of
$-A'-2A/r+B/r$.  Integrating the $A'$ terms by parts cancels their
endpoint contributions and gives \eqref{hs:three-exact}.
The integrals converge because $A,B$ are bounded and $\lambda<1$.
They show
\begin{equation}
 |p(r)|\leq Cr\|W\|_\infty,\qquad
 |p'(r)|\leq C\|W\|_\infty.
 \label{hs:three-particular-bound}
\end{equation}
For the second inequality, differentiation produces the additional
term $-A(r)$ and two bounded integral terms.  Finite $H^1$ energy
excludes the other homogeneous term $r^{-\lambda-1}$.

Average $r^{-\lambda}(U(r)-p(r))$ on $R/2<r<R$ to define $c(E)$.
This is a bounded functional of $v$ in $L^2$ and $W$ in $L^\infty$;
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-decomposition} proves the required continuity.
It is independent of the chosen lifting.  Indeed, two liftings
differ by a bounded curl-free vector field; the corresponding
potentials differ by a Lipschitz function.  Its angular projection
is $O(r)$ after subtracting its constant value, and the weighted
mean of $\phi$ is zero.  Such a difference has coefficient zero.

Angular integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{\nu}|U(r)|\leq
 \left(\int_{S^2}q|\nabla_{S^2}v(r,\widehat x)|^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{hs:three-gradient}
\end{equation}
If $c(E)\ne0$, then $|U(r)|\geq |c(E)|r^\lambda/2$ at all
sufficiently small admissible radii, since $p=O(r)$.
Consequently the essential supremum of $|\nabla v|$ on these spheres
is at least a positive constant times $r^{\lambda-1}$.
Subtracting the bounded $W$ cannot remove the divergence of $|E|$.
Inside each constant-coefficient region the Maxwell field is analytic;
the large values can be selected away from interfaces.  Its energy
is finite by the starting local $L^2$ assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: local positive-frequency excitation of the point coefficient]
\label{hs:three-local-excitation}
For the conical coefficient $q$, at every fixed $k>0$ there are local
source-free Maxwell solutions on a sufficiently small ball with
$c(E)\ne0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Scale a physical ball to $B_1$ and write $\kappa=kR$.
Let $E_0=\nabla(r^\lambda\phi)$ and consider
\begin{equation}
 X_q=\{w\in H_0(\operatorname{curl},B_1):
                 \operatorname{div}(qw)=0\}.
 \label{hs:three-local-space}
\end{equation}
There is a finite constant $C_q$ with
$\|w\|_2\leq C_q\|\operatorname{curl}w\|_2$ on $X_q$.
Here is a reduction to the ordinary ball estimate, which avoids an
unproved smoothness assumption on $q$.  Decompose
$w=w_T+\nabla p$ with $p\in H_0^1$ and
$\operatorname{div}w_T=0$, $w_T\in H_0(\operatorname{curl})$.
Weighted divergence yields
$\int q\nabla p\cdot\nabla\overline\psi
=-\int(q-1)w_T\cdot\nabla\overline\psi$, so
$\|\nabla p\|_2\leq d\|w_T\|_2$.
For a ball the usual Maxwell estimate is
$\|w_T\|_2\leq C\|\operatorname{curl}w_T\|_2$.
For clarity, $H^1$ membership in this assertion is not assumed.
The order-minus-one lifting also maps $L^2$ to $H^1$; lift
$\operatorname{curl}w_T$ to $V\in H^1$.  The difference
$w_T-V=\nabla p_0$ has $\Delta p_0=-\operatorname{div}V$ and
tangential boundary gradient $-V_t$.  The latter is in
$H^{1/2}(S^2)$, so the boundary trace of $p_0$, modulo a constant,
is in $H^{3/2}(S^2)$.  This follows by the scalar sphere Laplacian
estimate applied to its tangential gradient.  Dirichlet regularity
on the smooth ball gives $p_0\in H^2$, hence $w_T\in H^1$.
One can now use the Gaffney identity for tangential-zero fields,
\begin{equation}
 \|\operatorname{curl}w_T\|_2^2+\|\operatorname{div}w_T\|_2^2
 =\|\nabla w_T\|_2^2+2\|w_T\cdot n\|_{L^2(S^2)}^2,
 \label{hs:three-gaffney}
\end{equation}
initially for smooth fields and then by trace-preserving approximation
on the smooth ball.  Poincare's inequality with the tangential trace
condition excludes constant vectors and bounds the $L^2$ norm.
Thus $C_q\leq(1+d)C$ is sufficient.

Solve on $X_q$
\begin{equation}
 \int\operatorname{curl}w\cdot\operatorname{curl}\overline z
       -\kappa^2q w\cdot\overline z
 =\kappa^2\int qE_0\cdot\overline z.
 \label{hs:three-local-variational}
\end{equation}
For $\kappa^2\varepsilon_h C_q^2<1/2$ this is coercive and gives
$\|w\|_{H(\operatorname{curl})}\leq C\kappa^2\|E_0\|_2$.
It is also the equation for every test in $H_0(\operatorname{curl})$:
the weighted Helmholtz decomposition splits such a test into an
$X_q$ part and a gradient from $H_0^1$, and both sides vanish on
the gradient part.  Hence $E=E_0+w$ satisfies
$\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E=\kappa^2qE$ and
$H=(i\kappa)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}E$ completes the exact Maxwell
solution.

On an interior ball, cutoff curl/div regularity gives
\begin{equation}
 \|\operatorname{curl}w\|_{H^1(B_{1/2})}
 \leq C\left(\|\operatorname{curl}w\|_2
              +\kappa^2\|q(E_0+w)\|_2\right)=O(\kappa^2).
 \label{hs:three-local-curl-small}
\end{equation}
Apply the lifting to $\operatorname{curl}w$.  Its $W$ has $H^2$
norm $O(\kappa^2)$; the potential in $E=\nabla v+W$ differs from
$r^\lambda\phi$, up to a constant, by $O(\kappa^2)$ in $H^1$.
The averaged formula in Lemma~\ref{hs:three-coefficient} therefore
gives $c(E)=1+O(\kappa^2)$.  This proves nontriviality for small
positive $\kappa$.  The local tangential boundary data used to
construct this solution are a mathematical matching device.  The
physical construction below uses global incoming waves and has no
PEC boundary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A uniform finite-device Maxwell inverse at fixed small contrast}
\label{hs:three-volume-subsection}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: uniform volume-integral inverse]
\label{hs:three-volume}
Fix a ball $B_L$ and $k>0$.  There is $C_{kL}<\infty$ such that
the electric volume operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal K_k f=(k^2I+\nabla\nabla)\Phi_k*f,
 \qquad \Phi_k(x)=\frac{e^{ik|x|}}{4\pi|x|},
 \label{hs:three-volume-operator}
\end{equation}
restricted to $B_L$, with $f$ zero extended, has $L^2$ norm at most
$C_{kL}$.  For $0<d<(2C_{kL})^{-1}$ and any measurable
$0\leq\chi\leq d$ supported in $B_L$,
\begin{equation}
 E=E^{\rm in}+\mathcal K_k(\chi E)
 \label{hs:three-volume-equation}
\end{equation}
has a unique solution and
$\|(I-\mathcal K_k\chi)^{-1}\|\leq2$.
It is the unique outgoing Maxwell scattering field for
$\varepsilon_r=1+\chi$.  An outgoing electric source problem with
compact $L^2$ forcing is likewise well posed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split
$\mathcal K_k=\nabla\nabla\Phi_0*
 +[k^2I\Phi_k+\nabla\nabla(\Phi_k-\Phi_0)]*$.
The first operator has Fourier multiplier
$-\xi\xi^T/|\xi|^2$, of norm at most one on all-space $L^2$;
this includes its distributional diagonal term.  The bracketed
matrix kernel is locally integrable: the expansion
$\Phi_k-\Phi_0=ik/(4\pi)-k^2|x|/(8\pi)+O(k^3|x|^2)$
shows a Hessian of order $1/|x|$.  Away from zero it is smooth.
Young's inequality after cutting the kernel off at $|x|=2L$
therefore bounds the remainder by its finite matrix $L^1$ norm.
Rescaling $x$ by $L$ shows that the resulting constant depends only
on $kL$.  This proves the operator estimate.

The Neumann series proves the inverse bound, uniformly over all
the admitted geometries.  The dyadic kernel
$k^{-2}\mathcal K_k$ is the outgoing fundamental solution of
$\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}-k^2$.  Consequently
\eqref{hs:three-volume-equation} gives the Maxwell equations and
radiation condition in distributions.  Conversely, subtracting
that representation from an outgoing solution gives an entire
outgoing vacuum solution, which is zero by the spherical-wave
expansion.  This proves uniqueness and equivalence, including for
discontinuous $\chi$.  An electric forcing $f\in L^2_c$ replaces
$E^{\rm in}$ by $k^{-2}\mathcal K_k f$ on a ball enclosing its
support; the same inverse applies when that support is in $B_L$.
Only this latter case is needed below.
\end{proof}

For the theorem choose $L=1/k$ and fix once and for all
\begin{equation}
 0<d<\min\{\varepsilon_+-1,(2C_1)^{-1}\},
 \qquad \varepsilon_h=1+d.
 \label{hs:three-fixed-contrast}
\end{equation}
Every finite collection of tips and inclusions can be placed in a
much smaller ball whose projected closure lies in $\mathcal P$.
The containing comparison ball of radius $1/k$ is only used for
the inverse; it is not extra material.

\subsection{Physical illumination, total-field density, and rounding}
\label{hs:three-global-subsection}

We now restore physical units: $E$ is the physical electric field
and $H$ is the physical magnetic field, without the local $Z_0$
rescaling used above.  For a smooth tangential density
$g:S^2\to\mathbb C^3$,
$d\cdot g(d)=0$, use the physical electric incident field
\begin{equation}
 E_g^{\rm in}(x)=\frac{k\sqrt{Z_0}}{\sqrt2\,\pi}
                 \int_{S^2}g(d)e^{ikd\cdot x}\,dS(d),
 \qquad
 H_g^{\rm in}(x)=\frac{k}{\sqrt{2Z_0}\,\pi}
                 \int_{S^2}d\times g(d)e^{ikd\cdot x}\,dS(d).
 \label{hs:three-illumination}
\end{equation}
Stationary phase on the sphere gives an incoming electric amplitude
$\sqrt{2Z_0}\,g(-\widehat x)/r$, up to a phase of unit modulus.
The radial incoming flux is $|E^{\rm in}_{\rm incoming}|^2/(2Z_0)$,
so the total incoming power is exactly $\|g\|_{L^2(S^2)}^2$.
This is finite total power, rather than power per invariant length.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: total-field Maxwell Runge approximation for these devices]
\label{hs:three-runge}
Let the small-contrast coefficient be piecewise constant, with finitely
many smooth interface pieces and finitely many conical vertices and
joining curves.  Let $Q$ be a closed ball, or a finite disjoint union
of closed balls, compactly contained in the comparison ball $B_L$
and with connected complement.  A source-free Maxwell
electric field defined on a neighborhood of $Q$ can be approximated
in $L^2(Q)^3$ by total electric fields from
\eqref{hs:three-illumination}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $f\in L^2(Q)^3$ annihilating all such total fields under the
complex bilinear pairing, and solve
$(\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}-k^2\varepsilon_r)W=f$
outgoing by Lemma~\ref{hs:three-volume}.  Orthogonality to every
smooth tangential density says
$\int_Q f\cdot E_{d,p}=0$ for all incident plane waves
$p e^{ikd\cdot x}$ with $p\cdot d=0$.  Continuity in $d,p$
justifies passing from integrated illuminations to individual
plane waves.  Maxwell Green's identity, without complex conjugation,
equates this integral to a nonzero constant times
$p\cdot W_\infty(-d)$.  One may obtain the identity directly by
inserting the dyadic outgoing representation; the pairing of two
outgoing solutions has zero boundary Wronskian.  Since the electric
far field is tangential, testing all $p\perp d$ proves
$W_\infty=0$.  The vacuum vector spherical-wave expansion then
gives $W=0$ outside a sufficiently large sphere.

The exterior of $Q$ is connected.  Unique continuation here is
elementary within each material: the electric and magnetic fields
satisfy constant-coefficient Helmholtz equations.  Zero extends
across an open smooth interface because both tangential electric
and magnetic traces are zero; extending by zero gives a weak
constant-coefficient Maxwell solution on a ball crossing that
interface, hence an analytic one.  Paths avoid the finitely many
vertices and joining curves and cross only open interface pieces.
Thus $W=0$ outside $Q$.
Both $W$ and $\operatorname{curl}W$ are in local
$H(\operatorname{curl})$, and have zero tangential traces on
$\partial Q$.  For every local homogeneous solution $V$, Green's
identity consequently gives
$\int_Q f\cdot V=\int_QW\cdot
(\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}-k^2\varepsilon_r)V=0$.
The annihilator characterization of the $L^2$ closure proves density.
This is density of total fields in the actual material, not an
assumed free-space approximation inside it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED: finite-device rounding continuity]
\label{hs:three-rounding}
Let $\chi_\rho,\chi$ be supported in the common comparison ball,
$0\leq\chi_\rho,\chi\leq d$, with $\chi_\rho\to\chi$ almost
everywhere.  For one fixed incident field, their total electric
fields satisfy $E_\rho\to E$ in $L^2(B_L)^3$.
On a fixed compact observation set avoiding all vertices and joining
curves, and near which the coefficients are unchanged, convergence
is uniform on each closed material side.  This includes fixed
sphere collars intersecting smooth conical interfaces.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Subtract the volume equations:
\begin{equation}
 E_\rho-E=(I-\mathcal K_k\chi_\rho)^{-1}
                  \mathcal K_k((\chi_\rho-\chi)E).
 \label{hs:three-rounding-identity}
\end{equation}
Dominated convergence makes the last multiplication tend to zero
in $L^2$, and Lemma~\ref{hs:three-volume} bounds both operators
uniformly.  This proves the first claim.

For the second, the field difference solves the same homogeneous
Maxwell equation in every fixed observation neighborhood.
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-decomposition} produces
$E_\rho-E=\nabla v_\rho+W_\rho$ there, with
$\|v_\rho\|_{H^1}+\|W_\rho\|_{H^2}\to0$ on smaller balls.
The potential satisfies
$\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon_r\nabla v_\rho)
=-\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon_r W_\rho)$.
Across a fixed smooth interface this is the scalar positive
transmission problem with continuous potential and conormal jump
$-[\varepsilon_r W_\rho\cdot n]$.
Its local piecewise $H^3$ estimate is bounded by
$C(\|v_\rho\|_{H^1}+\|W_\rho\|_{H^2})$.
To see the required regularity, flatten the fixed smooth interface,
apply tangential difference quotients twice to the scalar weak
equation, and use ellipticity and cutoffs to estimate them.
The equation then determines second normal derivatives; differentiating
it once determines third normal derivatives.  The coefficient and
flattening derivatives are bounded on this fixed neighborhood.
The two material-side estimates include the displayed conormal
data, whose trace has the required $H^{3/2}$ regularity from
$W_\rho\in H^2$.  Thus
$\nabla v_\rho+W_\rho\to0$ piecewise in $H^2$.
Since $H^2$ embeds in $C^{0,\gamma}$ in three dimensions,
uniform one-sided convergence follows.  A finite cover treats the
observation set.  No estimate uniform at the shrinking tip is used.
The same local argument gives bounded one-sided electric fields
for each fixed smooth rounded geometry.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{hs:three-packing}]
Select distinct centers $p_j$ in the planar patch and sufficiently
small disjoint balls about them.  Put two opposite dielectric cones
at each center, with opening angle from \eqref{hs:three-caps}.
They agree exactly with the homogeneous conical geometry on a
smaller ball, and are smoothly capped farther away, all within
the selected neighborhood.  Their closures touch only at the
center in this sharp reference geometry.  The projections of all
these neighborhoods lie in $\mathcal P$, and their total support
fits in a ball of radius $1/k$.

For each selected vertex, Lemma~\ref{hs:three-local-excitation}
constructs a local finite-frequency field with nonzero coefficient.
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-runge} approximates it on a slightly larger
ball than that used to measure the coefficient.
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-coefficient} then shows that the coefficient
$c_j(E_g)$ is a nonzero continuous linear functional of the smooth
tangential incident density $g$.  Choose one density outside the
finite union of its kernels, exactly as in
Proposition~\ref{hs:simultaneous}, and divide by its $L^2(S^2)$ norm.
All coefficients remain nonzero, and the incoming power is one.

Fix disjoint observation balls $Q_j$ of diameter less than $\ell$
inside the exact conical regions.  The field is bounded on their
fixed interface collars by the regularity argument in
Lemma~\ref{hs:three-rounding}; call a common bound $B$.
Choose $S>\max\{M,2B\}$.  At each vertex the nonzero coefficient
supplies an ordinary interior point $x_j$ with $|E(x_j)|>4S$.
Now round all conical tips inward within radius $\rho$, leaving
the interfaces elsewhere unchanged.  The result has $2N$ disjoint
inclusions with smooth boundaries and the same two material values.
The changed sets shrink to the finite collection of vertices.

Lemma~\ref{hs:three-rounding} permits one sufficiently small common
$\rho$ for which every $|E_\rho(x_j)|>3S$ and every collar amplitude
is less than $S$.  One-sided maxima on the compact observation
balls are finite and attained for the fixed smooth configuration.
Each exceeds $3S>M$, and its half-amplitude component cannot cross
its collar.  This proves \eqref{hs:three-peak} and the theorem.
All choices of illumination precede rounding; the same normalized
input is used for every rounded field.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[PROVED: finite-region volume-to-peak ratios in three dimensions]
\label{hs:three-regional-volume}
Along the rounded sequence for one fixed sharp configuration and
one normalized input, electromagnetic energy in a fixed bounded
region stays bounded, while the electric maximum in every selected
ball tends to infinity.  Each resulting finite-region energy-to-peak
ratio tends to zero.  This is a driven local-energy statement, not
an all-space modal normalization.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The electric $L^2$ norms are uniformly bounded by the volume inverse.
The magnetic fields are bounded on fixed bounded regions by the
Maxwell equations, the local curl estimate, and exterior vacuum
regularity; a slightly larger comparison ball may be used for the
cutoffs.  For every finite amplitude threshold, select high ordinary
points in the sharp field first and then use rounding continuity.
The finite diagonal argument makes the lower limit of each selected
maximum infinite.  The material factors are bounded above and below.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What an exact mode can mean in a finite dielectric device}
\label{hs:three-modal-subsection}

\begin{proposition}[PROVED: absence of real-frequency finite-energy modes]
\label{hs:three-no-real-mode}
Let the real positive scalar permittivity be constant on finitely
many material pieces with piecewise smooth interfaces, and equal
one outside a bounded set.  At a fixed real $\omega>0$, a source-free
Maxwell field with finite all-space electromagnetic energy is zero.
The same conclusion holds for an outgoing source-free field,
without a finite-energy assumption.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the exterior vacuum every Cartesian component satisfies the
scalar Helmholtz equation.  A spherical harmonic expansion of an
$L^2$ exterior solution has radial terms proportional to incoming
and outgoing spherical Hankel functions.  Each nonzero radial
combination has nondecaying mean radial $L^2$ mass, because its
leading amplitude is a nonzero combination of $e^{ikr}$ and
$e^{-ikr}$ divided by $r$.  It cannot be square integrable on
$(R,\infty)$ with volume weight $r^2$.  Orthogonality of spherical
harmonics excludes cancellation between different angular terms.
Thus a finite-energy field is zero in the exterior.
For an outgoing source-free field, the real part of the complex
Poynting identity instead sets its nonnegative far-field radiation
power to zero, giving the same exterior conclusion.
Tangential electric and magnetic continuity then propagates zero
across each open smooth material interface by the constant-coefficient
extension argument used in Lemma~\ref{hs:three-runge}.
Paths can avoid the finite lower-dimensional interface singular sets.
Hence the field is zero everywhere.
\end{proof}

This proposition diagnoses an inappropriate literal interpretation
of the modal numerator; it does \emph{not} refute the tutorial's
high-$Q$ or quasinormal-mode interpretation.  A finite radiating
resonator has complex-frequency poles.  Their outgoing fields grow
at spatial infinity and require a specified regularized mode norm,
or a justified high-$Q$ approximation, instead of the ordinary
positive all-space energy integral.  The tutorial explicitly notes
open-cavity generalizations of its mode-volume definition.

\begin{remark}[The driven proof and the separate modal construction]
\label{hs:three-modal-gap}
Theorem~\ref{hs:three-packing} closes the finite-device \emph{driven}
point-localization target just stated.  It does not turn the chosen
illumination into a resonator mode.  A possible modal route starts
with a simple isolated dielectric resonance, inserts finitely many
small conical inclusions where its electric field is nonzero, and
then rounds their tips.  To complete that route one must prove
that a single continued resonance has nonzero conical coefficients
at every selected vertex, and that the chosen resonance normalization
stays finite and nonzero under insertion and rounding.  These are
not consequences of Runge approximation: Runge varies external
illumination, whereas a pole's eigenfield is fixed by its homogeneous
outgoing eigenproblem.  Strong local field convergence without a
coefficient topology also does not prove nonzero singular coefficients.
Sections~\ref{hs:modal-next-section} and~\ref{ms:section} now discharge
these conditions by a separate argument.  The exact coefficient
continuation through zero contrast is proved in
Theorem~\ref{hs:modal-coefficient-continuation}; a simple pole,
its genuine reciprocal normalization and its rounded continuation
are proved in Lemmas~\ref{ms:sphere-simple}--\ref{ms:rounding}.
The resulting closed modal statement is
Theorem~\ref{ms:modal-packing}.  It does not rely on varying a driven
illumination or on a divergent all-space positive energy integral.
\end{remark}
