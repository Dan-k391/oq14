2.1 Distributional consequences of actual Maxwell equations

Because epsilon is bounded and E,H are locally L², (M) gives locally L² curls. Taking distributional divergences and using div curl=0 gives

  ik div H=0,        -ik div(epsilon E)=0.

Since k!=0,

  div H=0,          div(epsilon E)=0.

Taking the curl of the first equation, with k a spatial constant, gives

  curl curl E=ik(-ik epsilon E)=k² epsilon E.                    (2.1)

The square is k², not |k|². No derivative of epsilon is introduced. These identities use the actual global-on-the-patch weak equations, not independent equations on each side with assumed interface matching.

For completeness, the stronger theorem 05.1 only assumes E and F in L² and the distributional equation curl curl E=F. The next step derives its initially missing H(curl) regularity.

2.2 Curl Caccioppoli estimate, justified from L² data

Fix real eta in C_c^infinity(U), 0<=eta<=1. Choose a slightly larger compact neighborhood of its support still inside U. Mollify E and the entire field F there, using the same mollifier scale t, small enough that the convolution sees only U. Since curl curl has constant coefficients,

  curl curl E_t=F_t

on a neighborhood of supp eta. We do NOT replace the convolution of epsilon E by epsilon times the convolution of E.

For the smooth fields integrate by parts using the compactly supported test eta² conjugate(E_t). With q_t=curl E_t, the Hermitian pairing and its real part give

  integral eta² |q_t|²
   = Re integral F_t dot eta² conjugate(E_t)
       -2 Re integral eta q_t dot (grad eta x conjugate(E_t)).

All integrals are finite: the fields are smooth on the compact test support, and the same estimates below use only local L² norms. There are no material-boundary terms because the identity is applied on the whole patch.

Set X=||eta q_t||_2 and D=||grad eta||_infinity ||E_t||_2, using an enlarged fixed set for the norms. Cauchy-Schwarz and 2XD <= X²/2+2D² yield

  ||eta q_t||_2²
     <=2||F_t||_2||E_t||_2
          +4||grad eta||_infinity² ||E_t||_2².                   (2.2)

Convolution is locally L² bounded on that enlarged set, and E_t -> E, F_t -> F in the local L² norms. Thus q_t is uniformly L² on a smaller set where eta=1. A weakly convergent L² subsequence has distributional limit curl E, because differentiation is continuous on distributions and E_t -> E there. It follows that q=curl E belongs to L² locally. Weak lower semicontinuity, followed if necessary by a slightly larger cutoff, gives (2.2) for E,F,q.

In particular, for nested fixed patches U_1 compactly contained in U,

  ||q||_{L²(U_1)} <= C(||E||_{L²(U)}+||F||_{L²(U)}).             (2.3)

One can instead test directly after H(curl) membership is known: compactly supported H(curl) tests are approximated in their graph norm by whole-space zero extension and mollification. Both sides of the weak curl identity are continuous in that norm. The preliminary mollification argument shows that no H(curl) assumption is being hidden in theorem 05.1.

The argument uses neither real k nor positivity of Re(k²). This is why it also applies to complex-frequency differences.

2.3 The Fourier div-curl estimate, including the order-one version

For real xi and complex z in C³,

  |xi|²|z|²=|xi x z|²+|xi dot z|².                              (2.4)

This follows by choosing an orthonormal basis with first vector xi/|xi| and summing the longitudinal and two transverse squared moduli. It remains valid for complex z because the norm is Hermitian and xi is real.

Plancherel and the distributional Fourier differentiation rule imply: if Z, curl Z and div Z belong to L²(R³), then Z belongs to H¹ and

  ||grad Z||_2²=||curl Z||_2²+||div Z||_2².                      (2.5)

Conversely, the Fourier functions i xi_j Zhat are L² by (2.4), so they define the weak derivatives; one is not first assuming Z in H¹. Multiplying (2.4) by 1+|xi|² gives, with the ordinary equivalence of integer Sobolev and Fourier norms,

  ||Z||_{H²} <= C(||Z||_2+||curl Z||_{H¹}+||div Z||_{H¹}).        (2.6)

These whole-space estimates localize by cutoffs. If theta is smooth and compactly supported,

  curl(theta Z)=theta curl Z+grad theta x Z,
  div(theta Z)=theta div Z+grad theta dot Z.                      (2.7)

The identities follow by the distributional product rule. First use (2.5) on an outer cutoff to obtain local H¹. On a smaller cutoff the commutator terms in (2.7) are then H¹, and (2.6) yields local H² if curl Z and div Z are locally H¹. This order accounts for the derivatives of cutoff terms without circularity.

Apply (2.5) to a cutoff of q=curl E. Since curl q=F and div q=0, (2.3) gives on a smaller fixed patch

  ||q||_{H¹(U_2)} <= C(||E||_{L²(U)}+||F||_{L²(U)}).             (2.8)

This H¹ regularity holds across the interface. Derivatives of epsilon have not been used.

2.4 A fixed linear local decomposition E=grad v+W

Choose a physical ball B inside U large enough to contain the enlarged interface patch of interest. Choose theta in C_c^infinity(U), equal to 1 on a slightly larger neighborhood of B. Extend theta E by zero to R³. Define the bounded Fourier transverse projection

  W=P_T(theta E),
  W_hat(xi)=(I-xi tensor xi/|xi|²)(theta E)_hat(xi), xi!=0.      (2.9)

The value of the multiplier at xi=0 is arbitrary and immaterial to L². It is an orthogonal projection at every nonzero xi, so

  ||W||_{L²(R³)} <= ||theta E||_2 <= ||E||_{L²(U)},
  div W=0,
  curl W=curl(theta E)                                         (2.10)

distributionally. The last equality follows because xi x P_T(xi)z=xi x z. Thus curl W=q wherever theta=1.

The nested versions of (2.5) and (2.6), together with (2.8), give

  ||W||_{H²(B)} <= C(||E||_{L²(U)}+||F||_{L²(U)}).               (2.11)

On B, curl(E-W)=0. Here is the weak potential argument on a slightly smaller ball B'. Mollify the curl-free L² vector on B and restrict to B'. For each small scale the result is smooth and curl free. A classical potential is obtained by integration along the straight segment from the center of B' to x. Differentiation of that segment formula and curl-freeness give gradient equal to the mollified vector. Subtract its mean on B'.

The mean-zero Poincare inequality bounds the H¹ norm of the potential by its gradient L² norm. Applying the same inequality to the difference of two mean-zero potentials shows they are Cauchy in H¹, because their gradients converge in L². Their limit v therefore satisfies

  grad v=E-W,
  integral_{B'}v=0,
  ||v||_{H¹(B')} <= C||E-W||_{L²(B')} <= C||E||_{L²(U)}.         (2.12)

All constructions are linear. Uniqueness of the mean-zero potential follows because an H¹ function with zero gradient on the connected ball is constant. The latter fact follows by mollifying locally, noting that the smooth gradients are zero, and using connected overlapping balls. In particular the potential is independent of the chosen approximation sequence.

For reference, the mean-zero Poincare inequality on a ball is the usual bounded-domain inequality ||v-v_B||_2 <= C radius(B)||grad v||_2. It follows first for smooth functions by the segment/averaging proof on a ball and then by H¹ density; the assumptions here are exactly a fixed ball and the mean-zero normalization. No boundary condition is imposed on E or on the physical problem.

This supplies the local decomposition required by the manuscript without invoking its unquoted regularized-lifting lemma. The estimates for v and W do not depend on derivatives of epsilon.

2.5 The scalar equation and the initial conormal trace

The global-on-the-patch identity div(epsilon E)=0 and (2.12) give, for every smooth compact scalar test phi,

  integral epsilon grad v dot grad conjugate(phi)
    =-integral epsilon W dot grad conjugate(phi).                (2.13)

Both products are integrable by L², boundedness of epsilon, and the compact support. The identity extends to H¹ tests with compact support by smooth density and Cauchy-Schwarz.

Since v is H¹ on the entire ball, its two scalar traces on Gamma agree in H^{1/2}. The flux

  p=epsilon(grad v+W)=epsilon E

belongs to L² and has distributional divergence zero. Restrict to local Lipschitz half-patches given by the smooth interface charts; on each it is an H(div) field. Its normal trace is initially an H^{-1/2} functional: for boundary datum psi with an H¹ lifting Phi, define the normal pairing by integral p dot grad Phi + integral (div p)Phi. The definition is independent of the lifting because their difference is H¹_0 and the distributional divergence identity extends by density. The bounded lifting/trace inequality, explicitly justified in 2.12, gives the H^{-1/2} bound. Also div(grad v)=-div W in each material side, so grad v is itself locally H(div) there and its initial normal trace is defined.

Adding the two side Green identities and comparing with the whole-patch identity yields matching normal flux. With the common normal convention,

  [v]=0,       [epsilon partial_nu v]=-[epsilon W dot nu]        (2.14)

initially as weak traces. We have not assumed H³ for v or classical normal derivatives. W in H² already gives an H^{3/2} trace of its values on the fixed smooth interface, so the known right-hand jump has that regularity. The proof below uses (2.13) directly and derives the regularity of the unknown side derivatives.

2.6 Actual flattening and transformed equations

After a rigid orientation-preserving coordinate change, write the interface as

  x_3=gamma(x_1,x_2)

on a fixed patch, with gamma in C³. Use the graph map

  Phi(y)=(y_1,y_2,y_3+gamma(y_1,y_2)),
  J=D Phi,    det J=1,
  G=(det J)J^{-1}J^{-T}.                                       (2.15)

Phi maps Q^+={y_3>0} and Q^-={y_3<0} to the material sides. G is real symmetric positive definite, belongs to W^{2,infinity} on a smaller fixed cube, and in this graph map depends only on y_1,y_2. Bounds for gamma through its third derivatives control G and its derivatives through order two. More general orientation-preserving C³ charts give the same formulas with their positive Jacobian.

Put

  u=v composed with Phi,
  w=J^T(W composed with Phi),
  A(y)=epsilon_± G(y) on Q^±,
  g(y)=-epsilon_± (det J)J^{-1}(W composed with Phi)=-A w.        (2.16)

Change of variables and grad_x v=J^{-T}grad_y u transform (2.13) into

  integral_Q A grad u dot grad conjugate(psi)
    =integral_Q g dot grad conjugate(psi)                       (2.17)

for every compactly supported H¹ test psi on the full cube. The substitution is justified for H¹ functions by smooth approximation and the bounded chart Jacobians; the chain rule and change of variables pass in L². Applying the same argument through orders two and three proves the Sobolev norm comparisons used below.

There are fixed lambda_0,Lambda_0>0 with

  lambda_0 |z|² <= conjugate(z) dot A(y)z <= Lambda_0 |z|²,
  max_± ||A||_{W^{2,infinity}(Q^±)} < infinity,
  ||g||_{H²_pw(Q)} <= C Lambda ||W||_{H²(Phi(Q))}.              (2.18)

For example lambda_0 >= lambda inf(det J)/sup||J||² and
Lambda_0 <= Lambda sup(det J) sup||J^{-1}||². The derivative constants are controlled by the fixed chart data. Products of a W^{2,infinity} matrix and an H² function are H² by the weak product rule and the L-infinity coefficient bounds.

For clarity, the corresponding transformed first-order Maxwell fields are the covariant fields e=J^T(E composed with Phi), h=J^T(H composed with Phi). The curl and Piola identities give

  curl_y e=ik G h,
  curl_y h=-ik epsilon G e,
  div_y(epsilon G e)=0,       div_y(G h)=0.                     (2.19)

The curl identity is curl_y(J^T E composed with Phi)=(det J)J^{-1}(curl_x E composed with Phi); the derivative-of-J terms cancel by symmetry of second derivatives. The divergence identity is the corresponding change-of-variables identity for compact tests. Smooth approximation proves both distributionally for the present fields. Thus flattening does not preserve the naive constant-coefficient Maxwell equations. Our scalar proof uses the correctly transformed equation (2.17), not that naive replacement.

The next five subsections prove the required transmission estimate for (2.17) from its weak formulation.

2.7 Scalar transmission lemma and difference-quotient facts

Here is the precise scalar lemma we prove. Let Q_R=(-R,R)^3, Q_R^±=Q_R intersect {±y_3>0}, 0<r<R. Let A^± be real symmetric W^{2,infinity} matrices on the half-cubes, with common ellipticity bounds lambda_0,Lambda_0. Let g^± be H² vector fields. Let u in H¹(Q_R) satisfy (2.17) for every compact H¹ test. Then

  ||u||_{H³_pw(Q_r)}
   <= C(lambda_0,Lambda_0,M_1,M_2,R-r)
        (||u||_{H¹(Q_R)}+||g||_{H²_pw(Q_R)}),                   (2.20)

where M_j=max_±||D^j A^±||_infinity, j=1,2. In particular no estimate on an unknown conormal trace is a hypothesis. This slightly more general lemma includes (2.16); it does not require A to be independent of y_3.

For alpha=1,2, write T_h f(y)=f(y+h e_alpha) and D_h f=(T_h f-f)/h. Use h small compared with the distance between the chosen nested cubes. Tangential translation preserves each half-cube. Consequently,

  ||D_h u||_{L²(inner)} <= ||partial_alpha u||_{L²(outer)},
  ||D_h g||_{L²(inner)} <= ||partial_alpha g||_{L²_pw(outer)},
  ||D_h A||_infinity <= M_1.                                  (2.21)

For smooth functions the first two inequalities follow from
D_h f=integral_0^1 partial_alpha f(y+t h e_alpha)dt, Jensen's inequality, and translation of the integral. Density gives the Sobolev versions. The coefficients' inequality follows from their Lipschitz representatives on the individual sides. Boundary terms at y_3=0 do not occur for tangential differentiation, because the interface normal has zero alpha-component. Hence the piecewise tangential derivatives of g and A are their global distributional tangential derivatives.

The difference-quotient compactness criterion used below is: if f in L² and D_h f is uniformly bounded in L² on every smaller domain for h ->0 in a fixed coordinate direction, that weak partial derivative belongs to L² with the same bound. To see it, take a weakly convergent subsequence of D_h f and use

  integral D_h f phi = -integral f D_{-h} phi.

For each compact smooth test, D_{-h}phi converges uniformly with fixed compact support to partial_alpha phi; Cauchy-Schwarz passes the integral to the limit. The weak limit is the distributional derivative. Apply this also to the components of grad u when estimating D_h u in H¹. These arguments use weak compactness in the Hilbert space L² and no unproved differentiability of u.

2.8 First tangential derivatives: the energy calculation

Translate (2.17), subtract, and use the exact product rule

  D_h(A grad u)=(T_h A) grad D_h u+(D_h A)grad u.

On a smaller cube the difference w=D_h u satisfies

  integral (T_h A) grad w dot grad conjugate(phi)
   =integral R_h dot grad conjugate(phi),
  R_h=D_h g-(D_h A)grad u.                                   (2.22)

For a fixed smooth cutoff eta supported in that cube, phi=eta²w belongs to H¹_0 and is admissible by the density extension of the weak equation. For each fixed nonzero h, w is H¹ because u is H¹. Put

  a=||eta grad w||_2, b=||w grad eta||_2, d=||eta R_h||_2.

Taking real parts, ellipticity and boundedness give

  lambda_0 a² <= 2 Lambda_0 a b + d a + 2 d b.

Apply Young's inequality to the first two terms, with lambda_0 a²/4 allocated to each, and use 2db<=d²+b²:

  (lambda_0/2)a²
   <= (4 Lambda_0²/lambda_0+1)b²+(1/lambda_0+1)d².              (2.23)

The b term is bounded by ||grad eta||_infinity ||partial_alpha u||_2 using (2.21). The d term is bounded by

  ||partial_alpha g||_{L²_pw}+M_1||grad u||_2.

Therefore, on nested cubes with gap d_0>0,

  ||partial_alpha u||_{H¹(inner)}
   <= C(lambda_0,Lambda_0)
      [||partial_alpha g||_{L²_pw(outer)}
        +(1+M_1+d_0^{-1})||u||_{H¹(outer)}].                   (2.24)

The passage h ->0 is the weak compactness argument of 2.7. This proves, across the whole flat interface, that u_1 and u_2 are H¹. All second derivatives with at least one tangential index are now L². The proof also establishes the same estimate for any weak solution whose forcing and its derivative in the selected tangential direction are L²; no normal derivative of that forcing was used.

2.9 Recover the second normal derivative

Restrict (2.17) to either open half-cube. In distributions there,

  sum_{i,j} partial_i(A_ij u_j)=sum_i partial_i g_i.

Let a=A_33. Expanding the weak product rule and separating u_33 gives

  a u_33
   =sum_i partial_i g_i
      -sum_{(i,j)!=(3,3)} A_ij u_ij
      -sum_{i,j}(partial_i A_ij)u_j.                           (2.25)

Every term on the right other than the initially unknown u_33 is L²: g is piecewise H¹, all u_ij with (i,j)!=(3,3) have a tangential index and are controlled by (2.24), and coefficient derivatives are bounded. To make division rigorous before u_33 is known to be a function, first keep the term partial_3(a u_3) unexpanded in the divergence equation. All remaining terms are L², so partial_3(a u_3) is L². Since a u_3 is L² and 1/a is W^{1,infinity}, the directional weak product rule applied to u_3=(1/a)(a u_3) proves u_33 is L² and then yields (2.25) as an equality of L² functions. The norm bound follows by the triangle inequality and lambda_0^{-1}. Thus u is piecewise H², proved before any third derivative is used.

All normal differentiations here and below take place separately inside the open material sides. No normal derivative of the discontinuous whole coefficient is suppressed.

2.10 Two tangential derivatives

The distributional tangential derivative of (2.17), justified by 2.8 and tangential difference quotients, is

  integral A grad u_alpha dot grad conjugate(phi)
   =integral g^alpha dot grad conjugate(phi),
  g^alpha=partial_alpha g-(partial_alpha A)grad u.              (2.26)

For alpha,beta in {1,2},

  partial_beta g^alpha
   =partial_{alpha beta}g
      -(partial_{alpha beta}A)grad u
      -(partial_alpha A)grad u_beta.                           (2.27)

This is L² on a slightly smaller full cube: the data have two tangential derivatives, the coefficients have two bounded derivatives, and u_beta is H¹ by (2.24). In particular this step does not presume u_alpha already has two derivatives, and no derivative crossing the coefficient jump is taken.

Apply the proved first-tangential estimate (2.24), in direction beta, to the H¹ function u_alpha and equation (2.26). It gives

  u_{alpha beta} in H¹(inner),
  sum_{alpha,beta=1}^2 ||u_{alpha beta}||_{H¹(inner)}
    <= C(||u||_{H¹(Q_R)}+||g||_{H²_pw(Q_R)}).                  (2.28)

All third derivatives containing at least two tangential indices are therefore L², with the claimed data bound. Nested cutoffs are fixed before applying these estimates.

2.11 Recover every third derivative, with no circular step

Differentiate (2.25) tangentially in alpha=1,2, within either material side:

  a u_{33 alpha}
   =sum_i partial_{alpha i}g_i -(partial_alpha a)u_33
     -sum_{(i,j)!=(3,3)}[(partial_alpha A_ij)u_ij+A_ij u_{ij alpha}]
     -sum_{i,j}[(partial_{alpha i}A_ij)u_j
                                      +(partial_i A_ij)u_{j alpha}]. (2.29)

All third derivatives on the right have at least two tangential indices and are controlled by (2.28). All second derivatives are controlled by 2.9. Data and coefficient derivatives of the required orders are controlled by H²_pw and W^{2,infinity}. Division by a>=lambda_0 gives u_{33 alpha} in L².

Now differentiate (2.25) in the normal direction, still separately on each side:

  a u_333
   =sum_i partial_{3i}g_i -(partial_3 a)u_33
     -sum_{(i,j)!=(3,3)}[(partial_3 A_ij)u_ij+A_ij u_{ij3}]
     -sum_{i,j}[(partial_{3i}A_ij)u_j+(partial_i A_ij)u_{j3}].    (2.30)

A derivative u_{ij3} on the right either has two tangential indices, controlled by (2.28), or has two normal and one tangential index, controlled by (2.29). Thus the right-hand side is L². Division by a proves u_333 in L². In the special graph map (2.15) all normal coefficient derivatives vanish on each side, but the calculation above does not need that simplification.

These formulas identify the weak derivatives: the equations were distributional identities and their right sides have now been proved L². As in 2.9, one can first recover partial_alpha(a u_33), then partial_3(a u_33), in L² and apply the weak product rule with 1/a; this rigorously justifies each displayed division. They do not differentiate an arbitrary pointwise representative. All derivatives of order at most three are accounted for. This proves the scalar estimate (2.20).

For quantitative bookkeeping take, for example, eight nested cubes with equal gaps (R-r)/8. Equations (2.23)–(2.30) construct C using finitely many sums and products of dimension-dependent constants, 1+lambda_0^{-1}, 1+Lambda_0, 1+M_1+M_2, and 1+(R-r)^{-1}. No field amplitude, frequency, or rounding index enters it. This specifies its dependence without claiming a sharp or unnecessary numerical value.

2.12 Completion of the local electric estimate and trace regularity

Apply (2.20) to (2.16). Sobolev chain rules, bounded Jacobians, and (2.18) give on smaller physical side patches

  ||v||_{H³_pw} <= C(||v||_{H¹}+||W||_{H²}).                   (2.31)

Equations (2.11)–(2.12) and E=grad v+W now prove (1.2). In a patch wholly inside one material the same proof works with a constant scalar coefficient, or one can apply difference quotients in all three directions. A finite cover proves the assertion on V. There is no global topological assumption on the material sides: the potentials were constructed on full physical balls, not on possibly non-simply-connected individual phases.

The Sobolev trace facts used above have the following exact scope. On a fixed smooth graph patch the value trace maps H¹ to H^{1/2} and H² to H^{3/2}; the normal derivative trace of an H³ scalar is H^{3/2}. A local verification is obtained by flattening and taking the Fourier transform in the two tangential variables. For a smooth compact function h on the upper half-space, integration of partial_3 |hhat(xi',y_3)|² and Cauchy-Schwarz give

  |hhat(xi',0)|²
   <= t integral_0^infinity |hhat|² dy_3
       +t^{-1} integral_0^infinity |partial_3 hhat|² dy_3,

where t=(1+|xi'|²)^{1/2}. Multiply by t for H¹ traces or by t³ for H² traces and integrate xi'. The resulting right sides are bounded by the corresponding H¹ or H² norms. Approximation and a bounded local half-space extension define the trace for general Sobolev functions. Apply the H² value result to the first derivatives of an H³ function for its derivative traces. Smooth chart changes and multiplication by the smooth normal preserve these local trace spaces. The H(div) normal trace in 2.5 is the bounded dual construction using H¹ lifts of H^{1/2} boundary functions.

The bounded H¹ lifting used in 2.5 is explicit on the half-space: for psi in H^{1/2}(R²), set

  Phi_hat(xi',y_3)=exp(-y_3 sqrt(1+|xi'|²)) psi_hat(xi'), y_3>0.

Direct integration in y_3 gives ||Phi||_{H¹}²<=C integral sqrt(1+|xi'|²)|psi_hat(xi')|² dxi'. Its boundary trace is psi, by the just-proved trace estimate and approximation. Fixed cutoffs and flattening provide local lifts on the half-patches; a partition of unity supplies the usual full-boundary lift when needed in the normal-trace definition. Thus the dual normal-trace construction has the asserted bound.

After (2.31), both sides of (2.14) belong to H^{3/2}; the identity is the same identity previously derived in H^{-1/2}. Thus the conormal compatibility was present in the weak formulation, and stronger unknown traces were conclusions, not premises.

For Maxwell fields, (2.1) gives ||F||_2<=|k|² Lambda ||E||_2. Substitution into (1.2) proves (1.4), including for complex k.

2.13 One-sided Sobolev embedding and representative gluing

Here is a local extension/embedding argument that also handles an observation ball tangent to the interface. Work on a flat half-patch slightly larger than the desired closed-side compact set. Multiply by a cutoff supported away from the artificial lateral and top boundaries. A function h in H² of the positive half-space has a bounded H² extension across y_3=0 given, on the negative side, by

  Ext h(y',y_3)=3h(y',-y_3)-2h(y',-2y_3), y_3<0.              (2.32)

For smooth h, the value and first normal derivative match at zero because 3-2=1 and -3+4=1. Therefore weak derivatives through order two have no interface delta terms. Change of variables bounds the L² norms of all the piecewise derivatives. Approximation by smooth functions up to the half-space boundary proves the bounded extension for general H² h. Such approximation follows by translating the function a small distance into the half-space and then mollifying at a smaller scale; the derivatives converge in L² by continuity of translations. The cutoff handles the artificial edges. Pulling this extension back through the fixed chart gives the local extension needed on a curved material side.

For f in H²(R³), Cauchy-Schwarz in Fourier space gives

  ||fhat||_1 <= (integral_R³ (1+|xi|²)^(-2) dxi)^(1/2)||f||_{H²}<infinity.

Thus the inverse Fourier integral is a continuous representative and controls its supremum. For 0<alpha<1/2,

  |exp(i xi dot x)-exp(i xi dot y)| <= C_alpha |xi|^alpha |x-y|^alpha,

and integral |xi|^{2alpha}(1+|xi|²)^(-2) dxi is finite. At infinity its radial integrand is of order r^{2alpha-2}, which is integrable precisely for this chosen alpha-range. Consequently

  ||f||_{C^{0,alpha}} <= C_alpha ||f||_{H²}.                    (2.33)

This proves the needed one-sided embedding; alpha=1/4 is one fixed admissible choice. Endpoint Holder exponents are unnecessary.

The continuous representatives are unique: continuous functions equal a.e. on an open material side agree there, because a nonzero difference at a point persists on an open set of positive volume. Their boundary values agree by continuity from that side. The locally constructed representatives therefore glue on overlapping patches, including at their one-sided boundary points.

2.14 Magnetic regularity and physical transmission conditions

The direct cutoff div-curl estimate applied to H, using div H=0 and curl H=-ik epsilon E, gives

  ||H||_{H¹(U_1)} <= C(||H||_{L²(U)}+|k| Lambda ||E||_{L²(U)}).  (2.34)

This is global H¹ across the interface. To obtain higher one-sided regularity, use curl curl H=grad div H-Delta H and the second Maxwell equation:

  Delta H=ik curl(epsilon E).

For each component a define the vector g^a by

  (g^a)_b=ik sum_c LeviCivita(a,b,c) epsilon E_c.

Then Delta H_a=div g^a, equivalently

  integral grad H_a dot grad conjugate(phi)
    =integral g^a dot grad conjugate(phi).

On a fixed intermediate patch, E is piecewise H², so g^a is piecewise H² with norm <=C|k|Lambda||E||_{H²_pw}. Apply the already proved scalar transmission lemma with principal coefficient 1, transformed by the same charts, to H_a in H¹. It gives piecewise H³ for H on a smaller patch, with

  ||H||_{H³_pw(V)}
    <= C(||H||_{H¹(U_1)}+|k|Lambda||E||_{H²_pw(U_1)}).

Use (1.4) and (2.34), with suitable nested patches, to obtain (1.5). We do not claim global H² for H; its normal derivatives can jump.

The distributional curls of piecewise H² fields have surface terms proportional to nu x [E] and nu x [H]. This is obtained by the ordinary side integration-by-parts formula, extended to Sobolev fields by density. The actual Maxwell right sides are L² and have no surface measure contribution, so the tangential jumps vanish as trace distributions. Similarly the zero global divergences give [epsilon E] dot nu=0 and [H] dot nu=0. This proves (1.6). The trace functions are continuous on the smooth interface after 2.13. A continuous trace function that is zero as a distribution is pointwise zero, since a nonzero value would persist on a surface-open set of positive area. Thus the one-sided continuous traces satisfy the physical conditions everywhere in the patch.

2.15 The modal frequency-error forcing and its exact topology

On a fixed open neighborhood where all late coefficients equal epsilon, put

  e_n=E_n-E_*,
  f_n=k_n² epsilon e_n+(k_n²-k_*²)epsilon E_*.

The actual equations imply

  curl curl e_n=f_n,          div(epsilon e_n)=0.                (2.35)

If only E_n -> E_* in local L² is initially given, the limit's identities follow by testing against compact smooth functions: multiplication by the bounded fixed epsilon is L² continuous, k_n² -> k_*², and derivatives are transferred to the tests. Hence no separate identification of the limiting field with an abstract regular solution is needed.

Fix an enlarged patch U' and choose K with |k_n|<=K eventually. Then

  ||f_n||_{L²(U')}
    <= Lambda K² ||e_n||_{L²(U')}
       +Lambda |k_n²-k_*²| ||E_*||_{L²(U')} ->0.                 (2.36)

This is ordinary L² convergence on the full interface-crossing patch. It uses no derivative of epsilon E_*. Generally epsilon E_* is not global H¹, because its tangential component can jump. Requiring global H¹ convergence of this forcing would be an unjustified extra hypothesis.

Apply theorem 05.1 to (2.35). It gives (1.7) directly. On closed material sides, (2.33) gives uniform and C^{0,alpha} convergence. With fixed real frequency the forcing reduces to f_n=k²epsilon e_n and exactly the same estimate applies.

To display the corresponding first-order equations, let h_n=H_n-H_* and delta_n=k_n-k_*. Then

  curl e_n=ik_n h_n+F_n,       F_n=i delta_n H_*,
  curl h_n=-ik_n epsilon e_n+G_n, G_n=-i delta_n epsilon E_*.

Since H_* is global H¹ locally by (2.34), F_n ->0 in that H¹ topology. G_n ->0 in L² and, after the electric regularity theorem, in piecewise H² on smaller patches; it need not be global H¹. Both have zero distributional divergence. The second-order proof uses exactly the weaker, sufficient L² quantity (2.36).

2.16 Magnetic convergence from electric convergence

Equations (2.2)–(2.8), applied to e_n and f_n, give

  curl e_n ->0 in H¹ on smaller fixed patches.

Since k_n ->k_*!=0, choose a tail with |k_n|>=|k_*|/2. On that tail,

  H_n-H_*=(curl e_n)/(ik_n)
     +(1/(ik_n)-1/(ik_*))curl E_* ->0 in H¹_loc.                (2.37)

This proves the magnetic convergence needed when an upstream theorem supplies only electric local L² convergence. For higher one-sided convergence, the component equations for h_n have divergence data obtained from

  i(k_n epsilon E_n-k_*epsilon E_*)
    =i k_n epsilon e_n+i(k_n-k_*)epsilon E_*.

These data tend to zero in piecewise H² on a fixed intermediate patch by (1.7). The scalar transmission estimate applied componentwise, together with (2.37), gives h_n ->0 piecewise H³ on a smaller patch, and therefore uniformly one-sided. This step uses the same fixed material charts.

2.17 Fixed collars: a finite atlas independent of the late rounding index

The local convergence theorem requires equality of material on an OPEN neighborhood of the collar, not only on the collar as a closed set. In the binary construction choose radii

  0<r_i<=b_i/2,              0<b_i<a_i.

The collars C_i and enlarged annuli A_i of (1.9) then satisfy C_i compactly contained in A_i and 3r_i/2<=3b_i/4<a_i. Apply the accepted CommonBinaryCollar.eventually_annuli_agree to the CLOSED annuli with lower radii r_i/2 and upper radii 3r_i/2. It gives one common tail on which every rounded susceptibility equals the same limiting susceptibility there.

The accepted CommonBinaryInterface.eventually_body_smooth gives another common tail of smooth configurations. For a common contrast d_mat>0, coefficient_body identifies the rounded susceptibility exactly as d_mat on body_n and 0 outside. Pick one n_0 on the intersection of those two tails. On each open A_i, every later susceptibility equals that of n_0. Because d_mat!=0, their material body sets agree there as well. Consequently all those interfaces are exactly the fixed smooth interface of n_0 within A_i.

For every point of C_i, choose a sufficiently small enlarged physical ball inside A_i that either lies in one material or crosses one regular smooth interface chart of n_0. Compactness of C_i yields a finite such cover. Fix it once, together with all smaller balls and cutoffs needed in 2.2–2.13. Its chart bounds and separations are independent of n>=n_0. Apply (1.7) on those fixed charts. Taking the maximum of their finitely many constants gives one-sided uniform convergence on all C_i.

This also obtains the fixed smooth geometry of the limiting coefficient locally; a separate unquoted theorem about smoothness of the sharp conical surface is unnecessary. If the precise Lean names of the limiting coefficients differ between source modules, pass the late actual weak equations to their L² limit on A_i, as in 2.15, to get the local limiting equation directly.

The constants needed to establish continuity on the ENTIRE observation ball for a single n can depend on that smooth rounded geometry. Only the collar estimates are uniform in n. Neither the argument nor the conclusion requires an estimate uniform on a neighborhood shrinking into the vertex.

2.18 Compact attainment with the exact one-sided convention

Let K be the closed observation ball. For each material region define

  K_a=K intersect closure(Omega_a).

It is compact, and E^a is continuous on K_a because it is the one-sided extension from an ambient open neighborhood of K. If K_a is nonempty, |E^a| attains a finite maximum m_a on K_a. There is at least one nonempty K_a, and there are finitely many regions. Therefore max_a m_a is finite and attained and equals max_K E_up. This proves theorem 05.4.

The use of K intersect closure(Omega_a) is deliberate. Replacing it by closure(K intersect Omega_a) can remove a physically incident one-sided value when Omega_a lies outside K and touches its boundary tangentially.

For each real t,

  {x in K:E_up(x)>=t}
    = union_a {x in K_a:|E^a(x)|>=t}.

Each set on the right is compact, so the finite union is closed in K. Thus E_up is upper semicontinuous. In general it is not continuous, because the magnitudes of the normal electric traces can differ.

On a region where the phase geometry is shared, the elementary finite-maximum inequality and the reverse triangle inequality give

  |E_{n,up}(x)-E_{*,up}(x)|
    <= max_{a active at x}|E_n^a(x)-E_*^a(x)|.                 (2.38)

Consequently one-sided uniform convergence gives uniform convergence of the upper envelopes, even though the envelopes may have jumps.

2.19 Positive-volume peaks give strict maxima of the continuous envelope

On each compact smooth patch an interface is a graph. A graph in R³ has three-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero by Fubini (each vertical section is a singleton); rotations and smooth Lipschitz chart changes preserve nullity. A finite chart cover gives nullity of the interfaces in K.

The actual weak field and its continuous representative on each open material side agree almost everywhere by construction. Removing these finitely many representative exceptional sets and the interfaces from an actual positive-volume coordinate superlevel leaves a nonempty, indeed positive-volume, set. At any remaining point,

  E_{n,up}(x)=|E_n^a(x)|>=|(E_n)_j(x)|.

Thus a positive-volume coordinate superlevel above T inside Q_i implies

  max_{closure(Q_i)}E_{n,up}>T.                                (2.39)

The high-field point can be chosen in an ordinary material interior; the eventual maximizing point need not be there.

Here is also the exact L² peak-preservation argument for a modal input when the sharp limiting field is known to be essentially unbounded in each tip ball. In a finite-volume ball D, for any T choose a>0 with

  A={x in D:|E_*(x)|>T+2a}

of positive volume. Such an A exists if |E_*| is not essentially bounded there. Chebyshev gives

  vol{x in D:|E_n(x)-E_*(x)|>=a}
    <= a^{-2}||E_n-E_*||_{L²(D)}² ->0.

For all sufficiently late n that volume is less than vol(A). The remaining subset of A has positive volume and |E_n|>T+a>T. A finite intersection of these tails gives simultaneous peaks at all finitely many tips. This needs actual essential unboundedness (or a proved positive-volume singularity theorem), not arbitrary pointwise values assigned to a weak field on a null set.

2.20 One common index, parameter order, and strict margins

All illumination choices precede the following construction. For the driven case retain the one fixed input d_in supplied by the accepted simultaneous theorem, with its exact incoming-power normalization. For the modal case retain the already normalized sequence with the compatible scale/phase from its upstream theorem. No field is rescaled after selecting the peak threshold.

First fix the finite geometry and frequency/input, then the observation radii r_i and the fixed collars, then n_0 and the fixed collar atlas of 2.17. By regularity the limiting envelope is bounded on each C_i. Define the finite numbers

  L_i=sup_{C_i}E_{*,up},       B_i=L_i+1.

Uniform convergence of the side representatives and (2.38) provide a common tail with

  sup_{C_i}E_{n,up}<B_i for every i.                           (2.40)

For the prescribed target vector M_i choose

  T_i=max{M_i,2B_i}+1.                                         (2.41)

The accepted simultaneous positive-volume theorem, with inner radii r_i/2 and these thresholds, provides another common tail on which (1.10) holds. The coordinate choices j_i precede the thresholds, as required by that source theorem.

Intersect the four tails for:

  (i) smoothness on the rounded observation neighborhoods;
  (ii) exact material agreement on all enlarged annuli;
  (iii) the simultaneous collar bounds (2.40);
  (iv) the simultaneous positive-volume superlevels at (2.41).

Each is an eventual statement in the natural index. Their finite intersection is eventual, so there exists one N such that EVERY n>=N satisfies all four for EVERY i. This is stronger than separately selecting different rounding indices for different tips.

For each such n, compact attainment and (2.39) give

  m_{n,i}>T_i>M_i,
  sup_{C_i}E_{n,up}<B_i<m_{n,i}/2.                             (2.42)

The +1 choices make every inequality strict. Nothing depends on finding an ordinary point of the limiting field first or on pointwise convergence near the shrinking tip.

Because the part of closure(Q_i) with radius >=3r_i/4 lies in C_i, no maximizing point can lie there. Therefore every maximizer has |x-c_i|<3r_i/4. This is a spatial separation from the observation-ball boundary, not a separation from the dielectric interface.

If diameter <ell is requested, choose 2r_i<ell at the radius-selection step. Pairwise disjointness follows from the already disjoint available neighborhoods and choosing the radii inside them. Restrictions on the planar projection and the existence of the initial geometry belong to their separate construction theorem; they are not inferred from local regularity.

The logical order of the driven conclusion is

  fixed finite geometry -> delta>0 -> chosen k in (0,delta)
  -> one normalized input d_in -> observation radii and collars
  -> fixed collar bounds -> arbitrary target vector M
  -> one N -> every n>=N works for all tips.

In particular, d_in does not depend on M or n. The source theorem even permits the radii to be selected after d_in, and all its component choices precede the thresholds.

2.21 Exact superlevel-component convention with jumps

Use the ambient upper-envelope convention: physical interface points are single points, their amplitude is the maximum of the incident side values, and

  S={x:E_{n,up}(x)>m_{n,i}/2}.

Take S in an ambient open neighborhood containing closure(Q_i) union C_i where the one-sided envelope is defined, or use the global field when available. Take the connected component D of S containing a selected maximizing point. A connected component is taken in the subspace topology of S. S need not be open, and D need not be an open subset of R³ or lie in one material phase.

By (2.42), S is disjoint from C_i. The continuous radial map x -> |x-c_i| maps the connected set D to a connected subset of R. Its image contains a value <3r_i/4. If it contained a value >=3r_i/4, connectedness of subsets of R would force a value in the forbidden collar. Hence D is contained in B(c_i,3r_i/4).

For the closure assertion, {E_{n,up}>=m_{n,i}/2} is closed locally by upper semicontinuity and contains S. Therefore it contains closure(D), which cannot meet C_i. The closure is bounded, and it is closed in the ambient neighborhood of the compact observation ball, hence compact. It is contained in the open ball B(c_i,3r_i/4); equivalently it has a positive distance from the collar.

One could instead retain distinct copies of interface points in the disjoint union of closed material sides and take components there. Their projections are also localized, but that is a different convention. This report uses the ambient upper-envelope convention required by the packet and makes no assertion that a strict superlevel component is open.

This completes the new mathematical local analysis, compact attainment, and common-index localization argument.

3.5 Generic analytic toolkit and external references

The new transmission estimate and its application above are proved directly, rather than invoked from an external interface-regularity theorem. The generic ingredients are:

  (a) L² completeness, weak compactness, and weak lower semicontinuity;
  (b) smooth cutoff existence, local mollification, L² continuity of translations, and smooth density in the relevant local Sobolev spaces;
  (c) the Fourier-Plancherel theorem and distributional differentiation;
  (d) the mean-zero Poincare inequality on a ball;
  (e) smooth graph charts from the finite-dimensional implicit-function theorem;
  (f) elementary Sobolev product and change-of-variables rules;
  (g) compact extreme-value, finite-cover, and connected-image theorems.

Their specific uses and hypotheses were spelled out in section 2. The quotient criterion, curl-div estimate, weak potential, trace bounds, extension, and embedding needed here were derived there. Their future Lean implementations remain obligations even though the mathematics is standard.

The simple-pole residue A(0) is nonzero nilpotent, not a projection. Thus
neither ell(u)!=0 nor projection idempotence belongs in the theorem.

2.6 Exact transport to the actual compact reduced pencil

On the sector, A_S(z)=B_S F_S(z), with B_S constant and invertible.
The right kernels are identical. The bijection of left kernels is

  ell_A -> ell_F=ell_A B_S,       ell_F -> ell_A=ell_F B_S^(-1).

The derivative crossing is unchanged:

  ell_F(F_S'(k)u)=ell_A(A_S'(k)u)=d_src(u).

On the punctured disk,

  F_S(z)^(-1)=A_S(z)^(-1)B_S.

Consequently

  Res F_S^(-1) = u tensor (ell_src B_S) / d_src(u),
  Res A_S^(-1) = (Res F_S^(-1)) B_S^(-1).                  (2.13)

The static factor acts on the RIGHT of the residue. In particular the
compact-reduced residue is not in general u tensor ell_src/d_src.


2.7 Construct and estimate the canonical physical operator

For any bounded ball B, extend f in X_B by zero. The distributional Newton
Hessian has whole-space Fourier multiplier -xi xi^T/|xi|^2. This includes the
distributional local term; a bare classical Hessian away from x=0 would miss it.
Therefore the compressed static operator is -P_B, and

  K_B(z)=-P_B+R_B(z),
  R_ij(z,x)=z^2 Phi_z(x) delta_ij
                 +partial_i partial_j(Phi_z-Phi_0)(x).      (2.14)

Here R_ij is an ordinary locally integrable function, with no additional
point mass. Indeed

  Phi_z(r)-Phi_0(r)=sum_{m>=1} (iz)^m r^(m-1)/(4 pi m!).

The m=1 term is constant and has zero Hessian. The remaining leading term
is a multiple of r, whose first derivative is bounded and whose boundary
flux at radius epsilon is O(epsilon^2); hence its distributional Hessian
has no point mass.

Here are explicit useful bounds. Suppose |z|<=M and 0<r<=D. For p>=1,

  |partial_i partial_j r^p|
      <=p(1+|p-2|) r^(p-2).

For m>=2, p=m-1 gives p(1+|p-2|)<=m(m-1). Consequently

  |R_ij(z,x)| <= M^2 exp(MD)/(2 pi |x|)=C(M,D)/|x|.          (2.15)

If D is the diameter of B, then for each y in B,

  integral_B |x-y|^(-2) dx <=4 pi D.

Summing the nine entries yields

  ||R_B(z)||_HS <=3 C(M,D) sqrt(4 pi D |B|).                 (2.16)

Every kernel entry is entire in z for x!=0. The power series and the integrable
square majorant prove holomorphy in Hilbert--Schmidt norm, hence in operator
norm. Cauchy's estimate on a unit parameter circle supplies, for every q>=0,

  sup_{|z|<=M} ||R_B^(q)(z)||_HS
    <=q! 3 C(M+1,D) sqrt(4 pi D |B|).                       (2.17)

In particular R_B(0)=0. These constants depend only on the ball, the chosen
frequency compact set and derivative order, not material smoothness.

The same estimates work between two fixed bounded source/observation balls,
with a containing difference radius in place of D. The compressed Newton
operator is bounded because the whole-space Fourier projection has norm one.
Thus K_global(z)f is in L2_loc.

Put Q=Phi_z*f. Its distributional Helmholtz equation is
(-Delta-z^2)Q=f. On each bounded observation ball, the truncated gradient kernel
satisfies a bound C(r^(-2)+r^(-1)), which is in L1 in three dimensions. Young's
inequality gives grad Q in L2 locally. Hence

  E=K_global(z)f=z^2Q+grad div Q,
  curl E=z^2 curl Q in L2_loc.

For z!=0 put H=(iz)^(-1)curl E. Direct distributional algebra gives

  curl E=izH,              curl H=-iz(E+f),                 (2.18)
  (curl curl-z^2)E=z^2 f.

All differentiations are distributional or are performed on locally L1
kernels after the static splitting. No unproved classical interface trace
or integration by parts across a rough material surface is used.

For completeness, the outgoing branch can be justified without making a
lower-half-plane decay argument. Outside the compact support, each scalar
component is smooth and solves Helmholtz. For Im z>0, direct differentiation
of Phi_z(x-y), uniformly for y in the source ball, bounds E and its radial
derivative by a polynomial in 1/|x| times exp(-Im z |x|). The source is L1
by finite support and Cauchy-Schwarz. Every scalar spherical harmonic radial
coefficient therefore excludes the exponentially growing incoming solution
and is proportional to h_l^(1)(z r).

For a scalar harmonic coefficient e_l(r,z), put y_l(r,z)=r e_l(r,z).
For fixed exterior radii, y_l and its radial derivative are holomorphic in z
by differentiation under an integral over a separated compact source region.
The determinant of the pair (y_l,partial_r y_l) with
(xi_l(zr),partial_r xi_l(zr)) is holomorphic on C minus {0};
it vanishes for Im z>0 and therefore
vanishes throughout this connected domain. The outgoing solution and its
derivative do not vanish simultaneously, by their nonzero Wronskian.
Thus all coefficients remain outgoing for EVERY z!=0. This is analytic
continuation of a finite-radius identity, not a decay assertion below the
real axis.

2.8 Prove the actual-source-to-physical operator bridge

The original Packet 04 file was additionally retrieved and inspected, because

Fix the sphere material and let sigma=sqrt(n^2-1)>0. For arbitrary

The supplemented source dictionary gives source(c_n,u)=J_{B_1}f exactly a.e.
This use of division is legitimate for this constant positive sphere contrast;

For every observation radius T>1, the inspected

OutgoingModeGluing.actual_exterior_moment also applies to arbitrary u, for

There is no kernel hypothesis, no reality hypothesis on z, and no sign

with xi_l. We first work on one fixed observation ball B_T; no general gluing

  curl F=izG,       curl G=-izF.

Thus div F=0 and (Delta+z^2)F=0 on B_T. The actual electric response is an L2

Every Cartesian scalar spherical harmonic coefficient of this smooth F,

  psi_l(zr) d_r xi_l(zr) - xi_l(zr) d_r psi_l(zr) = iz!=0

On the unit ball, for z!=0, we have consequently proved

  r_1 A(z) J_1 = I-s K_{B_1}(z)s = W_{B_1}(z).             (2.19)

Both sides are norm-holomorphic at zero, so continuity gives the same identity

Because every weighted correction has an outer multiplier s, and every

  A(z) = J_1 W_{B_1}(z) r_1 direct-sum I_exterior.           (2.20)

More precisely use the orthogonal decomposition into material-ball-supported

2.9 Weighted/unweighted equivalence, left kernels, and the physical pairing

Work on a fixed containing ball and sector. For the present paragraph allow
any real bounded 0<=chi<=C supported there, and put s=sqrt(chi). The subsequent
algebra does not divide by s and remains valid even when chi vanishes.

Write W=I-sKs and L=I-Ks^2. Direct multiplication gives

  W s=s L,                L K s=K s W.                     (2.21)

The right kernel maps at a fixed frequency are

  E in ker L -> u=sE in ker W,
  u in ker W -> E=Ksu in ker L.                            (2.22)

They are inverse: if LE=0 then Ks(sE)=Kchi E=E; if Wu=0 then sKsu=u.
Therefore neither map kills a nonzero kernel vector.

For left functionals, the exact inverse maps are

  Phi W=0 -> lambda=Phi s,        lambda L=0 -> Phi=lambda K s.
                                                               (2.23)

Indeed Phi=Phi sKs and lambda=lambda K s^2 prove the two inverse identities.
This also proves nonvanishing of transported left functionals.

With nondispersive chi, differentiating (2.21) gives

  W'(k)s=s L'(k).

For u=sE and lambda=Phi s,

  Phi(W'(k)u)=lambda(L'(k)E).                               (2.24)

For punctured invertible values, direct multiplication proves both inverse
identities

  L^(-1)=I+K s W^(-1)s,
  W^(-1)=I+s L^(-1)K s.                                    (2.25)

These identities prove invertibility equivalence and transport the analytic
regular parts as well as the poles. Taking residues gives

  Res L^(-1)=K(k)s (Res W^(-1))s,
  Res W^(-1)=s (Res L^(-1))K(k)s.                           (2.26)

All products involving K are evaluated at k in the residue term; derivatives
of these analytic factors contribute only to the regular part.

Now introduce the physical complex-bilinear pairing

  beta_B(f,g)=integral_B f dot g.

Its bound is |beta_B(f,g)|<=||f||_2 ||g||_2. The static compressed projection is
real and Hermitian selfadjoint, and hence symmetric for beta_B. The dynamic
kernel satisfies R_ij(z,x-y)=R_ji(z,y-x). For f,g in L2(B), the double integral
is absolutely integrable: the Hilbert--Schmidt kernel belongs to L2(BxB),
and (x,y)->g(x)f(y) has L2 norm ||g|| ||f||. Fubini therefore proves

  beta_B(K(z)f,g)=beta_B(f,K(z)g).                          (2.27)

The same argument or norm differentiation proves reciprocity for K'(z).
Real scalar multiplication by s or chi is symmetric too. These are physical
proofs independent of the imported beta_src definition.

Let E!=0 satisfy L(k)E=0, and put q=chi E. If q=0, then E=K(k)q=0, a
contradiction. Since the fixed sector is invariant under conjugation and
real chi, conjugate(q) belongs to it. Thus

  lambda_E(conjugate(q))=integral_B |q|^2>0.

So lambda_E(v)=beta_B(q,v) is nonzero ON THE SECTOR. Reciprocity gives

  lambda_E(L(k)v)
      =beta_B(q,v)-beta_B(K(k)q,chi v)
      =beta_B(q,v)-beta_B(E,chi v)=0.                       (2.28)

For the sphere, Sections 2.3 and 2.8 give ran W(k)=ker Phi, and (2.23)
gives a source-transported nonzero lambda_0=Phi s with crossing d_src!=0.
Its left kernel is one-dimensional by the same bijection. Closed range and
the cokernel statement also follow directly: if lambda_0(y)=0, choose v
with W(k)v=sy. Equation (2.21) then gives

  L(k)[y+K(k)sv]=y-K(k)s^2y+K(k)sW(k)v=y.

Thus ran L(k)=ker lambda_0, a closed codimension-one subspace. Comparing
the two nonzero left functionals gives c!=0 with

  lambda_E=c lambda_0,        d_phys=c d_src!=0.             (2.29)

This is the promised way to establish every invariant physical conclusion
without silently assuming the missing numerical (PAIR) adapter.

The derivative is exactly L'(k)=-K'(k)chi, so

  d_phys=lambda_E(L'(k)E)=-beta_B(q,K'(k)q).                (2.30)

The integral is finite and
|d_phys|<=||K'(k)|| ||q||^2<=C^2 ||K'(k)|| ||E||^2.
It is a complex bilinear quantity and can have any complex phase.

The corresponding PHYSICAL weighted left functional is
ell_w,phys(v)=beta_B(u,v), u=sE. Equations (2.23), (2.27) give

  lambda_E=ell_w,phys s,       ell_w,phys=lambda_E K(k)s,
  ell_w,phys(W'(k)u)=d_phys.                                 (2.31)

Indeed W'=-sK's makes the last identity immediate. Thus the exact physical
weighted/unweighted derivative equality is proved, even though identifying
ell_w,phys with the pre-existing source pairFunctional still needs (PAIR).

Substitute (2.29) into the transported source residue. The scalar c cancels:

  E tensor lambda_0/d_src
    = E tensor lambda_E/d_phys.

This proves (1.8) and its rank-one assertion for the physical unweighted pencil.

If chi depended on spectral frequency, L' would instead contain
-K'chi-K chi', and d would have an extra term. The displayed normalization
theorem is therefore explicitly nondispersive.

2.10 Unweighted static inverse and the second compact reduction

Write

  B_e=I+P_B chi,       B_w=I+sP_Bs,
  F_e(z)=I-B_e^(-1)R_B(z)chi,
  F_w,phys(z)=I-B_w^(-1)sR_B(z)s.

B_w is positive selfadjoint and >=I, so ||B_w^(-1)||<=1.
A direct two-sided multiplication using B_w s=s B_e gives

  B_e^(-1)=I-P_B s B_w^(-1)s,       ||B_e^(-1)||<=1+C.      (2.32)

The resulting exact factorizations are

  W=B_w F_w,phys,                L=B_e F_e.                (2.33)

The same statements hold on the fixed closed sector because P_B, R_B and
the material multiplication preserve it. In particular their restrictions
and restricted inverses are legitimate operators on that sector.
The compact terms are Hilbert--Schmidt followed/preceeded by bounded maps.
Both reduced pencils are identity-minus-compact, entire, and equal I at zero.

For any left functional ell_w of W and lambda_E of L, the reduced left
functionals and residue transforms are

  ell_Fw=ell_w B_w,          Res F_w,phys^(-1)=(Res W^(-1))B_w,
  ell_Fe=lambda_E B_e,       Res F_e^(-1)=(Res L^(-1))B_e.   (2.34)

Right kernels are unchanged by these left invertible static factors.
Derivative pairings are also unchanged:

  ell_Fw(F_w,phys'(k)u)=ell_w(W'(k)u),
  ell_Fe(F_e'(k)E)=lambda_E(L'(k)E)=d_phys.                  (2.35)

In particular the exact physical residue table is:

  Operator              Right vector   Left functional       Residue
  W                     u=sE           beta_B(u, .)           u tensor ell_w/d_phys
  L                     E              beta_B(chi E, .)       E tensor lambda_E/d_phys
  F_w,phys              u              ell_w B_w              u tensor (ell_w B_w)/d_phys
  F_e                   E              lambda_E B_e           E tensor (lambda_E B_e)/d_phys

The source table for the actual A/F_w uses ell_src,d_src in place of the
physical pair. Sections 2.8-2.9 give their exact transport, including the
possible scalar c until (PAIR) is inspected.

If a left factor B(z) is allowed to depend analytically on z, the derivative
identity on a kernel vector still holds: differentiating A=BF gives
A'u=B F'u+B'Fu=B F'u at the root. Here the static factors are in fact
frequency independent.

2.11 Derive the genuine Green residue, its sign and its local domain

Let M_chi(z)=curl curl-z^2(1+chi). Equation (2.18) implies that the vacuum
electric Green operator for the source convention M_0(z)E=f is

  G0(z)=z^(-2)K_global(z),                                  (2.36)

near every nonzero k. It is analytic between fixed compact-source and bounded
observation L2 spaces there. Its pole at z=0 is outside the chosen disk.

For f supported in B, the material field restricted to B is

  E_f(z)=L_chi(z)^(-1)G0,B(z)f.

The global field is G0(z)f+K_global(z)chi E_f(z).
Substitution into M_chi shows its right-hand side is precisely f:
the scattered term contributes z^2chi E_f to the vacuum equation,
which cancels the material part. Off the characteristic values its outgoing
solution is unique in the selected sector by vacuum uniqueness plus
invertibility of L_chi.

Because G0 is analytic near k, its multiplication contributes only its value
at k to the singular part. Reciprocity and K(k)chi E=E give

  lambda_E(G0,B(k)f)
    =k^(-2) beta_B(chi E,K_B(k)f)
    =k^(-2) beta_B(E,f).                                    (2.37)

Combining with (1.8),

  (Res G_B)f = E beta_B(E,f)/(k^2 d_phys).                  (2.38)

For compact sources outside the original material ball, take a larger
containing ball. Alternatively cross-domain reciprocity, justified by the
same local operator bounds, gives directly

  beta_B(chi E, r_B G0(k)f)
    =k^(-2) integral E_global(y) dot f(y) dy.

The outgoing extension of the right residue vector is
K_global(k)chi E=E_global. Formula (2.38) therefore holds between every fixed
bounded source ball and bounded observation ball, with their sector
restrictions. It has precisely the tensor kernel in (1.9).

If a larger containing ball is used, q=chi E has the same compact support and
K'(k)q agrees on that support. Hence d_phys and N(E) are independent of the
containing ball. This is also why growing outgoing resonant fields in the
lower half-plane cause no divergence in the normalization integral.

Finally k^2 d_phys=-2k N(E), so the sign is

  Res G = -E_global tensor E_global/(2k N(E)).              (2.39)

There is no conjugation in either tensor factor. If one changes the differential
operator to its negative, or chooses a physical-current right-hand side
containing an additional frequency factor, the corresponding Green residue
changes accordingly. Equations (2.36)-(2.39) use exactly the manuscript's
displayed operator and its direct right-hand-side source f.

The full rotational sphere is degenerate. The restriction in this proof is
essential: no step infers that the full sphere Green residue has rank one.

2.12 Nonzero norm and the two different normalizations

Equations (2.29)-(2.30), k!=0 and (1.7) prove N(E)!=0.
For a complex constant alpha,

  q(alpha E)=alpha q(E),
  d_phys(alpha E)=alpha^2 d_phys(E),
  N(alpha E)=alpha^2 N(E).                                  (2.40)

Thus a nonzero alpha with alpha^2=1/N(E) gives physical unit normalization.
Such an alpha exists over C and has two possible signs.

For the prescribed source-unit U, d_src(U)=1 remains its original property.
Without (PAIR), (1.11) gives N(E)=-k c_U/2, so a physical unit scale satisfies
alpha^2=-2/(k c_U). After imposing it,

  d_phys(alpha E)=-2/k,       d_src(alpha U)=alpha^2.         (2.41)

With (PAIR), c_U=1 and this becomes alpha^2=-2/k. Even then source-unit
normalization and physical unit-N normalization are distinct operations.
The physically normalized vector is called alpha U, not silently relabeled
as the original U supplied by normalized_isolated_poles.

The tensor residue is invariant under every nonzero rescaling: its numerator
and denominator both acquire alpha^2. No choice of branch or sign changes
the Green operator.

2.13 Analytic material perturbation with fixed support and sector

Let chi_eta be as in Theorem 06-D and let ||chi_eta-chi_0||_infty=delta_eta.
The map eta -> M_chi_eta is norm-holomorphic because multiplication is a
bounded linear map from L-infinity to bounded operators. Therefore

  B_e(eta)=I+P_B chi_eta

is holomorphic. At eta=0, ||B_e(0)^(-1)||<=1+C. A sufficient condition for
its inverse to continue holomorphically is

  (1+C) delta_eta<1.                                        (2.42)

Indeed factor B_e(eta)=B_e(0)[I+B_e(0)^(-1)P_B(chi_eta-chi_0)]
and use the uniformly convergent Neumann series. Its inverse norm is at most

  (1+C)/(1-(1+C)delta_eta).

Consequently F(eta,z)=I-B_e(eta)^(-1)R_B(z)chi_eta is jointly holomorphic.
It preserves the fixed sector, and its kernel at (0,k0) has dimension one
with nonzero crossing, by the preceding actual sphere argument and transports.
For continuation from a later nearby material, the same property is inherited.

Keep the domain/codomain complements chosen at (0,k0). Their large block
remains invertible in a product neighborhood. The scalar Schur function
f(eta,z) is jointly holomorphic, f(0,k0)=0 and partial_z f(0,k0)!=0.

Choose a small closed disk about k0, excluding zero, on whose boundary f(0,.)
has no zeros and whose only zero inside is the simple one at k0. Uniform
closeness on the boundary gives exactly one zero, counting multiplicity, for
small eta by the scalar argument principle/Rouche theorem. The zero is
holomorphic in eta; explicitly it equals

  k_eta = (1/(2 pi i)) integral_boundary
             z partial_z f(eta,z)/f(eta,z) dz.               (2.43)

Holomorphy follows by a denominator bounded away from zero and differentiation
under the finite contour integral. The unique zero has multiplicity one.
Back substitution w_*(eta,k_eta) from Section 2.5 gives a nonzero holomorphic
kernel vector. Multiplying by a fixed constant chooses E0 at eta=0.
A fixed continuous complex-linear p with p(E0)!=0 remains nonzero nearby and
fixes the eigenfield scale uniquely. A varying Hermitian norm is not used
as an analytic normalization.

The product q_eta=chi_eta E_eta is holomorphic in L2, and K'(k_eta) is
holomorphic in operator norm. Continuity of the bilinear pairing gives
holomorphic d_phys and N. Shrink the disk so |N_eta-N_0|<|N_0|/2.
Choose alpha_0^2 N_0=1 and write

  h(eta)=N_eta/N_0-1,
  alpha_eta=alpha_0 exp[-(1/2) log(1+h(eta))].              (2.44)

The logarithm is its convergent power series for |h|<1. This proves a local
holomorphic nonzero normalization with alpha_eta^2 N_eta=1.

No claim is made of a global square root on a parameter domain with nontrivial
winding. No assumption that sqrt(chi_eta) itself is holomorphic was used.
A nonconstant holomorphic complex parameter family cannot be real-valued
throughout its disk; physical positivity is a condition on an intended real
parameter slice only.

For reference, if dot chi is the eta derivative at zero, differentiating
L(eta,k_eta)E_eta=0 and applying lambda_E gives the legitimate branch formula

  dot k = beta_B(E,(dot chi)E)/d_phys
        = -k beta_B(E,(dot chi)E)/(2N(E)).                  (2.45)

All integrals are finite because dot chi is bounded and E is L2.

2.14 Bounded a.e. rounding: prove the needed norm convergence

Suppose 0<=chi_j<=C, supports lie in one B, chi_j->chi a.e., and all materials
preserve the fixed sector. Let M_j be multiplication by chi_j. Dominated
convergence gives (M_j-M)f->0 in L2 for every fixed f. This is strong operator
convergence with uniform bound C. It is generally not operator-norm convergence;
a shrinking indicator set still has multiplier norm one.

The static operators B_j=I+P_B M_j have uniformly bounded inverses,
||B_j^(-1)||<=1+C. The resolvent identity yields, for every fixed f,

  (B_j^(-1)-B^(-1))f
    =B_j^(-1)P_B(M-M_j)B^(-1)f ->0.                        (2.46)

Thus their inverses converge strongly and are uniformly bounded.

For fixed z, the integral kernel of R_B(z)(M_j-M) is
R(z,x-y)(chi_j(y)-chi(y)). Its squared modulus tends to zero a.e. and is
dominated by 4C^2 times the square-integrable dynamic kernel. Therefore

  ||R_B(z)(M_j-M)||_HS ->0.                                (2.47)

This convergence is locally uniform in z: on a compact frequency set the
family R_B(z) is compact in Hilbert--Schmidt norm, and the maps
T -> T(M_j-M) have norm at most 2C. A finite net upgrades pointwise
convergence to uniform convergence. Alternatively (2.15) and pointwise uniform
kernel bounds on a compact frequency set give the same dominated argument.

A uniformly bounded strongly convergent sequence T_j->T satisfies
||(T_j-T)K||->0 for every fixed compact K. To prove it, approximate K in norm
by a finite-rank K_epsilon. Convergence is uniform on the finite-dimensional
range of K_epsilon, while the remainder is uniformly bounded. The same
finite-net argument is uniform over a norm-compact family of compact K.

Now decompose the reduced compact terms:

  B_j^(-1)R_B(z)M_j - B^(-1)R_B(z)M
    =B_j^(-1)R_B(z)(M_j-M)
       +(B_j^(-1)-B^(-1))R_B(z)M.                          (2.48)

The first term tends to zero in norm by (2.47) and the uniform static bound.
The second does so by compact composition, since R_B(z)M is compact.
The convergence is locally uniform in z by the preceding finite-net arguments.
Thus

  F_j(z)=I-B_j^(-1)R_B(z)M_j -> F(z) in operator norm
  locally uniformly in z.                                 (2.49)

Cauchy's formula gives locally uniform convergence of every z derivative:
on a circle of radius r contained in a slightly larger compact neighborhood,

  ||F_j^(m)(z)-F^(m)(z)||
       <=m! r^(-m) sup_circle ||F_j-F||.                   (2.50)

Restriction to the fixed sector preserves this convergence. The large Schur
block stays invertible on a sufficiently small closed disk, since its limiting
inverse is uniformly bounded there. Consequently the scalar Schur functions
f_j and their derivatives converge uniformly on that disk.

Let m_boundary=min_boundary |f|>0. For sufficiently large j,
sup_boundary |f_j-f|<m_boundary. Rouche gives exactly one zero k_j inside,
counting multiplicity. It is therefore simple. Repeating the argument on
every smaller disk proves k_j->k. The same back-substitution expression,
with a fixed p nonzero on E, yields E_j->E in L2(B).

For clarity, the scalar Rouche step uses only this standard elementary
statement: holomorphic f,g on a neighborhood of a closed disk, with
|g-f|<|f| on its boundary, have the same number of interior zeros with
multiplicity. The homotopy f+t(g-f) has no boundary zeros; its integer
argument-principle count is constant in t. The base f=(z-k)g0, g0!=0 on
the disk, has exactly one zero. All hypotheses are explicitly supplied above.

Finally

  ||chi_j E_j-chi E||_2
    <=C||E_j-E||_2 + ||(chi_j-chi)E||_2 ->0.                (2.51)

With q_j=chi_j E_j, continuity of k->K'(k) and the bound on q_j show

  -beta(q_j,K'(k_j)q_j) -> -beta(q,K'(k)q).                 (2.52)

For example split the difference into a term with q_j-q in each slot and
the term beta(q,[K'(k_j)-K'(k)]q); Cauchy-Schwarz and local operator bounds
make all three vanish. Multiplying by -k_j/2 proves N_j->N!=0.

Choose the same local logarithm square-root branch around the nonzero limiting
N. Then the physical unit-N scale and the scaled E_j converge. The global
extensions K_global(k_j)q_j converge in L2 on each fixed observation ball by
the local operator bounds. The rank-one formulas show operator-norm convergence
of the local Green residues and of the unweighted residue operators.

This proof needs no material-interface regularity, lower feature-size bound,
or norm convergence of the leading multiplication in L_chi. It also supplies
no rate without quantitative information beyond a.e. convergence.
