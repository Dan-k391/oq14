### 1.3 Theorem A: actual sphere magnetic covariance and radial vanishing

(A2) For EVERY z in C, EVERY theta in R, and EVERY x in D_rho,

  H_z[rho](R_theta x) = R_theta H_z[rho](x),

## 2. Complete numbered proof

### Step 1. Measurable representatives and the actual scalar integrals

Choose a Borel representative of rho, zero outside its support ball. This is permitted for a finite-dimensional Lebesgue-measurable L^2 function: replace each set in a countable sequence of measurable simple approximants by a Borel set equal to it modulo a null set, and take the almost-everywhere limit, set to zero on the remaining null set. Finite-dimensional coordinate measurability suffices.

This choice DOES NOT CHANGE (1) AT ANY OBSERVATION POINT. For a fixed x and a fixed pair of indices, the old and new integrands differ only where the source representatives differ. Integral congruence almost everywhere therefore gives exactly the same scalar integral, whether or not it is integrable. The same statement holds for every nonnegative majorant introduced below. Fixed-Q source symmetry is preserved, since orthogonal transformations preserve null sets.

All parameter-integral functions used below can consequently be taken Borel. For clarity, if f(x,y) is one of the scalar integrands, its absolute integral is a measurable nonnegative parameter integral by Tonelli. Its totalized complex integral is measurable as well: on the measurable set where the absolute integral is finite, it is the limit of integrals of f 1_{|f|<=N} over the finite source ball; those bounded parameter integrals are measurable. On the complement the totalized scalar integral is zero. This also justifies restriction of the actual field to an individual sphere; an arbitrary volume-a.e. representative alone would not justify such a restriction.

### Step 3. Kernel covariance and orientation

For any real orthogonal Q, |Qv|=|v|. The explicit radial formula therefore gives

  g_z(Qv)=Q g_z(v) for every v, including v=0.                          (11)

Real orthogonal Q is unitary for the Hermitian Euclidean norm, so |Qg|_2=|g|_2. The cross-product identity is

  (Qv) cross (Qw) = det(Q) Q(v cross w).                               (12)

One proof is to pair both sides with Qa using the bilinear dot product. The scalar triple product acquires det(Q), while the dot product is preserved by Q^T Q=I. Nondegeneracy gives (12) for real vectors, and complex bilinearity extends it to complex vectors. In particular det(R_theta)=1 and det(S)=-1. Reflection does not conjugate z or the source.

Expanding coordinates also gives

  |v cross w|_2^2 = |v|_2^2 |w|_2^2 - |sum_j v_j conjugate(w_j)|^2,

so |v cross w|_2 <= |v|_2 |w|_2. This supplies the norm inequality used below.

### Step 4. One absolute-convergence set for all complex frequencies

Define b(v)=|v|^{-2} for v!=0 and b(0)=0. Define an EXTENDED nonnegative integral

  W_rho(x) = integral b(x-y) |rho(y)|_2 dy in [0,infinity],

This majorant does not depend on z. Nonnegative integration is used here; infinity is never silently replaced by Lean's totalized real integral zero.

For |x|<=R and |y|<=a, x-y lies in B_{R+a}. Polar integration gives

  integral_{B_L} b(v) dv = 4 pi L.

Tonelli and translation invariance, with L=R+a, imply

  integral_{B_R} W_rho(x) dx <= 4 pi (R+a) ||rho||_1 < infinity.         (14)

Thus W_rho is finite almost everywhere in each integer-radius ball, and the countable union of these balls proves volume(D_rho^c)=0. Its Borel measurability follows from Step 1 and Tonelli.

For 0<|v|<=L,

  |g_z(v)|_2 <= exp(|z|L)/(4 pi) [|z|/|v|+1/|v|^2]

At zero both sides are zero. For a fixed x choose L=|x|+a>0. It follows that, whenever x is in D_rho, all nine scalar functions

### Step 5. Local L^2 bounds, with the limiting passages justified

Set J_z(x)=integral |g_z(x-y)|_2 |rho(y)|_2 dy as another extended nonnegative integral. Direct radial integration of the first bound in (15) gives

  integral_{B_L} |g_z(v)|_2 dv <= C_z(L).                              (16)

The needed L^1*L^2 estimate can be proved without invoking an unverified regularity theorem. If k>=0 is L^1 and f>=0 is L^2, let f_N=min(f,N). For each x, Cauchy-Schwarz with measure k(v)dv gives

  [integral k(v) f_N(x-v) dv]^2
    <= ||k||_1 integral k(v) f_N(x-v)^2 dv.

Integrate in x. Tonelli applies to the nonnegative right side and translation preserves the inner L^2 integral. Consequently

  ||k*f_N||_2^2 <= ||k||_1^2 ||f||_2^2.

Monotone convergence as N tends to infinity gives the same bound for k*f, including its almost-everywhere finiteness. This argument verifies, rather than assumes, the local convolution estimate.

Apply it with f=|rho|_2 and k=1_{B_{R+a}} |g_z|_2. On B_R the convolution is J_z, because rho vanishes outside B_a. Thus

  ||J_z||_{L^2(B_R)} <= C_z(R+a) ||rho||_2.

On D_rho, (2) and Step 3 give |H_z|_2<=|z|J_z. Its complement is null, so the same L^2 estimate holds for the actual field (1) and for H_z^sharp. This proves (5). Replacing k by 1_{B_{R+a}}b also gives ||W_rho||_{L^2(B_R)}<=4 pi(R+a)||rho||_2.

These estimates identify H_z^sharp with the SAME locally L^2 actual magnetic field. In particular, against any compactly supported smooth scalar test f, both integral H_i f and integral H_i partial_j f agree for the two representatives. ### Step 6. Invariance of D_rho without an uncountable intersection

Fix Q=R_theta or S. Equations (9)-(10) and Euclidean norm preservation give |rho(Qy)|_2=|rho(y)|_2 for almost every y. For EVERY fixed observation point x, change variables y=Qv in the extended nonnegative integral:

  W_rho(Qx)
    = integral b(Qx-y)|rho(y)|_2 dy
    = integral b(Q(x-v))|rho(Qv)|_2 dv
    = integral b(x-v)|rho(v)|_2 dv
    = W_rho(x).                                                        (17)

The substitution is legitimate even when the value is infinity: Q is a Borel bijection with |det Q|=1, and the integrand is nonnegative and measurable. The source null set occurs in v and can be discarded inside the integral for each x.

Thus (17) has the quantifiers FOR EVERY theta, FOR EVERY x. It is not merely a separate almost-everywhere identity in x. Hence D_rho is invariant under every rotation and S, and the SAME set works for all z. No uncountable intersection of conull observation sets is taken.

### Step 7. Covariance of the actual magnetic integral

Let x be in D_rho. By (17), Qx is also in D_rho, so every integral and finite linear combination below is absolutely integrable. Using (11), the source law, the measure-preserving change of variables, and finally (12),

  H_z[rho](Qx)
    = -i z integral g_z(Qx-y) cross rho(y) dy
    = -i z integral Qg_z(x-v) cross Qrho(v) dv
    = -i z det(Q) Q integral g_z(x-v) cross rho(v) dv
    = det(Q) Q H_z[rho](x).                                            (18)

Bounded complex-linear maps commute with these integrals because their integrands are absolutely integrable. Inserting det(R_theta)=1 and det(S)=-1 proves the actual rotation and axial-reflection laws in (A2).

The operator form is also valid. If rho_Q(y)=Qrho(Q^{-1}y), then W_{rho_Q}(Qx)=W_rho(x) and

  H_z[rho_Q](Qx)=det(Q)QH_z[rho](x) for x in D_rho.

By the source-action identity (8), this applies to the actual source of A_theta u. It supplies covariance of the magnetic source-to-field construction, not just covariance of the explicit `centerH` model.

Define H_z^sharp as in (A3). D_rho and its complement are Q-invariant. Equation (18) applies on D_rho; off D_rho both sides are zero. Therefore

  for all z, theta, x,
    H_z^sharp(R_theta x)=R_theta H_z^sharp(x),
    H_z^sharp(Sx)=-S H_z^sharp(x).                                      (19)

This is the simultaneous pointwise representative needed before choosing a rotation as a function of x.

  s_z(R_theta x)=s_z(x),      s_z(Sx)=-s_z(x).                          (20)

The point-dependent angle is used only AFTER (19), whose quantifiers are all theta and all x. This is the resolution of the rotation-dependent-null-set problem; evaluating (9) at a point-dependent angle would not have been legitimate.

### Step 11. Every positive-radius sphere meets D_rho in full area measure

Volume-a.e. zero by itself would give only an a.e.-radius result. The following additional estimate proves the stronger actual-convolution conclusion (4).

Fix r>0, and for y in R^3 define the nonnegative, possibly infinite quantity

  A_r(y)=integral_{S^2} b(r omega-y) d sigma(omega).

Let t=|y|. By rotational invariance of area, for t>0 and t!=r,

  A_r(t)=2 pi integral_{-1}^1 [r^2+t^2-2rt s]^(-1) ds
        = (2 pi/(rt)) log((r+t)/|r-t|).                               (24)

At t=0 the value is 4 pi/r^2. At t=r the integral is infinite, even though b(0)=0 at a single angular point. That exceptional sphere has three-dimensional measure zero. We treat A_r as a finite-a.e. L^2 representative when applying Cauchy-Schwarz; changing its values on that null sphere does not change an integral in y.

Polar integration in y gives

  ||A_r||_{L^2(B_a)}^2
    = (16 pi^3/r^2) integral_0^a [log((r+t)/|r-t|)]^2 dt.              (25)

Put L=r+a>0. For 0<=t<=a, t!=r,

  0 <= log((r+t)/|r-t|) <= log(L/|r-t|).

The interval for s=t-r lies in [-L,L]. Therefore

  integral_0^a [log((r+t)/|r-t|)]^2 dt
    <= 2 integral_0^L [log(L/s)]^2 ds
    = 2L integral_0^infinity v^2 exp(-v) dv
    = 4L.                                                             (26)

The substitution s=L exp(-v) proves the middle equality. Two integrations by parts give the last integral as 2; v^2 exp(-v) and v exp(-v) have zero endpoint limits at infinity, so these integrations by parts have no omitted boundary terms. Consequently

  ||A_r||_{L^2(B_a)} <= 8 pi^(3/2) sqrt(r+a)/r.                         (27)

Tonelli is applicable to b(r omega-y)|rho(y)|_2 because it is nonnegative and jointly measurable. Equations (13), (27), and Cauchy-Schwarz give

  integral_{S^2} W_rho(r omega) d sigma
    = integral_{B_a} |rho(y)|_2 A_r(y) dy
    <= 8 pi^(3/2) sqrt(r+a)/r ||rho||_2 < infinity.                     (28)

This holds for EACH fixed r>0, not just almost every r. It proves sigma({omega:r omega not in D_rho})=0 for every positive radius. The exceptional angular set in (28) depends on rho and r, but not on z or on a harmonic weight.

On this sphere, |g_z|<=B_z(r+a)b over the support differences, so (28) also gives

  integral_{S^2} J_z(r omega) d sigma
    <= 2 sqrt(pi) exp(|z|(r+a)) [1+|z|(r+a)]
       sqrt(r+a)/r ||rho||_2.

## 3. Exact additional vector-harmonic reconstruction needed for TE exclusion

This section separates a weak coefficient identity that can be derived directly from Maxwell from the full reconstruction theorem still needed for the project's TE terminology. It does not declare TE classification already proved.

### 3.1 Normalization and the required reconstruction statement

Let J=(a,b) with 0<=a<b<=infinity, and Omega_J={x:|x| in J}. On this open spherical shell, suppose E,H are actual L^2_loc fields and

  curl E = i z H,       curl H = -i z epsilon E                         (30)

as distributions against every compact smooth vector test. Assume z!=0, constant epsilon!=0, and permeability normalized to 1. Take scalar spherical harmonics Y_lm orthonormal for standard area sigma, satisfying -Delta_S Y_lm=lambda_l Y_lm, lambda_l=l(l+1). For l>=1 put

  R_lm=Y_lm omega,
  Psi_lm=grad_S Y_lm/sqrt(lambda_l),
  Phi_lm=omega cross Psi_lm.

Use R_00=Y_00 omega separately. If the project instead uses a constant multiple of sigma, renormalize the Y_lm for that measure; the coefficient formulas below then retain the same form.

For F=E,H, define

  d^F_lm(r)=integral_{S^2} [r omega dot F(r omega)] conjugate(Y_lm) d sigma,
  a^F_lm(r)=d^F_lm(r)/r,
  b^F_lm(r)=integral F(r omega) dot conjugate(Psi_lm) d sigma,
  c^F_lm(r)=integral F(r omega) dot conjugate(Phi_lm) d sigma.

These coefficients are defined for almost every radius using L^2 polar slices. The conjugations in these normalized harmonic coordinates are explicit; the packet's polynomial moment definition remains the unconjugated one in (4).

REQUIRED ADDITIONAL THEOREM, NOT SUPPLIED OR FORMALLY PROVED HERE:

(V1) {R_00} together with {R_lm,Psi_lm,Phi_lm : l>=1, |m|<=l} is a complete orthonormal basis of L^2(S^2;C^3).

(V2) For every pair satisfying (30), d^E_lm,d^H_lm belong to H^1_loc(J), the degree-zero coefficients are zero, and the pair reconstructs as

  E(r omega) = sum_{l>=1,m} [
      (d^E_lm/r) R_lm
      + ((r d^E_lm)'/(sqrt(lambda_l) r)) Psi_lm
      - (i z d^H_lm/sqrt(lambda_l)) Phi_lm ],                           (31)

  H(r omega) = sum_{l>=1,m} [
      (d^H_lm/r) R_lm
      + ((r d^H_lm)'/(sqrt(lambda_l) r)) Psi_lm
      + (i z epsilon d^E_lm/sqrt(lambda_l)) Phi_lm ].                   (32)

Derivatives are weak radial derivatives. The series converge in angular L^2 for almost every r and in L^2 on every compact subannulus. This statement includes the absence of an invisible tangential remainder: that is why completeness cannot be replaced by merely naming some scalar coefficients.

The radial, surface-gradient, and surface-curl vector harmonics and their completeness are classical; a primary reference is Barrera, Estevez and Giraldo (1985), Section 3 [E2]. The exact weak-field theorem (V1)-(V2), with the stated normalization, topology, and project-field identification, remains a Lean obligation. Theorem A does not depend on it.

### 3.2 Weak radial-curl identity proved here

The coefficient relation relevant to TE exclusion can be justified directly without assuming differentiable E or H. Fix l>=1 and a smooth radial eta compactly supported in J. The vector test

  V(x)=eta(r) conjugate(Y_lm(omega)) omega, r=|x|, omega=x/r,

is smooth with compact support away from r=0 and from the shell's endpoints. Direct differentiation of this smooth test gives

  curl V = -eta(r) sqrt(lambda_l)/r conjugate(Phi_lm(omega)).           (33)

The radial derivative of eta contributes zero because its gradient is parallel to omega. The remaining angular gradient gives (33), with the displayed minus sign from grad_S Y cross omega = -omega cross grad_S Y.

By the definition of distributional curl, integral E dot curl V = integral (i z H) dot V. All these integrals are absolutely convergent: the tests and their derivatives are bounded on a compact shell, and E,H are L^2 there. Polar Fubini is consequently valid. Substituting (33) yields

  -sqrt(lambda_l) integral_J eta(r) r c^E_lm(r) dr
    = i z integral_J eta(r) r^2 a^H_lm(r) dr.                          (34)

The coefficient functions are L^2 on every compact radial interval by Cauchy-Schwarz on the sphere and polar disintegration; on such intervals r is bounded above and below. Thus the difference of the two sides' densities is L^1_loc. The fundamental lemma for distributions represented by locally integrable functions gives

  c^E_lm(r) = -i z d^H_lm(r)/sqrt(lambda_l) for almost every r.         (35)

For completeness, that fundamental lemma does not assume hidden regularity: convolving a locally integrable density which annihilates every compact smooth test with a compact mollifier gives zero on each smaller interval; the usual local L^1 convergence of mollification then gives zero almost everywhere. It applies separately to real and imaginary parts.

The same test in the second Maxwell equation gives

  c^H_lm(r) = i z epsilon d^E_lm(r)/sqrt(lambda_l).                     (36)

The signs agree with curl(c Phi)'s radial coefficient -sqrt(lambda_l)c/r. Taking divergence in (30) gives div E=div H=0 under z!=0 and epsilon!=0; weak scalar-gradient tests justify div curl=0. The radial/poloidal divergence identity is

  (r^2 a^F_lm)'=sqrt(lambda_l) r b^F_lm.                              (37)

To check (37), use the compact scalar test eta(r) conjugate(Y_lm). Its gradient is eta'(r) conjugate(Y_lm) omega + eta(r) sqrt(lambda_l)/r conjugate(Psi_lm). Distributional divergence and absolutely convergent polar integration give integral r^2 a^F_lm eta' + sqrt(lambda_l) integral r b^F_lm eta = 0, which is precisely (37). The right side of (37) is L^2 on each compact radial interval, so r^2 a^F_lm, and therefore d^F_lm=r a^F_lm, belong to H^1_loc. This identifies the poloidal coefficients in (31)-(32). A complete formal proof must implement these compact-test steps and establish (V1) separately for reconstruction. For l=0 the radial vector test has zero curl; (30) and the nonzero constants force both a^E_00 and a^H_00 to vanish. No Psi_00 or Phi_00 is defined or divided by.

Equation (35), proved in (33)-(34), plus Theorem A implies every toroidal electric coefficient is zero on each spherical annulus where E,H are L^2_loc and the first Maxwell equation holds distributionally. This forward implication itself needs neither epsilon!=0 nor z!=0. The stronger two-field reconstruction and nonstatic interpretation require the assumptions of (V1)-(V2).

After the reconstruction theorem is established, (31)-(32) with d^H=0 show that the electric field has only radial/poloidal terms and the magnetic field only toroidal terms. This is the precise TE-exclusion consequence in this convention. It does not say the magnetic field is zero or select a single harmonic degree.
