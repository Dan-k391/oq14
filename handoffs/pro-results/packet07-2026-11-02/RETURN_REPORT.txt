2.B. ANGULAR BRANCH AND ITS EXACT DERIVATIVE

B1. Let V be the complex H¹(S²) subspace invariant under every rotation about e
and odd under the reflection of the axial coordinate omega.e. Its constants
vanish. On V, define sesquilinear forms
  a_eta(u,v)=integral q_eta grad u . conjugate(grad v),
  m_eta(u,v)=integral q_eta u conjugate(v).

product, A_eta=A_0+eta A_1 is invertible for |eta|<1/||A_0^{-1}A_1|| by its
operator Neumann series. M_eta is compact V→V* because H¹(S²)→L²(S²) is
compact. Thus T_eta=A_eta^{-1}M_eta is norm holomorphic and compact.

B2. At eta=0, the spherical harmonics in V are exactly the m=0, odd-degree
harmonics. Their Laplace eigenvalues are l(l+1), l=1,3,5,... . Consequently
1/2 is an isolated algebraically simple eigenvalue of T_0. A small contour
around 1/2, excluding all other spectral values, has uniformly invertible
resolvent for small |eta| by another Neumann series. The Riesz integral P_eta
is a holomorphic rank-one projection; rank remains one since ||P_eta-P_0||<1.
v_eta=P_eta phi_0 is nonzero. For a fixed continuous functional L with
L(phi_0)=1, put mu_eta=L(T_eta v_eta)/L(v_eta); then
T_eta v_eta=mu_eta v_eta. Let nu_eta=1/mu_eta.

B3. The bilinear mass b_eta=integral q_eta v_eta² is holomorphic and b_0=1.
Use the square root with value +1 at zero and put phi_eta=v_eta/sqrt(b_eta).
This gives integral q_eta phi_eta²=1. The generalized eigen-equation holds
against every test in V. Averaging an arbitrary H¹ test over axial rotations
and then taking its odd part gives a bounded projection Pi onto V. Invariance
of q_eta and the parity of phi_eta show that both form pairings against a test
equal their pairings against Pi(test). Thus the equation holds for every
H¹(S²) test, including the bilinear version obtained by conjugating the test.
The weighted mean integral q_eta phi_eta is zero by oddness.

B4. Define lambda_eta=(-1+sqrt(1+4nu_eta))/2, choosing the square root with

  nu'_0=integral a (|grad phi_0|²-2phi_0²)

normalization. Since 3lambda'_0=nu'_0,
  lambda'_0=-t(1-t²)<0.
Therefore 0<lambda_eta<1 for all sufficiently small real eta>0.

Dominated convergence makes the bracket tend to zero in L2(B_1), and the two operator bounds are
uniform. Therefore E_n -> E in L2(B_1). This is the mathematical analogue of supplied
MaterialVolumeLimit.volumeField_tendsto and is valid for our explicit sequence, not merely for the
existing opaque rounding.

4.C. ANGULAR DATA AT A FIXED DIELECTRIC

Fix t=3/4 (any t in (1/sqrt(3),1) works), axis e3, beta=t/sqrt(1-t^2)=3/sqrt(7), and
 q(omega)=1+d 1_{|omega dot e3|>t}.

eliminates constants, giving coercivity and compactness; a real minimizer phi exists, normalized by

 [2+d(2-3t+t^3)]/[1+d(1-t^3)]<2.
Thus the positive eigenvalue nu satisfies 0<nu<2, and its positive indicial root
lambda=(-1+sqrt(1+4nu))/2 lies in (0,1). Also int q phi=0. These angular quantities depend only on
the already fixed d,t, not N.

The electrostatic field E0=grad(r^lambda phi) is L2, curl free, and div(qE0)=0 in the whole ball.

4.D. LOCAL POSITIVE-FREQUENCY EXCITATION (NO UNPROVED RUNGE HYPOTHESIS)

D1. Ordinary-ball Maxwell inequality, proved with a Fourier projection.
For w in H0(curl,B_1) with div w=0 in B_1, we prove
  ||w||_2 <= C ||curl w||_2.
H0(curl) means closure of compact smooth vector fields in the H(curl) norm.
Their zero extensions show that u=extend_0(w) belongs to H(curl,R^3), with
curl u=extend_0(curl w). Its divergence need NOT vanish globally.

Set W=P_T u. Plancherel and the real transverse Fourier symbol give
  div W=0, curl W=curl u,
  ||grad W||_{L2(R3)}=||curl u||_{L2(R3)}.
W is also in L2 because P_T is bounded. The homogeneous three-dimensional
Sobolev inequality ||W||_6<=C||grad W||_2, followed by Holder on B_1, gives
  ||W||_{H1(B_1)} <= C ||curl w||_{L2(B_1)}.
Every hypothesis of that Sobolev inequality holds: W belongs to H1(R3),
as established by the Fourier identity; no boundary extension regularity
beyond the proved H(curl) zero extension is assumed.

Inside B_1 the curl-free field w-W is grad p for p in H1, by the same weak
ball potential construction as section 2. Since div w=div W=0 there, p is
weakly harmonic. Let h=Tr p in H^(1/2)(S2). The zero tangential trace of w
gives, in surface distributions,
  grad_S h=-W_t|_{S2}.
For rigor, the H(curl) tangential trace is the weak integration-by-parts
functional. Zero trace follows from the compact smooth approximants defining
H0(curl). The gradient trace identity follows by smooth H1 approximation of p:
their gradients converge in the H(curl) graph norm because all curls are zero.
The usual H1 trace estimate for W on the smooth ball puts W_t in H^(1/2),
hence in L2(S2). A distribution with L2 weak first derivatives and L2 value
on the sphere is H1 in its smooth charts. Thus h is H1, and surface Poincare
gives
  ||h-mean(h)||_{H1(S2)} <= C||W_t||_{L2(S2)} <= C||W||_{H1(B1)}.

Choose a fixed smooth radial cutoff zeta, zero on r<=1/2 and one near r=1.
The explicit shell extension v(r,omega)=zeta(r)(h(omega)-mean(h)) is H1(B1)
and ||grad v||_2<=C||h-mean(h)||_{H1(S2)}. Now
p-mean(h)-v has zero trace, hence belongs to H0^1(B1). Testing harmonicity
against this function gives
  ||grad p||_2^2 = int grad p dot conjugate(grad v)
                <= ||grad p||_2 ||grad v||_2
after taking real parts where needed. Consequently
  ||grad p||_2 <= C||W||_{H1(B1)} <= C||curl w||_2,
and w=W+grad p proves the desired estimate. The initial construction of p
may use ||w||_2 for existence, but the final bound does not: there is no
circular coercivity argument. This proof requires only the standard trace,
surface Poincare, and whole-space Sobolev facts, with their precise domains;
it does not import a conical compactness theorem, a regularized lifting,
a Gaffney identity, or boundary H2 regularity.

D2. Weighted ball estimate and the exact finite-frequency local field.
Define X_q={w in H0(curl,B_1): div(qw)=0}. Decompose w=w_T+grad p, p in H0^1, div w_T=0, by the
ordinary Dirichlet Helmholtz decomposition. Since div w_T=0,
 int q grad p dot grad conj(psi)=-int(q-1)w_T dot grad conj(psi).
Testing psi=p gives ||grad p||<=d||w_T||, hence
 ||w||<=C_q||curl w||, C_q<=(1+d)C.

For 0<xi with xi^2(1+d)C_q^2<1/2, solve on X_q by Lax-Milgram
 int curl w dot curl conj(z)-xi^2 q w dot conj(z)
 =xi^2 int q E0 dot conj(z).
Then ||w||_Hcurl<=C xi^2 ||E0||. The weighted Helmholtz decomposition of any test into X_q plus grad
H0^1 shows the same equation holds for every H0(curl) test, because both sides vanish on the
gradient part. Thus E_xi=E0+w is an actual local Maxwell electric field with div(q E_xi)=0 and curl
curl E_xi=xi^2 q E_xi.

On a smaller ball F=curl w has div F=0 and curl F=xi^2 q(E0+w) in L2. The cutoff Fourier curl/div
identity implies ||F||_H1=O(xi^2). Apply section 2's fixed graph lift to w, whose L2 norm and
curl-curl L2
norm are both O(xi^2). It gives W in H2 with ||W||_H2=O(xi^2), and
E_xi=grad v+W with ||v-r^lambda phi||_H1=O(xi^2), after constants
are fixed. Thus no extra lifting theorem is needed here either.

For the fixed real exponent lambda<1, the coefficient functional is obtained by the radial projected equation
 (r^2 U')'-lambda(lambda+1)U=-(r^2 A)'+rB,
 U=int q v phi, A=int q W_r phi, B=int q W_t dot grad_S phi.
Its finite-energy solutions have U=c r^lambda+p_W(r), with
 p_W(r)=(1/(2lambda+1))[r^lambda int_0^r s^(-lambda)(B-(lambda+1)A)ds
   -r^(-lambda-1)int_0^r s^(lambda+1)(B+lambda A)ds].
Because W is bounded, p_W=O(r), p'_W=O(1). Averaging r^(-lambda)(U-p_W) over a fixed annulus defines
c as a bounded linear functional of v in H1 and W in H2. The singular r^(-lambda-1) homogeneous
solution is excluded by finite H1 energy. A different bounded curl lifting changes v by a Lipschitz
potential; weighted mean zero makes its angular projection O(r), so c is intrinsic. Therefore
c(E_xi)=1+O(xi^2), and is nonzero for all sufficiently small positive xi.

If c!=0, U=c r^lambda+O(r). The angular eigen-equation/Cauchy-Schwarz bound gives sqrt(nu)|U(r)| <=
(int q |grad_S v|^2)^(1/2). A bound on E in an entire vertex ball would bound grad v=E-W and give
U=O(r), impossible. This proves essential unboundedness in every smaller ball, not merely a formal
ray asymptotic. Interior Helmholtz regularity on each homogeneous region permits ordinary high-value
points away from interface null sets. This is the real-parameter specialization of the main Packet07
coefficient analysis.

Rescaling the unit local construction gives a nonzero-coefficient local Maxwell
solution at any fixed kappa on a sufficiently small ball: set xi=kappa r_local.
Indeed E_local(x)=E_xi((x-c)/r_local) solves the equation at kappa; its
potential is r_local*v((x-c)/r_local), so its conical coefficient is
r_local^(1-lambda)c(E_xi), still nonzero. It does not create an internal source or a physical PEC
boundary. Tangential boundary data were only used to construct a local comparison field for the
density argument.

4.E. TOTAL-FIELD RUNGE FOR THIS SAME COMMON MATERIAL

Let Q be a finite disjoint union of closed balls compactly contained in B_1. Assume the coefficient
is the single finite collection of truncated double cones defined in F below. The complement R3\Q is
connected. Let V be any actual local Maxwell electric field on a neighborhood U of Q for that same
coefficient.

Suppose f in L2(Q)^3 annihilates all total fields generated by smooth tangential Herglotz densities,
under the complex BILINEAR integral int f dot E_g. Extend f by zero. Let W be the source solution
from B, J=f+kappa^2 chi W. Reciprocity of the actual volume operator gives
 int f dot E_g = int J dot E_in,g.
Indeed f=(I-chi K)J, E_in,g=(I-K chi)E_g, and K is bilinear symmetric. For its static part this
follows from the real even symmetric Fourier multiplier -xi xi^T/|xi|^2; for its dynamic part it
follows from its even symmetric matrix kernel and Fubini/Young. Thus no unverified Green boundary
identity is hidden here.

With the physical incident normalization in G, annihilation for all smooth tangential g implies
 (I-dd^T) int J(y) exp(i kappa d dot y)dy=0, for every d in S2.
The Fourier integral is continuous (J compactly supported L2, hence L1), and all smooth tangent
tests suffice. The actual outgoing representation W=kappa^(-2)KJ has far field
 W_infty(omega)=(1/(4pi))(I-omega omega^T) int J(y)exp(-i kappa omega dot y)dy.
It is zero. Each exterior component satisfies scalar Helmholtz and is outgoing; its outgoing
spherical-Hankel expansion has far-field coefficients equal to nonzero constants times the expansion
coefficients. Orthogonality on S2 therefore makes every coefficient zero. Hence W=0 outside a large
sphere.

Propagate zero throughout R3\Q. Within each material, the homogeneous Maxwell equations imply the
constant-coefficient vector Helmholtz equation and hence analyticity. Across any smooth interface
patch disjoint from Q, if W vanishes on one side, then epsilon W equals the other side's constant
epsilon times W on the whole patch. Thus the original weak equation becomes a constant-coefficient
Maxwell equation on the whole crossing ball, with no distributional interface term. Its divergence
is zero because kappa!=0; it is a weak Helmholtz equation, hence analytic, and vanishes throughout
the ball. This supplies the crossing argument directly from the weak equations; classical traces are
not assumed.

For our finite cones and sphere caps, any two ordinary points in R3\Q can be joined by a polygonal
path avoiding the finite vertex/joining-circle set and crossing interfaces finitely and
transversely. Start with an exterior path, possible since finitely many disjoint balls have
connected/path-connected complement, then perturb its finitely many vertices in small open
neighborhoods. The excluded exceptional choices are finitely many algebraic tangency/intersection
constraints for cones/spheres. Their complements are dense; line/interface intersections are finite
polynomial-root sets. A finite chain of the preceding continuation arguments proves W=0 a.e. off Q.
The lower-dimensional exceptional sets have volume zero.

Now choose zeta in C_c^infinity(U) equal to one near Q. W is H(curl), supported in Q, and curl curl
W=f+kappa^2 epsilon W is L2. Since V satisfies the homogeneous equation on U, use W as its weak
H(curl) test by compact mollification inside U. Equivalently, use the bilinear identity with zeta V
and W; all cutoff terms are outside supp W. It follows that int_Q f dot V=0. This step uses H(curl)
density and compact support, not tangential traces on the possibly interface-crossing boundary of Q.

Every continuous complex-linear functional on L2(Q)^3 has the form int f dot V after absorbing
conjugation in its representing vector. Hahn-Banach therefore proves that total electric fields for
the actual common material are L2(Q)-dense among the displayed local solutions. This is total-field
Runge in the material, not vacuum approximation inside the material.

Choose one local nonzero-coefficient comparison field near each tip. On disjoint neighborhoods they
form one local V. Approximate V on the union Q to enough L2 accuracy that the bounded coefficient
functional at every tip remains nonzero. A finite collection of positive tolerances has a positive
minimum. This produces ONE smooth density g0 and ONE actual total field with all coefficients
nonzero. Since they are nonzero g0!=0. Dividing by ||g0||_L2 fixes the incoming power to one and
preserves every nonzero coefficient. This g is chosen once before ANY rounding and is kept fixed for
every rounded coefficient.

Cone/sphere joining circles are allowed in this SHARP comparison device and will be rounded too.

Here is an explicit inward smooth rounding with exactly two connected inclusions per center. Take a
smooth nondecreasing theta:[0,infinity)->[0,1], theta=0 near 0, theta=1 for s>=3/4. Define for
0<=s<=1

and h(s)=s for s>=1. It is smooth, nondecreasing, convex, constant near zero, h(s)>=s, h(0)>0, and
0<=h(s)-s<=1. Thus h_eps(rho)=eps h(rho/eps) is a smooth convex radial function of the transverse
vector, equals rho for rho>=eps, and is >=eps h(0).

Define H_eps(u)=eps h(|u|/eps); it is smooth even convex, H_eps>=|u|, |H'_eps|<=1, and H_eps=|u|
when |u|>=eps. Set

This is smooth, jointly convex and nondecreasing in both arguments;
max(u,v)<=m_eps(u,v)<=max(u,v)+eps/2, with equality to max when |u-v|>=eps.

In coordinates centered at a tip define

 F_eps(x)=m_eps(f_eps(x),b(x)),
 D_eps^+={F_eps<0}, D_eps^-=-D_eps^+.
Both inputs to m_eps are smooth convex functions, so F_eps is smooth convex. Since F_eps>=max(beta
rho-z,b), D_eps^+ lies inside the original sharp upper truncated cone. It is bounded and open. At
x=(0,0,a/2), it is negative whenever eps<a/[8(beta+1)], so the set is nonempty. Every boundary point
has F_eps=0 and grad F_eps!=0: a zero gradient for a differentiable convex function would give a
global minimum at value zero, contradicting the interior negative point. Conversely every F_eps=0
point is approached by negative points along its segment to the interior point. Thus the boundary is
precisely the regular level F_eps=0 and is C-infinity. D_eps^+ is convex, hence connected. Its
closure has z>=beta eps h(0)>0. The reflected lower closure has z<=-beta eps h(0), so their closures
are disjoint. Different tips' closures lie in disjoint radius-a balls. Consequently the union
contains EXACTLY 2N nonempty connected smooth inclusions and no other components. This argument
proves cardinality, not merely smooth local defining functions.

Choose eps_n=eps_0/(n+1), eps_0<a/[8(beta+1)], common across the finite collection. F_eps converges
uniformly on bounded sets to max(beta rho-z,b); hence indicators converge at every point off the
sharp cone/sphere boundary, and those finitely many surfaces have measure zero. The common rounded
susceptibilities chi_n=d sum_j 1_{D_{j,eps_n}^+ union D_{j,eps_n}^-} satisfy 0<=chi_n<=chi<=d, all
have support in the SAME B_1, and converge a.e. to the SAME sharp common chi.

Exact fixed-collar agreement is also proved, not presumed. Fix any compact annulus r0<=|x|<=b0 with

uniformly negative. The uniform inequalities 0<=f_eps-f_0<=beta eps and 0<=m_eps-max<=eps/2 then
preserve its sign relative to zero, using b<=-sigma. Thus D_eps^+ and D_0^+ agree POINTWISE on the
entire annulus for all sufficiently small eps. Reflection proves the lower case. Choose an enlarged
