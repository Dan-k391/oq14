2. COMPLETE NUMBERED MATHEMATICAL PROOF

2.1 Polar slices and changes of representatives

For each of the three coordinates, the physical-coordinate interface gives E_j=ℰ_j volume-a.e. Polar integration/disintegration transfers this to

  E_j(rω)=ℰ_j(rω)

for a.e. r>0 and, at each such radius, for μ-a.e. ω. Explicitly, a volume-null exceptional set N gives

  ∫_0^∞ r² ∫ 1_N(rω) dμ(ω) dr=0.

The integrand is nonnegative. Since r²>0 on (0,∞), its inner integral vanishes for unweighted-dr-a.e. r>0. The equivalence of weighted and unweighted null sets can also be checked on the countable annuli [1/m,m].

Likewise, E_j∈L²(volume) and polar integration give

  ∫_0^∞ r² ∫ |E_j(rω)|² dμ(ω) dr <∞,

so the angular slice is L² for a.e. r>0. Intersect these finitely many full-measure radial sets, and then the finitely many angular full-measure sets for the three coordinates.

The radial and gradient polynomial weights belong to L²(μ), as stated by accepted HarmonicMomentCoupling.weight_memLp. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore makes every product in A_P and G_P angular integrable at the good radii. Their finite sums and differences are integrable too. No statement about integrability at every radius is required.

At these radii, finite sum/integral interchange and the representative equalities give

  A_P(r)
    =∫ Σ_j E_j(rω)ω_jY_P(ω) dμ
    =∫ (Σ_j ω_jℰ_j(rω))Y_P(ω) dμ
    =A_P^act(r).

The same argument with D_jP and with D_jP-Lω_jY_P gives the literal gradient and poloidal angular integrals.

This proves the mathematical content of the pending KernelRadialMomentBridge.radial_moment_actual. Its existing hypotheses suffice; hz, the kernel equation, homogeneity, and harmonicity are not needed for this bridge. The proof does not assume equality of arbitrary representatives on every sphere.

2.2 Explicit integrability bounds and their dependence

Define finite constants

  C_A²=∫ |Y_P|² dμ,
  C_G²=∫ Σ_j |D_jP|² dμ,
  C_B²=∫ Σ_j |D_jP-Lω_jY_P|² dμ.

The triangle inequality in angular vector L² gives C_B≤C_G+L C_A. These constants depend only on p, L, and the supplied angular measure. They do not depend on n,z,u.

Set

  Q(r)²=∫ Σ_j |E_j(rω)|² dμ.

At good radii, vector Cauchy–Schwarz and Σ_jω_j²=1 give

  |A_P(r)|≤C_A Q(r),
  |G_P(r)|≤C_G Q(r),
  |B_P(r)|≤C_B Q(r).

Absolute values used in this estimate do not change the bilinear convention of the moments.

For 0<a<b<1 put

  N_ab²=Σ_j ∫_{a<|x|<b} |E_j(x)|² dx
       =∫_a^b r²Q(r)² dr.

Consequently,

  ||rA_P||_{L²(a,b)}≤C_A N_ab,
  ||rG_P||_{L²(a,b)}≤C_G N_ab,
  ||rB_P||_{L²(a,b)}≤C_B N_ab.

In particular,

  ||A_P||_{L¹(a,b)}≤a^(-1)√(b-a) C_A N_ab,
  ||G_P||_{L¹(a,b)}≤a^(-1)√(b-a) C_G N_ab,
  ||B_P||_{L¹(a,b)}≤a^(-1)√(b-a) C_B N_ab,
  ||r²A_P||_{L¹(a,b)}≤b√(b-a) C_A N_ab,
  ||rB_P||_{L¹(a,b)}≤√(b-a) C_B N_ab.

Thus all moments and the functions F=r²A_P and H=rB_P are locally integrable on I, in fact locally square integrable. The same holds for their literal actual counterparts by §2.1. Compact tests and any of their derivatives can be multiplied into these functions and integrated absolutely. This proves the necessary regularity instead of assuming it as a theorem hypothesis.

No numerical 4π factor is inserted: the constants use μ exactly as supplied. No estimate uniform in degree, frequency, or distance to either endpoint is claimed or needed.

2.3 Identification of the actual tangential weight

Homogeneity gives P(tω)=t^L P(ω), with the same complex evaluation as in the packet. Differentiate this polynomial identity at t=1:

  Σ_j ω_jD_jP(ω)=L Y_P(ω).

Therefore T_P(ω)=DP(ω)-LωY_P(ω) has ω·T_P=0. For any tangent vector v with v·ω=0, the directional derivative of the restricted polynomial in direction v equals DP(ω)·v=T_P(ω)·v. This is the defining property of the surface gradient. Hence

  T_P=∇_{S²}Y_P,
  B_P(r)=∫ ℰ(rω)·∇_{S²}Y_P(ω) dμ

for a.e. r. This is the requested literal poloidal moment. No division by L(L+1) is appropriate for this definition.

2.4 Reduction of the accepted weighted test identity

Use the accepted HarmonicMomentIntegrability.actual_radial_tangential_relation with its Lean index l=L-1. It states, for every complex η smooth on ℝ with compact topological support contained in I,

  ∫_0^∞ η' r^(L+2) A_P dr
  +∫_0^∞ η r^(L+1)(B_P+L A_P) dr=0.                 (W)

The displayed source already justifies the two weighted integrals. We additionally established the integrability needed to split and cancel the terms below.

Let φ be an arbitrary complex C_c^∞(I) test, viewed as its smooth zero extension to ℝ. Set

  η(r)=φ(r)/(r:ℂ)^L for r≠0, and η(0)=0.

There is a neighborhood of 0 on which φ is identically zero, because its topological support is a compact subset of I. Hence η is identically zero there and smooth at 0. Away from 0 it is a quotient of smooth functions with nonvanishing denominator. Its support is contained in the support of φ, so it is an admissible global smooth compact test. In Lean's totalized division, the same function can simply be written φ r/(r:ℂ)^L.

For r>0,

  η'=r^(-L)φ' - L r^(-L-1)φ.

Writing F=r²A_P and H=rB_P, the integrands become

  η' r^(L+2)A_P = φ'F-Lφ rA_P,
  η r^(L+1)(B_P+LA_P)=φH+Lφ rA_P.

All four terms are integrable on the common compact support, by §2.2. Their cancellation in (W) is therefore legitimate and gives

  ∫_I φ'F dr +∫_I φH dr=0.                         (U)

Both φ and φ' vanish outside the topological support of φ. The integrals can consequently be taken over I, (0,∞), or all ℝ, as needed. This justifies the change from the source's restricted integrals to the whole-line convention in WeakDerivativeIdentification.

By the convention <F',φ>=-∫Fφ', equation (U) is exactly F'=H, with positive sign. No angular integral has been differentiated.

2.5 Weak-derivative uniqueness, with the representative change justified

Here is the precise analytic principle being used:

  If J⊂ℝ is an open interval, f has derivative df at every point of J,
  df is continuous on J, g∈L¹_loc(J), and
  ∫φ'f+∫φg=0 for every compact smooth real test φ in J,
  then g=df a.e. on J.

For a compact test, choose an interval whose interior contains its support and whose closure lies in J. The classical product rule and fundamental theorem of calculus, applied to real and imaginary parts, give ∫φ'f=-∫φdf; there are no boundary terms because φ vanishes near the interval endpoints. Thus ∫φ(g-df)=0.

For completeness, the test-annihilation principle for an L¹_loc function h follows by mollification. On each compact subinterval K⊂J, choose a compact cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of K and extend the product with h by zero. Testing against translated, sufficiently narrow smooth mollifiers makes its convolutions vanish on K. The convolutions converge in L¹(K) to h. Indeed, the approximate-identity estimate follows from continuity of translations in L¹, which follows by approximating L¹ functions by continuous compactly supported functions and using their uniform continuity. Hence h=0 a.e. on each K, and a countable exhaustion gives the conclusion on J.

The supplied accepted theorem WeakDerivativeIdentification.classical implements the displayed analytic principle. Its tests are real-valued; equation (U), proved for complex tests, supplies them by taking the complex coercion of a real test. Its derivative is the complex coercion of the real derivative.

If F=f a.e. on I, (U) transfers to f: multiplying the a.e. equality by the compactly supported φ' preserves equality, and both products are integrable. Outside I the test derivative is zero. No classical differentiability of the original arbitrary representative F is asserted.

2.7 Separate degree-zero flux calculation from actual divergence

Now take p=1. Polynomial evaluation gives Y_1=1 and its polynomial derivatives are zero. Put F_0(r)=r²A_1^act(r), equivalently r²A_1(r) a.e. By §2.2, F_0∈L¹_loc(I).

Given η∈C_c^∞(I;ℂ), define Φ(x)=η(|x|). It vanishes on a neighborhood of the origin, is smooth everywhere, and has compact support in an annulus contained in the unit ball. For x=rω and r>0,

  ∂_jΦ(rω)=η'(r)ω_j.

Apply the accepted ComplexSphereDivergence.actual_divergence to this actual test:

  0=Σ_j ∫ ℰ_j(x)∂_jΦ(x) dx.

Therefore F_0'=0 in D'(I). This is a direct degree-zero argument. It does not specialize the positive-degree coupling at an invalid index.

2.8 Derive the actual degree-zero radial equation

Let q=DebyeScalarL2.datum 1 (ComplexKernelDebye.coordinates n hn z u).

The accepted scalar_weak states

  ∫ q(x)(ΔΦ(x)+k²Φ(x)) dx=0

for every smooth compact test supported in the unit ball. This is a theorem about the actual L² Debye datum, not an assumed scalar substitute.

The accepted scalar_actual identifies q with x·ℰ inside the ball. More directly, accepted moment_actual at p=1 gives

  m_0(r):=∫q(rω)dμ = r A_1^act(r) a.e. on I.        (M)

Let ψ∈C_c^∞(I;ℂ), set χ(r)=ψ(r)/r for r>0 and zero for r≤0, and use Φ(x)=χ(|x|). The same support argument as in §2.4 proves that χ and Φ are globally smooth with compact support in an interior annulus.

For r>0,

  χ'=ψ'/r-ψ/r²,
  χ''=ψ''/r-2ψ'/r²+2ψ/r³.

To compute the three-dimensional radial Laplacian, differentiate
∂_jΦ=χ'(r)x_j/r and sum over j:

  ΔΦ=χ''(r)Σ_jx_j²/r²
      +χ'(r)Σ_j(1/r-x_j²/r³)
     =χ''(r)+2χ'(r)/r
     =ψ''(r)/r.

At the origin the test vanishes on a neighborhood, so no singularity or distribution supported at the origin is introduced.

The product q(ΔΦ+k²Φ) is integrable since q∈L² and the other factor is bounded with compact support. Thus polar integration and (M) give

  0=∫_I r²m_0(r)[ψ''(r)+k²ψ(r)]/r dr
   =∫_I F_0(r)[ψ''(r)+k²ψ(r)]dr.                  (R0)

All one-dimensional products are integrable by §2.2. This proves

  F_0''+k²F_0=0 in D'(I).

The coefficient is k²=(zn)², not |k|². This derivation needs no differentiation of q or of its angular integral.

As an independent sign check, the scalar spherical-Bessel equation is
t²w''+2tw'+[t²-L(L+1)]w=0; after the Riccati change its degree-zero radial form is exactly F''+k²F=0. See NIST DLMF 10.47.1:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.47#E1
The proof above derives the required equation from the actual field, so the external formula is only a check, not a new premise or an identification of the project's regular-mode normalization.

2.9 Eliminate the monopole

The derivative ψ' is also a compact smooth test supported in I: differentiation cannot enlarge topological support. Take η=ψ' in (F), obtaining

  ∫_I F_0 ψ'' dr=0.

Subtract this from (R0):

  k²∫_I F_0 ψ dr=0.

Since k≠0, k²≠0, so ∫F_0ψ=0 for every compact smooth test. The test-annihilation principle proved in §2.5 gives F_0=0 a.e. on I. Division by the nonzero r² yields

  A_1^act=0 a.e. on I,

and the representative bridge gives A_1=0 a.e.

For Lean, the final test-annihilation step can reuse the accepted identification theorem: the identity ∫ψF_0=0 is precisely the weak-derivative condition with f=0 and g=F_0. Apply WeakDerivativeIdentification.classical with f=df=0 and the proved local integrability of F_0. It returns F_0=0 a.e.

Flux conservation by itself only proves a constant distribution on the connected interval; it does not by itself force that constant to vanish. Here the actual nonzero-frequency radial equation supplies the missing argument.

2.10 Relation to the regular degree-zero representation and degree bookkeeping

The accepted Debye radial theorem is valid at p=1,L=0, because 1 is homogeneous of degree zero and harmonic. It gives F_0=c regular(0,k) a.e. The proof above forces this representative to be zero as a distribution and, by continuity, throughout I. It is therefore legitimate to choose the zero amplitude for the monopole.

A second valid route transfers (F) to c regular(0,k), uses weak-derivative identification to get c regularD(0,k)=0, and combines that with the degree-zero Riccati equation. However, the supplied regularD_hasDerivAt refers to an imported RadialRellich.radialCoefficient whose definition is not printed. That shortcut would need the exact interface

  RadialRellich.radialCoefficient 0 k r = -(k^2)

at r≠0. The direct proof in §2.8 removes that dependency.

No angular-component classification was used. The scalar degree in the Debye moment belongs to x·ℰ. A componentwise harmonic degree of ℰ_j is a different object; the result does not eliminate those componentwise constant harmonics. The constant angular polynomial has zero surface gradient, but the positive-degree-indexed tangentialMoment must still not be called at l=0 to represent it.
