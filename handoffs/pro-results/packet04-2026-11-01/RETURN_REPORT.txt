2.2 Angular integrability, completeness, and the correct degree bookkeeping

Choose a real orthonormal scalar spherical-harmonic basis Y_Lm for the supplied
angular measure. The real choice matters: the Packet 01 bilinear moment then
equals the corresponding Hermitian coefficient. Complex coefficients remain
allowed for the field. Write lambda_L=L(L+1), and define

  R_Lm=omega Y_Lm,
  G_Lm=grad_S Y_Lm,
  T_Lm=omega cross grad_S Y_Lm.

For L>=1 their squared norms are 1, lambda_L, lambda_L. The radial family
includes L=0; the two tangent families begin at L=1.

Integration by parts on the closed smooth sphere gives
integral grad Y.grad Z = -integral Y Delta_S Z, hence gradient orthogonality
and the norm lambda_L. Also div_S(omega cross grad_S Z)=0: in any oriented
local chart it is the cancellation of the two mixed second derivatives.
An integration by parts therefore gives integral grad Y.(omega cross grad Z)=0.
Rotation by omega cross is an isometry on each tangent plane. This proves
orthogonality of all the normalized R,G,T families.

Here is a finite-dimensional proof of completeness, rather than an assumed
Hodge decomposition. Let H_j denote homogeneous harmonic scalar polynomials
of degree j restricted to the sphere. Its dimension is 2j+1. One way to see
this and scalar polynomial completeness is the decomposition of homogeneous
polynomials into |x|^(2s)H_(j-2s). The calculation

  Delta(|x|^(2s) H_d)
    =2s(2d+2s+1)|x|^(2s-2)H_d

has a nonzero coefficient for s>=1, proving that decomposition inductively.
The dimension difference is
binomial(j+2,2)-binomial(j,2)=2j+1. Polynomial restrictions are dense in
continuous functions on the compact sphere because their real algebra
contains constants and separates points; continuous functions are dense in
L2 for the finite spherical measure. Alternatively the supplied accepted
scalar harmonic expansion gives this last scalar completeness directly.

For a harmonic polynomial P of degree L define vector polynomials

  H(P)=grad P,
  T(P)=x cross grad P,
  Q(P)=x P-|x|^2 grad P/(2L+1).

Their Cartesian components are harmonic of degrees L-1, L, L+1 respectively.
For Q this follows from

  Delta(x_i P)=2 partial_i P,
  Delta(|x|^2 partial_i P)=2(2L+1) partial_i P.

For T the extra second-derivative terms cancel by antisymmetry. On the sphere,

  H(P)=L R+G,
  T(P)=T,
  Q(P)=((L+1)R-G)/(2L+1).

Their squared norms, for ||Y||=1, are respectively
L(2L+1), L(L+1), and (L+1)/(2L+1). Thus H is injective for L>=1,
T for L>=1, and Q for L>=0.

For each scalar component degree j>=1, the space H_j tensor C^3 contains
the mutually orthogonal images

  H(H_(j+1)),     T(H_j),     Q(H_(j-1)).

Their dimensions sum to
(2j+3)+(2j+1)+(2j-1)=3(2j+1), the entire dimension. For j=0,
H(H_1) already has dimension 3 and fills the space. Therefore all vector
polynomial restrictions lie in the span of R,G,T. Scalar density applied
coordinatewise proves vector completeness.

This proves, for every vector angular L2 field F,

  F = sum a_Lm R_Lm + sum b_Lm G_Lm + sum t_Lm T_Lm

in angular L2, with

  a_Lm=integral F.R_Lm,
  b_Lm=lambda_L^(-1) integral F.G_Lm,
  t_Lm=lambda_L^(-1) integral F.T_Lm.

In particular there is no invisible radial or tangent remainder. This proof
also explains why physical electric vector degree L contains Cartesian scalar
degrees L-1 and L+1; toroidal degree L has Cartesian scalar degree L.
They must not be identified.

For actual volume fields, polar integration gives angular L2 slices for
dr-a.e. r>0. If Q_F(r)^2=integral |F(r omega)|^2 dmu, then

  |a_Lm(r)|<=Q_F(r),
  |b_Lm(r)|,|t_Lm(r)|<=Q_F(r)/sqrt(lambda_L).

All compact radial test pairings are absolutely integrable by Cauchy-Schwarz.
The supplied slice_actual_ae theorem identifies the actual slices, and the
accepted reconstruction bound is consistent with these estimates. Taking
one countable intersection over L,m, coordinates and rational radial collars
gives a common full-measure radial set. Angular reconstruction there, followed
by polar integration, gives genuine volume-a.e. equality.

2.3 Interior magnetic scalar vanishing and removal of the TE part

This section supplies the minimal missing Packet 02 conclusion directly.

The accepted sector actions therefore imply U_Q E=E in L2(B) for every axial
rotation Q and for the meridional reflection S_ref.

For an orthogonal Q, the distributional identity is

  curl(U_Q F)=det(Q) U_Q(curl F).                              (2.3)

For smooth F it follows from the chain rule and
(Qa) cross (Qb)=det(Q) Q(a cross b). For a locally L2 field the same identity
follows by changing variables in a compact smooth test; the Jacobian has
absolute value one. Alternatively mollify on smaller invariant balls and
pass to distributions. All test products are integrable.

Use curl E=izH and z!=0 in (2.3). The actual H transforms as an axial vector:
U_Q H=H for rotations and U_S_ref H=-H for the reflection. Consequently
f(x)=x.H(x) is rotation-invariant and reflection-odd as an L2(B) scalar.

The a.e. quantifier issue is resolved as follows. Extend f by zero outside
the invariant ball and convolve with a smooth compactly supported radial
approximate identity rho_delta. For each fixed Q, a change of variables
transfers the L2 symmetry to the smooth function f_delta pointwise. Thus the
smooth f_delta has all these symmetries at every point. For every x,
S_ref x is on the same axial rotation orbit as x, including the axis itself.
It follows that

  f_delta(S_ref x)=f_delta(x)=-f_delta(x),

so f_delta=0. Approximate-identity convergence in L2 gives

  x.H(x)=0 a.e. in B.                                         (2.4)

This does not choose an x-dependent rotation within an a.e. identity.
Approximate-identity convergence follows, for example, from continuity of
translations in L2, first for continuous compact functions and then by density.

To eliminate the electric toroidal coefficient, fix Y=Y_Lm, L>=1, put
T=omega cross grad_S Y, and test curl E=izH with eta(r)Y(omega)omega,
where eta is smooth and compactly supported in (0,1). Since

  curl(eta R)=-(eta/r)T,

the weak equation reads

  -integral r eta(r) integral E(r omega).T(omega) dmu dr
    = iz integral r^2 eta(r) integral H(r omega).omega Y dmu dr.

The right side is zero by (2.4). Test-annihilation for the locally integrable
left coefficient gives t_Lm=0 a.e. The radial and gradient projections of
curl E=izH then also make the radial and gradient coefficients of H zero.
Thus the interior field has only the physical TM blocks. The electric radial
L=0 coefficient is separately zero by reviewed Packet 01 Theorem D.

2.4 Global modal equations and one-dimensional H1 regularity

Fix L>=1 and a normalized real Y. Write lambda=L(L+1) and R,G,T as above.
For a compact radial test eta away from the origin,

  curl(eta R)=-(eta/r)T,
  curl(eta G)=((r eta)'/r)T,
  curl(eta T)=-(lambda eta/r)R-((r eta)'/r)G.                   (2.5)

These identities follow by differentiating on spherical-coordinate charts
and using Delta_S Y=-lambda Y. Each side is smooth on the punctured space,
so the chart identities agree at chart boundaries. Only these finite smooth
tests are differentiated; no infinite L2 series is differentiated.

Define actual moments

  a(r)=integral E.R,       b(r)=lambda^(-1) integral E.G,
  h(r)=lambda^(-1) integral H.T.

Apply curl H=-iz epsilon E to eta R and eta G, and curl E=izH to eta T.
Polar integration gives, respectively,

  -lambda integral r h eta = -iz integral epsilon r^2 a eta,
  lambda integral r h (r eta)' =
       -iz lambda integral epsilon r^2 b eta,
  -lambda integral [r a eta+r b(r eta)'] =
       iz lambda integral r^2 h eta.

Let

  U(r)=r h(r)/(iz)=epsilon(r) r^2 a(r)/lambda,
  V(r)=r b(r).

Because phi=r eta ranges over all compact smooth tests on a positive-radius
collar, the preceding identities are exactly

  U'=epsilon V,
  V'=(lambda/(epsilon r^2)-z^2)U                              (2.6)

in distributions, including across r=1. These TM equations hold even before
any TE block has been eliminated.

Let J=(r0,r1), with 0<r0<1<r1<T, and let shell(J) be its spherical shell.
Angular Cauchy-Schwarz and the polar Jacobian give the explicit estimates

  ||U||_L2(J) <= ||H||_L2(shell(J))/(|z| sqrt(lambda)),
  ||V||_L2(J) <= ||E||_L2(shell(J))/sqrt(lambda),
  ||U'||_L2(J) <= n^2 ||E||_L2(shell(J))/sqrt(lambda),
  ||V'||_L2(J) <= (lambda/r0^2+|z|^2)||U||_L2(J).             (2.7)

Thus U,V have H1 representatives on a collar crossing the interface.
For completeness, a locally integrable function with weak derivative g in L2
equals a constant plus the indefinite integral of g: subtract that integral;
the difference has distributional derivative zero. Testing against derivatives
of compact functions shows it annihilates every compact test of integral zero,
so it is a constant distribution. This proves the absolutely continuous
representative assertion without assuming classical differentiability of the
original moment.

Both representatives are continuous at 1. On either constant-material side,
(2.6) bootstraps to smoothness and gives

  U''+(epsilon z^2-lambda/r^2)U=0.                            (2.8)

Consequently the interface conditions are

  U(1-)=U(1+),
  U'(1-)/n^2=U'(1+).                                        (2.9)

The trace estimate

  |f(1)| <= |J|^(-1/2)||f||_2+|J|^(1/2)||f'||_2

follows by integrating f(1)=f(t)+integral_t^1 f' over t. The constants in (2.7)
and this estimate state all dependence needed for the finite-mode proof.
No estimate uniform in L or z is asserted.

For the TM block the physical pair is

  E_TM= lambda U/(epsilon r^2) R + U'/(epsilon r) G,

Equivalently M_L[U]=-(U/r)T and

2.5 These are actual weak tangential traces; exterior TE cannot remain

For F in H(curl) on one side of a smooth spherical boundary, define its
distributional tangential trace, on a smooth boundary test phi, by

  <nu cross F,phi>
     = integral_D [curl F.Phi-F.curl Phi],

where Phi is a smooth extension with any other boundary component suppressed.
This is independent of the extension. Indeed, an extension difference zero on
the boundary is O(distance). Cutting it off in a collar of width delta gives
interior compact tests. The cutoff derivative is O(1/delta), but its product
with the extension difference is bounded in that collar. The error is bounded
by a fixed smooth-test constant times the L2 norms of F,curl F in a shrinking
collar times its volume^(1/2), and tends to zero. This also proves continuity
as a distribution on smooth boundary tests.

Use the same outward-from-the-ball normal on both sides. Applying the global
weak curl equations to a test crossing the interface, and splitting its volume
integral into the two regions, gives

  nu cross E_- = nu cross E_+,
  nu cross H_- = nu cross H_+

as tangential trace distributions. Their fixed harmonic coefficients are
precisely the scalar H1 traces above: E_TM,t=V G/r and H_TM,t=iz U T/r.
Thus (2.9) is derived from actual weak Maxwell fields, not imposed on unknown
classical boundary values of their infinite angular sum.

Only interior magnetic-scalar vanishing was used. To rule out an exterior
TE remainder, let t=lambda^(-1) integral E.T, let d=integral H.R and
e=lambda^(-1) integral H.G, and set K=-r t. The other three projections of
the same global weak equations give

  d=lambda K/(iz r^2),   K'=iz r e,
  (r e)'=d+iz epsilon K.

Hence

  K''+(epsilon z^2-lambda/r^2)K=0                            (2.11)

distributionally on the collar. Its coefficients are bounded there and the
moments are L2. It follows that K and K' have H1 representatives, so both are
continuous across 1. Interior t=0 implies K(1)=K'(1)=0. On the exterior,
the ordinary linear ODE has unique solutions for specified Cauchy data; thus
K=0. Uniqueness follows directly by writing the first-order integral equation
and applying the elementary Gronwall inequality on each compact interval.
This eliminates the entire exterior TE block. No exterior symmetry theorem
and no far-field condition are needed for this step.

For completeness, exterior radial monopoles cannot remain either. Take the
constant normalized harmonic Y_00 and test curl H=-iz epsilon E with eta R_00
on any positive-radius annulus. Its curl is zero, so
0=-iz integral epsilon r^2 eta a_00 for every such test. Since z!=0 and
epsilon>0, test-annihilation gives a_00=0. Testing curl E=izH in the same way
kills the magnetic radial monopole. This extends the already reviewed
interior electric monopole conclusion to the exterior, without applying
Packet 01 beyond its stated domain.

To identify the span with the standard psi_L without assuming an unquoted

  psi_L(t)=t j_L(t),    eta_L(t)=t y_L(t),
  xi_L=psi_L+i eta_L,   zeta_L=psi_L-i eta_L.

They solve w''+(1-lambda/t^2)w=0 away from zero. The regular series is

  psi_L(t)=sum_(p>=0)
      (-1)^p t^(L+2p+1)/(2^p p! (2L+2p+1)!!).

checks the ODE. The singular solution can be obtained from eta_0=-cos t,
eta_1=-cos t/t-sin t and the adjacent-degree recurrence. Its leading behavior
is

  psi_L(t)~t^(L+1)/(2L+1)!!,
  eta_L(t)~-(2L-1)!! t^(-L).

The ODE makes their Wronskian constant; these leading terms give

  psi_L eta_L'-psi_L' eta_L=1,
  W(xi_L,zeta_L)=-2i,       W(psi_L,xi_L)=i.                 (2.12)

Here and below primes on special functions are argument derivatives.
These conventions and recurrences agree with NIST DLMF 10.47, 10.50, 10.51;
the series and Wronskian calculation above give the needed normalization.

Since nz!=0, the two radial solutions psi_L(nzr), eta_L(nzr) have nonzero
radial Wronskian nz. By ordinary ODE uniqueness, every interior profile is
a linear combination of them on the connected interval (0,1).

Actual L2 integrability and a=lambda U/(n^2 r^2) give

  integral_0^1 |U(r)|^2/r^2 dr
    <= n^4/lambda^2 ||E||_L2(B)^2 < infinity.

A nonzero eta_L coefficient would have leading magnitude a nonzero constant
times r^(-L), and this integral would diverge for L>=1. Therefore

  U_-(r)=alpha psi_L(nzr).                                  (2.13)

No exact value for the proportionality between regular and psi_L is needed.
There is no missing regular-normalization prerequisite in this mathematical
route. Degree zero is excluded separately by Packet 01 Theorem D.

2.7 Analytically outgoing physical vector degrees

The accepted outgoing theorem is a Cartesian scalar theorem. In its exact
form, for T>1, a homogeneous harmonic polynomial p of scalar degree d, any
Cartesian coordinate i, any u in X, and z!=0, it supplies one constant c with

  r integral E_T[u](r omega)_i p(omega) dmu
      =c RadialRellich.outgoing d z r

for almost every r in (1,T). It requires no kernel hypothesis. Both the
nonzero-frequency outgoing selection and annular amplitude gluing are
accepted in the supplied packet. Under (O) this is a multiple of xi_d(zr).

Fix the physical degree L>=1 and its real polynomial P. Define

  Q_i(x)=x_i P(x)-|x|^2 partial_i P(x)/(2L+1).

Every Q_i is homogeneous harmonic of scalar degree L+1, as proved in 2.2.
On the sphere, the vector Q=((L+1)R-G)/(2L+1). Consequently, for the actual
coefficients from 2.4,

  integral E.Q dmu
      =((L+1)a-lambda b)/(2L+1)
      =lambda[(L+1)U-rU']/((2L+1)r^2)                       (2.14)

in the exterior, where epsilon=1. Only finitely many actual component
integrals are involved. No assertion identifying scalar degree L+1 with
physical vector degree L has been made.

By the exterior ODE and its nonzero Wronskian, write initially

  U=A xi_L(zr)+B zeta_L(zr).

Both branches are allowed at this stage. The exact raising recurrence is

  (L+1)w_L(t)-t w_L'(t)=t w_(L+1)(t)                       (2.15)

for either Riccati-Hankel family. Applying the accepted Cartesian outgoing
theorem to each Q_i and summing, equations (2.14)-(2.15) yield on (1,T)

  [lambda z/(2L+1)] [A xi_(L+1)(zr)+B zeta_(L+1)(zr)]
       = C xi_(L+1)(zr).

The functions have smooth ODE representatives; equality a.e. on an open
interval implies equality everywhere there. Their radial Wronskian is -2iz,
which is nonzero. Since lambda z/(2L+1)!=0, it follows that B=0. Hence

  U_+(r)=beta xi_L(zr).                                    (2.16)

One amplitude holds throughout the observation annulus. On overlapping
annuli it is unique because xi_L is not identically zero, so the amplitudes
agree. This extends to the whole exterior when desired.

This argument uses the accepted analytic outgoing selection at complex
frequency. It is valid when Im z<0. No asymptotic decay or scaled far-field
limit is used to reject an incoming solution.

For later sufficiency, the explicit Cartesian formula is also useful.
Writing P_i=partial_i P and using the same Q_i, for U=xi_L(zr),

  (E_TM)_i =
      (L z/r) xi_(L+1)(zr) Q_i(omega)
    + ((L+1)z/((2L+1)r)) xi_(L-1)(zr) P_i(omega).            (2.17)

It follows by substituting R=Q+grad P/(2L+1) and
G=(L+1)grad P/(2L+1)-LQ into (2.10), then using both adjacent-degree
recurrences. Thus the constructed outgoing physical TM block has outgoing
Cartesian scalar degrees L+1 and L-1. Multiplying by beta restores its
amplitude. The regular formula is identical with xi replaced by psi,
z replaced by nz, and an overall factor alpha/n^2.

2.8 The finite matching system, with every n and z factor

Substitute (2.13) and (2.16) into the actual weak trace conditions (2.9).
The radial derivatives are

  d/dr [alpha psi_L(nzr)] = alpha nz psi_L'(nzr),
  d/dr [beta xi_L(zr)] = beta z xi_L'(zr).

At r=1 the equations are exactly

  alpha psi_L(nz) - beta xi_L(z)=0,
  alpha (z/n) psi_L'(nz) - beta z xi_L'(z)=0.                (2.18)

The matching matrix is

  [ psi_L(nz)           -xi_L(z)       ]
  [ (z/n)psi_L'(nz)     -z xi_L'(z)    ].

Its determinant is

  -(z/n)[n psi_L(nz)xi_L'(z)-xi_L(z)psi_L'(nz)]
     =-(z/n)D_L(n,z).                                    (2.19)

Here n and z are nonzero. Dividing the second equation by z would instead
give determinant -D_L/n. Dividing the interior derivative by n^2 before
cancelling z is essential; the original radial derivative includes nz.

If D_L!=0 the two amplitudes vanish. If D_L=0 the matrix has rank exactly one:
its exterior column cannot vanish because xi_L and xi_L' cannot both vanish
at a nonzero argument, by (2.12). Its nullspace therefore has dimension one.
Moreover a nonzero null vector has alpha!=0 and beta!=0. If alpha=0, both
beta xi_L and beta xi_L' vanish, forcing beta=0. If beta=0 the same argument
uses the nonzero Wronskian of psi_L with eta_L at nz!=0.
No division by a possibly zero individual Bessel value has occurred.

2.9 Isolation, reconstruction, and the actual one-dimensional kernel

Apply the exact accepted theorem ModalTMIsolation.isolated_degree with

  l=1, n_complex=(n:C), z=z,
  IsAlgebraic Q (n:C),
  (n:C)!=0, (n:C)!=1, (n:C)!=-1, z!=0,
  tmDet 1 (n:C) z=0.

All three index exclusions follow from real n>1. Its conclusion is

  deriv(tmDet 1 (n:C)) z != 0,
  for every j in N, tmDet j (n:C) z=0 iff j=1.

Under (D), D_L(n,z)!=0 for every positive L other than 1. Thus (2.18)
eliminates every such physical degree and every azimuthal coefficient.
The radial degree zero has already vanished, and all toroidal coefficients
have vanished. Completeness in 2.2 leaves only the physical degree-one TM
field, with a constant vector of its three angular amplitudes.

Only one of those three amplitudes is allowed by the actual sector.
Indeed a degree-one scalar harmonic is v.omega for v in C^3. The radial
coefficient of the polar rotation-invariant E is an invariant scalar.
Its degree-one part therefore has v fixed by every rotation about axis 2.
Taking rotation angle pi gives v_0=v_1=0; v_2 is unrestricted. This argument
is an identity in the finite-dimensional coefficient space, so it does not
require a common pointwise representative for uncountably many rotations.
Equivalently average the angular action in L2 over the compact rotation group.

More generally the fixed scalar harmonic space in each degree has dimension
one: a rotation-invariant homogeneous polynomial has the form
sum_j c_j(x_0^2+x_1^2)^j x_2^(L-2j). Harmonicity gives

  4(j+1)^2 c_(j+1)+(L-2j)(L-2j-1)c_j=0,

so all coefficients are determined by c_0. The degree-one case is all that
the final rank-one conclusion needs.

It follows that for one fixed axial degree-one profile F_dip and every
genuine sector-kernel vector u there is one alpha_u with

  E[u]=alpha_u F_dip a.e. in B.

This is equality of actual volume fields: the countable full-measure
intersection and angular-to-volume reconstruction were justified in 2.2.
Under (W),

  u^phys=alpha_u s_n F_dip a.e. on ALL R^3.                 (2.20)

The global qualification is essential. It eliminates vectors invisible to
the material source outside B.

Now use the supplied accepted
ModalSphereVolumeKernel.numerator_in_kernel. At our n,z it gives N0!=0
and A_n(z)N0=0. The additionally inspected accepted
ModalMeridionalReflection.numerator_in_electricSector gives N0 in S.
Apply the preceding arbitrary-vector analysis to N0 itself:

  N0^phys=alpha_0 s_n F_dip.

Because N0!=0, alpha_0!=0. Hence every u in the sector kernel satisfies
u=(alpha_u/alpha_0)N0. Conversely every scalar multiple of N0 lies in the
sector and kernel by linearity. Uniqueness of its scalar follows from N0!=0.
This proves Theorem 04-A and its dimension statement.

Scalar simplicity was not used as a substitute for this geometric argument.
Noncoincidence eliminates degrees; the vector reconstruction, traces, sector
symmetry and weighted recovery prove the actual upper bound.

Full-space rotational degeneracy is compatible with the conclusion: before
restricting to the axial sector, the degree-one TM harmonic space has three
angular amplitudes. No claim that the full volume kernel has dimension one
has been made.

2.10 Sufficiency in both the physical and weighted-volume formulations

For any L>=1, z!=0, n>1 and nonzero null vector (alpha,beta) of (2.18), define
U by (2.13) inside and (2.16) outside, and define E,H by (2.10).
The exterior is taken only locally on finite observation balls; outgoing
growth in the lower half-plane is permitted.

The interior field is smooth at the origin. For example
psi_L(nzr)=r^(L+1)A(r^2) for an entire function A. If P is the harmonic
solid polynomial, then

  M_L[U]= -alpha A(|x|^2) (x cross grad P(x)),

which extends smoothly to x=0; N_L[U]=curl M_L[U] is smooth too.
In particular E=O(r^(L-1)) and H=O(r^L). The regional Maxwell equations
follow by substituting the ODE into the curl formulas (2.5).

The fields are locally L2 and smooth up to r=1 from each side. The matching
conditions give equal tangential E,H. Splitting integration by parts on the
two regions therefore cancels their surface terms; no interface delta occurs
in either curl. This proves the global weak Maxwell equations, not merely
regional ones. Also epsilon E_r=lambda U/r^2 is continuous and H_r=0,
so both normal constraints are satisfied. Equation (2.17) verifies the
analytically outgoing Cartesian convention.

To prove the weighted converse, put u^phys=s_n E. This is a compactly
supported L2 vector. Under (W), its actual source is

  rho=s_n u^phys=chi_n E.

Fix any T>1. Apply accepted actual_maxwell to this u without any kernel
assumption. Subtract its actual response pair E_T[u],H[u] from the
constructed physical pair. The difference F,G satisfies on B_T

  curl F=izG,        curl G=-izF.

Therefore div F=0 and (Delta+z^2)F=0 distributionally.

Here is a supporting smoothness proof including the origin. For a scalar
component w in L2_loc satisfying (Delta_3+z^2)w=0, define
v(x,t)=exp(zt)w(x) on B_T times an open real interval. Then Delta_4 v=0
distributionally. Mollifying v gives smooth harmonic functions on smaller
balls. For any smooth normalized radial kernel rho_R supported in such a
ball, the harmonic mean-value property gives v_delta=rho_R*v_delta.
Let delta tend to zero in local L2. Convolution with the fixed smooth kernel
converges with all derivatives on a still smaller ball by Cauchy-Schwarz,
so v=rho_R*v a.e. and has a smooth representative. The identity
partial_t v=zv then identifies v=exp(zt)v(x,0), giving a smooth representative
of w. This proves the required local Helmholtz regularity without a boundary
regularity assumption.

Every Cartesian scalar harmonic coefficient of this smooth F, multiplied
by r, solves the scalar Riccati equation and is regular at the origin.
Thus for every scalar degree d>=0 it equals c psi_d(zr) on (0,T).
Smoothness explicitly excludes the d=0 Neumann branch as well: its scalar
field behaves as 1/r, which mere local L2 would not have excluded.

On (1,T), the same coefficient is a multiple of xi_d(zr), because the
constructed E has that property by (2.17) and the actual response has it
by accepted OutgoingModeGluing.actual_exterior_moment and (O). The radial
Wronskian of psi_d(zr) and xi_d(zr) is iz!=0. Thus both coefficients are zero.
Scalar angular completeness gives F=0 throughout B_T a.e.; then G=0 by
curl F=izG. In particular E=E_1[u] inside the material ball.

Finally (W) gives A_n(z)u=u-s_n E_1[u]=0. This establishes sufficiency for
the concrete actual volume formulation once its defining dictionary is
provided. It does not assume a general Maxwell-to-volume representation
theorem as a substitute for the proof.

For the selected dipole specifically, the accepted theorem
weighted_mode_in_volume_kernel already supplies actual kernel membership
for every amplitude f. Its nontriviality/normalization theorems supply the
chosen nonzero vector. The converse above is nevertheless useful to make
the finite matching equivalence explicit.
