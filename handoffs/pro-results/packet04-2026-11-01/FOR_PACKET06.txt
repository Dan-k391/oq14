2.2 Angular integrability, completeness, and the correct degree bookkeeping

Choose a real orthonormal scalar spherical-harmonic basis Y_Lm for the supplied
angular measure. The real choice matters: the Packet 01 bilinear moment then
equals the corresponding Hermitian coefficient. Complex coefficients remain
allowed for the field. Write lambda_L=L(L+1), and define

  R_Lm=omega Y_Lm,
  G_Lm=grad_S Y_Lm,
  T_Lm=omega cross grad_S Y_Lm.

For L>=1 their squared norms are 1, lambda_L, lambda_L. The radial family
includes L=0; the two tangent families begin at L=1.

Integration by parts on the closed smooth sphere gives
integral grad Y.grad Z = -integral Y Delta_S Z, hence gradient orthogonality
and the norm lambda_L. Also div_S(omega cross grad_S Z)=0: in any oriented
local chart it is the cancellation of the two mixed second derivatives.
An integration by parts therefore gives integral grad Y.(omega cross grad Z)=0.
Rotation by omega cross is an isometry on each tangent plane. This proves
orthogonality of all the normalized R,G,T families.

Here is a finite-dimensional proof of completeness, rather than an assumed
Hodge decomposition. Let H_j denote homogeneous harmonic scalar polynomials
of degree j restricted to the sphere. Its dimension is 2j+1. One way to see
this and scalar polynomial completeness is the decomposition of homogeneous
polynomials into |x|^(2s)H_(j-2s). The calculation

  Delta(|x|^(2s) H_d)
    =2s(2d+2s+1)|x|^(2s-2)H_d

has a nonzero coefficient for s>=1, proving that decomposition inductively.
The dimension difference is
binomial(j+2,2)-binomial(j,2)=2j+1. Polynomial restrictions are dense in
continuous functions on the compact sphere because their real algebra
contains constants and separates points; continuous functions are dense in
L2 for the finite spherical measure. Alternatively the supplied accepted
scalar harmonic expansion gives this last scalar completeness directly.

For a harmonic polynomial P of degree L define vector polynomials

  H(P)=grad P,
  T(P)=x cross grad P,
  Q(P)=x P-|x|^2 grad P/(2L+1).

Their Cartesian components are harmonic of degrees L-1, L, L+1 respectively.
For Q this follows from

  Delta(x_i P)=2 partial_i P,
  Delta(|x|^2 partial_i P)=2(2L+1) partial_i P.

For T the extra second-derivative terms cancel by antisymmetry. On the sphere,

  H(P)=L R+G,
  T(P)=T,
  Q(P)=((L+1)R-G)/(2L+1).

Their squared norms, for ||Y||=1, are respectively
L(2L+1), L(L+1), and (L+1)/(2L+1). Thus H is injective for L>=1,
T for L>=1, and Q for L>=0.

For each scalar component degree j>=1, the space H_j tensor C^3 contains
the mutually orthogonal images

  H(H_(j+1)),     T(H_j),     Q(H_(j-1)).

Their dimensions sum to
(2j+3)+(2j+1)+(2j-1)=3(2j+1), the entire dimension. For j=0,
H(H_1) already has dimension 3 and fills the space. Therefore all vector
polynomial restrictions lie in the span of R,G,T. Scalar density applied
coordinatewise proves vector completeness.

This proves, for every vector angular L2 field F,

  F = sum a_Lm R_Lm + sum b_Lm G_Lm + sum t_Lm T_Lm

in angular L2, with

  a_Lm=integral F.R_Lm,
  b_Lm=lambda_L^(-1) integral F.G_Lm,
  t_Lm=lambda_L^(-1) integral F.T_Lm.

In particular there is no invisible radial or tangent remainder. This proof
also explains why physical electric vector degree L contains Cartesian scalar
degrees L-1 and L+1; toroidal degree L has Cartesian scalar degree L.
They must not be identified.

For actual volume fields, polar integration gives angular L2 slices for
dr-a.e. r>0. If Q_F(r)^2=integral |F(r omega)|^2 dmu, then

  |a_Lm(r)|<=Q_F(r),
  |b_Lm(r)|,|t_Lm(r)|<=Q_F(r)/sqrt(lambda_L).

rotation Q and for the meridional reflection S_ref.

For an orthogonal Q, the distributional identity is

  curl(U_Q F)=det(Q) U_Q(curl F).                              (2.3)

For smooth F it follows from the chain rule and
(Qa) cross (Qb)=det(Q) Q(a cross b). For a locally L2 field the same identity
follows by changing variables in a compact smooth test; the Jacobian has
absolute value one. Alternatively mollify on smaller invariant balls and
pass to distributions. All test products are integrable.

Use curl E=izH and z!=0 in (2.3). The actual H transforms as an axial vector:
U_Q H=H for rotations and U_S_ref H=-H for the reflection. Consequently
f(x)=x.H(x) is rotation-invariant and reflection-odd as an L2(B) scalar.

The a.e. quantifier issue is resolved as follows. Extend f by zero outside
the invariant ball and convolve with a smooth compactly supported radial
approximate identity rho_delta. For each fixed Q, a change of variables
transfers the L2 symmetry to the smooth function f_delta pointwise. Thus the
smooth f_delta has all these symmetries at every point. For every x,
S_ref x is on the same axial rotation orbit as x, including the axis itself.
It follows that

  f_delta(S_ref x)=f_delta(x)=-f_delta(x),

so f_delta=0. Approximate-identity convergence in L2 gives

  x.H(x)=0 a.e. in B.                                         (2.4)

This does not choose an x-dependent rotation within an a.e. identity.
Approximate-identity convergence follows, for example, from continuity of
translations in L2, first for continuous compact functions and then by density.

To eliminate the electric toroidal coefficient, fix Y=Y_Lm, L>=1, put
T=omega cross grad_S Y, and test curl E=izH with eta(r)Y(omega)omega,
where eta is smooth and compactly supported in (0,1). Since

  curl(eta R)=-(eta/r)T,

the weak equation reads

  -integral r eta(r) integral E(r omega).T(omega) dmu dr
    = iz integral r^2 eta(r) integral H(r omega).omega Y dmu dr.

The right side is zero by (2.4). Test-annihilation for the locally integrable
left coefficient gives t_Lm=0 a.e. The radial and gradient projections of
curl E=izH then also make the radial and gradient coefficients of H zero.
Thus the interior field has only the physical TM blocks. The electric radial
L=0 coefficient is separately zero by reviewed Packet 01 Theorem D.

2.4 Global modal equations and one-dimensional H1 regularity

Fix L>=1 and a normalized real Y. Write lambda=L(L+1) and R,G,T as above.
For a compact radial test eta away from the origin,

  curl(eta R)=-(eta/r)T,
  curl(eta G)=((r eta)'/r)T,
  curl(eta T)=-(lambda eta/r)R-((r eta)'/r)G.                   (2.5)

These identities follow by differentiating on spherical-coordinate charts
and using Delta_S Y=-lambda Y. Each side is smooth on the punctured space,
so the chart identities agree at chart boundaries. Only these finite smooth
tests are differentiated; no infinite L2 series is differentiated.

Define actual moments

  a(r)=integral E.R,       b(r)=lambda^(-1) integral E.G,
  h(r)=lambda^(-1) integral H.T.

Apply curl H=-iz epsilon E to eta R and eta G, and curl E=izH to eta T.
Polar integration gives, respectively,

  -lambda integral r h eta = -iz integral epsilon r^2 a eta,
  lambda integral r h (r eta)' =
       -iz lambda integral epsilon r^2 b eta,
  -lambda integral [r a eta+r b(r eta)'] =
       iz lambda integral r^2 h eta.

Let

  U(r)=r h(r)/(iz)=epsilon(r) r^2 a(r)/lambda,
  V(r)=r b(r).

Because phi=r eta ranges over all compact smooth tests on a positive-radius
collar, the preceding identities are exactly

  U'=epsilon V,
  V'=(lambda/(epsilon r^2)-z^2)U                              (2.6)

in distributions, including across r=1. These TM equations hold even before
any TE block has been eliminated.

Let J=(r0,r1), with 0<r0<1<r1<T, and let shell(J) be its spherical shell.
Angular Cauchy-Schwarz and the polar Jacobian give the explicit estimates

  ||U||_L2(J) <= ||H||_L2(shell(J))/(|z| sqrt(lambda)),
  ||V||_L2(J) <= ||E||_L2(shell(J))/sqrt(lambda),
  ||U'||_L2(J) <= n^2 ||E||_L2(shell(J))/sqrt(lambda),
  ||V'||_L2(J) <= (lambda/r0^2+|z|^2)||U||_L2(J).             (2.7)

Thus U,V have H1 representatives on a collar crossing the interface.
For completeness, a locally integrable function with weak derivative g in L2
equals a constant plus the indefinite integral of g: subtract that integral;
the difference has distributional derivative zero. Testing against derivatives
of compact functions shows it annihilates every compact test of integral zero,
so it is a constant distribution. This proves the absolutely continuous
representative assertion without assuming classical differentiability of the
original moment.

Both representatives are continuous at 1. On either constant-material side,
(2.6) bootstraps to smoothness and gives

  U''+(epsilon z^2-lambda/r^2)U=0.                            (2.8)

Consequently the interface conditions are

  U(1-)=U(1+),
  U'(1-)/n^2=U'(1+).                                        (2.9)

The trace estimate

  |f(1)| <= |J|^(-1/2)||f||_2+|J|^(1/2)||f'||_2

follows by integrating f(1)=f(t)+integral_t^1 f' over t. The constants in (2.7)
and this estimate state all dependence needed for the finite-mode proof.
No estimate uniform in L or z is asserted.

For the TM block the physical pair is

  E_TM= lambda U/(epsilon r^2) R + U'/(epsilon r) G,
  H_TM= iz U/r T.                                           (2.10)

Equivalently M_L[U]=-(U/r)T and
N_L[U]=curl M_L[U]=lambda U R/r^2+U'G/r, so
E_TM=epsilon^(-1)N_L[U], H_TM=-iz M_L[U].
This agrees with the convention in the quoted RiccatiTransmission source.

2.5 These are actual weak tangential traces; exterior TE cannot remain

For F in H(curl) on one side of a smooth spherical boundary, define its
distributional tangential trace, on a smooth boundary test phi, by

  <nu cross F,phi>
     = integral_D [curl F.Phi-F.curl Phi],

where Phi is a smooth extension with any other boundary component suppressed.
This is independent of the extension. Indeed, an extension difference zero on
the boundary is O(distance). Cutting it off in a collar of width delta gives
interior compact tests. The cutoff derivative is O(1/delta), but its product
with the extension difference is bounded in that collar. The error is bounded
by a fixed smooth-test constant times the L2 norms of F,curl F in a shrinking
collar times its volume^(1/2), and tends to zero. This also proves continuity
as a distribution on smooth boundary tests.

Use the same outward-from-the-ball normal on both sides. Applying the global
weak curl equations to a test crossing the interface, and splitting its volume
integral into the two regions, gives

  nu cross E_- = nu cross E_+,
  nu cross H_- = nu cross H_+

as tangential trace distributions. Their fixed harmonic coefficients are
precisely the scalar H1 traces above: E_TM,t=V G/r and H_TM,t=iz U T/r.
Thus (2.9) is derived from actual weak Maxwell fields, not imposed on unknown
classical boundary values of their infinite angular sum.

Only interior magnetic-scalar vanishing was used. To rule out an exterior
TE remainder, let t=lambda^(-1) integral E.T, let d=integral H.R and
e=lambda^(-1) integral H.G, and set K=-r t. The other three projections of
the same global weak equations give

  d=lambda K/(iz r^2),   K'=iz r e,
  (r e)'=d+iz epsilon K.

Hence

  K''+(epsilon z^2-lambda/r^2)K=0                            (2.11)

distributionally on the collar. Its coefficients are bounded there and the
moments are L2. It follows that K and K' have H1 representatives, so both are
continuous across 1. Interior t=0 implies K(1)=K'(1)=0. On the exterior,
the ordinary linear ODE has unique solutions for specified Cauchy data; thus
K=0. Uniqueness follows directly by writing the first-order integral equation
and applying the elementary Gronwall inequality on each compact interval.
This eliminates the entire exterior TE block. No exterior symmetry theorem
and no far-field condition are needed for this step.

For completeness, exterior radial monopoles cannot remain either. Take the
constant normalized harmonic Y_00 and test curl H=-iz epsilon E with eta R_00
on any positive-radius annulus. Its curl is zero, so
0=-iz integral epsilon r^2 eta a_00 for every such test. Since z!=0 and
epsilon>0, test-annihilation gives a_00=0. Testing curl E=izH in the same way
kills the magnetic radial monopole. This extends the already reviewed
interior electric monopole conclusion to the exterior, without applying
Packet 01 beyond its stated domain.

2.6 Regular interior profile and the Packet 01 normalization

For a real normalized Y coming from P, reviewed Packet 01 gives

  r^2 a=c regular(L,nz,r),
  lambda b=B_P=c regularD(L,nz,r)/r.

Thus the same c gives U=n^2 c regular/lambda and the associated poloidal
coefficient; no independent tangent amplitude is left. In particular this
does not differentiate an arbitrary L2 representative.

To identify the span with the standard psi_L without assuming an unquoted
normalization of regular, use the newly derived ODE (2.8). Define

  psi_L(t)=t j_L(t),    eta_L(t)=t y_L(t),
  xi_L=psi_L+i eta_L,   zeta_L=psi_L-i eta_L.

They solve w''+(1-lambda/t^2)w=0 away from zero. The regular series is

  psi_L(t)=sum_(p>=0)
      (-1)^p t^(L+2p+1)/(2^p p! (2L+2p+1)!!).

It converges on compact subsets with all derivatives, and direct substitution
checks the ODE. The singular solution can be obtained from eta_0=-cos t,
eta_1=-cos t/t-sin t and the adjacent-degree recurrence. Its leading behavior
is

  psi_L(t)~t^(L+1)/(2L+1)!!,
  eta_L(t)~-(2L-1)!! t^(-L).

The ODE makes their Wronskian constant; these leading terms give

  psi_L eta_L'-psi_L' eta_L=1,
  W(xi_L,zeta_L)=-2i,       W(psi_L,xi_L)=i.                 (2.12)

Here and below primes on special functions are argument derivatives.
These conventions and recurrences agree with NIST DLMF 10.47, 10.50, 10.51;
the series and Wronskian calculation above give the needed normalization.

Since nz!=0, the two radial solutions psi_L(nzr), eta_L(nzr) have nonzero
radial Wronskian nz. By ordinary ODE uniqueness, every interior profile is
a linear combination of them on the connected interval (0,1).

Actual L2 integrability and a=lambda U/(n^2 r^2) give

  integral_0^1 |U(r)|^2/r^2 dr
    <= n^4/lambda^2 ||E||_L2(B)^2 < infinity.

A nonzero eta_L coefficient would have leading magnitude a nonzero constant
times r^(-L), and this integral would diverge for L>=1. Therefore

  U_-(r)=alpha psi_L(nzr).                                  (2.13)

No exact value for the proportionality between regular and psi_L is needed.
There is no missing regular-normalization prerequisite in this mathematical
route. Degree zero is excluded separately by Packet 01 Theorem D.

2.7 Analytically outgoing physical vector degrees

The accepted outgoing theorem is a Cartesian scalar theorem. In its exact

  r integral E_T[u](r omega)_i p(omega) dmu

for almost every r in (1,T). It requires no kernel hypothesis. Both the

accepted in the supplied packet. Under (O) this is a multiple of xi_d(zr).

Fix the physical degree L>=1 and its real polynomial P. Define

  Q_i(x)=x_i P(x)-|x|^2 partial_i P(x)/(2L+1).

Every Q_i is homogeneous harmonic of scalar degree L+1, as proved in 2.2.
On the sphere, the vector Q=((L+1)R-G)/(2L+1). Consequently, for the actual

  integral E.Q dmu
      =((L+1)a-lambda b)/(2L+1)
      =lambda[(L+1)U-rU']/((2L+1)r^2)                       (2.14)

in the exterior, where epsilon=1. Only finitely many actual component

physical vector degree L has been made.

By the exterior ODE and its nonzero Wronskian, write initially

  U=A xi_L(zr)+B zeta_L(zr).

Both branches are allowed at this stage. The exact raising recurrence is

  (L+1)w_L(t)-t w_L'(t)=t w_(L+1)(t)                       (2.15)

for either Riccati-Hankel family. Applying the accepted Cartesian outgoing
theorem to each Q_i and summing, equations (2.14)-(2.15) yield on (1,T)

  [lambda z/(2L+1)] [A xi_(L+1)(zr)+B zeta_(L+1)(zr)]
       = C xi_(L+1)(zr).

The functions have smooth ODE representatives; equality a.e. on an open

which is nonzero. Since lambda z/(2L+1)!=0, it follows that B=0. Hence

  U_+(r)=beta xi_L(zr).                                    (2.16)

One amplitude holds throughout the observation annulus. On overlapping
annuli it is unique because xi_L is not identically zero, so the amplitudes
agree. This extends to the whole exterior when desired.

This argument uses the accepted analytic outgoing selection at complex
frequency. It is valid when Im z<0. No asymptotic decay or scaled far-field
limit is used to reject an incoming solution.

For later sufficiency, the explicit Cartesian formula is also useful.

  (E_TM)_i =
      (L z/r) xi_(L+1)(zr) Q_i(omega)
    + ((L+1)z/((2L+1)r)) xi_(L-1)(zr) P_i(omega).            (2.17)

It follows by substituting R=Q+grad P/(2L+1) and
G=(L+1)grad P/(2L+1)-LQ into (2.10), then using both adjacent-degree

2.8 The finite matching system, with every n and z factor

Substitute (2.13) and (2.16) into the actual weak trace conditions (2.9).
The radial derivatives are

  d/dr [alpha psi_L(nzr)] = alpha nz psi_L'(nzr),
  d/dr [beta xi_L(zr)] = beta z xi_L'(zr).

At r=1 the equations are exactly

  alpha psi_L(nz) - beta xi_L(z)=0,
  alpha (z/n) psi_L'(nz) - beta z xi_L'(z)=0.                (2.18)

The matching matrix is

  [ psi_L(nz)           -xi_L(z)       ]
  [ (z/n)psi_L'(nz)     -z xi_L'(z)    ].

Its determinant is

  -(z/n)[n psi_L(nz)xi_L'(z)-xi_L(z)psi_L'(nz)]
     =-(z/n)D_L(n,z).                                    (2.19)

Here n and z are nonzero. Dividing the second equation by z would instead
give determinant -D_L/n. Dividing the interior derivative by n^2 before
cancelling z is essential; the original radial derivative includes nz.

If D_L!=0 the two amplitudes vanish. If D_L=0 the matrix has rank exactly one:
its exterior column cannot vanish because xi_L and xi_L' cannot both vanish
at a nonzero argument, by (2.12). Its nullspace therefore has dimension one.
Moreover a nonzero null vector has alpha!=0 and beta!=0. If alpha=0, both
beta xi_L and beta xi_L' vanish, forcing beta=0. If beta=0 the same argument
uses the nonzero Wronskian of psi_L with eta_L at nz!=0.
No division by a possibly zero individual Bessel value has occurred.

2.9 Isolation, reconstruction, and the actual one-dimensional kernel

Apply the exact accepted theorem ModalTMIsolation.isolated_degree with

  l=1, n_complex=(n:C), z=z,

  (n:C)!=0, (n:C)!=1, (n:C)!=-1, z!=0,
  tmDet 1 (n:C) z=0.

All three index exclusions follow from real n>1. Its conclusion is

  deriv(tmDet 1 (n:C)) z != 0,
  for every j in N, tmDet j (n:C) z=0 iff j=1.

Under (D), D_L(n,z)!=0 for every positive L other than 1. Thus (2.18)
eliminates every such physical degree and every azimuthal coefficient.
The radial degree zero has already vanished, and all toroidal coefficients
have vanished. Completeness in 2.2 leaves only the physical degree-one TM
field, with a constant vector of its three angular amplitudes.

Only one of those three amplitudes is allowed by the actual sector.
Indeed a degree-one scalar harmonic is v.omega for v in C^3. The radial
coefficient of the polar rotation-invariant E is an invariant scalar.
Its degree-one part therefore has v fixed by every rotation about axis 2.
Taking rotation angle pi gives v_0=v_1=0; v_2 is unrestricted. This argument
is an identity in the finite-dimensional coefficient space, so it does not
require a common pointwise representative for uncountably many rotations.
Equivalently average the angular action in L2 over the compact rotation group.

More generally the fixed scalar harmonic space in each degree has dimension
one: a rotation-invariant homogeneous polynomial has the form
sum_j c_j(x_0^2+x_1^2)^j x_2^(L-2j). Harmonicity gives

  4(j+1)^2 c_(j+1)+(L-2j)(L-2j-1)c_j=0,

so all coefficients are determined by c_0. The degree-one case is all that
the final rank-one conclusion needs.

It follows that for one fixed axial degree-one profile F_dip and every
genuine sector-kernel vector u there is one alpha_u with

  E[u]=alpha_u F_dip a.e. in B.

intersection and angular-to-volume reconstruction were justified in 2.2.

  u^phys=alpha_u s_n F_dip a.e. on ALL R^3.                 (2.20)

The global qualification is essential. It eliminates vectors invisible to

2.10 Sufficiency in both the physical and weighted-volume formulations

    (K : Set Space) (hK : IsCompact K)

theorem response_actual_spatial_pairing (c : Coefficient) (u : VectorL2)
    (K : Set Space) (hK : IsCompact K)
    (hc : ∀ x : Space, x ∉ K → c.value (VectorFourier.coordinateMap x) = 0)
    (g : 𝓢(Space, ℂ)) (i : Fin 3) :
    (∫ x, response c u (VectorFourier.coordinateMap x) i * g x) =
      ∑ j, ∫ y, ((Real.sqrt (c.value (VectorFourier.coordinateMap y)) : ℂ) *
        u (VectorFourier.coordinateMap y) j) *
        ∫ x, GreenKernel.phi 0 (x - y) *

  have h := NewtonL1L2.newtonOperator_spatial_pairing

  calc
    _ = ∫ x, NewtonL2.physicalCoordinate

      apply integral_congr_ae
      filter_upwards [NewtonL2.physicalCoordinate_ae
        (NewtonL2.newtonOperator (StaticCoefficient.sqrtMultiplier c u)) i] with x hx
      rw [hx]
      rfl

      apply Finset.sum_congr rfl

theorem coefficient_value (n : ℝ) (hn : 1 < n) (x : Space) :
    (coefficient n hn).value (VectorFourier.coordinateMap x) =
      if ‖x‖ < 1 then n ^ 2 - 1 else 0 := by
  simp only [coefficient, StaticSphereMaterial.coefficient_value, mem_ball, dist_zero_right]

theorem coefficient_support (n : ℝ) (hn : 1 < n) (x : Space)
    (hx : x ∉ closedBall (0 : Space) 1) :
    (coefficient n hn).value (VectorFourier.coordinateMap x) = 0 := by
  have h : ¬ ‖x‖ < 1 := by
    intro hlt
    apply hx
    simpa only [mem_closedBall, dist_zero_right] using hlt.le
  rw [coefficient_value, if_neg h]

    ring
  · have ho : ¬ ‖x‖ ≤ 1 := fun h => hi (lt_of_le_of_ne h hx.2)

    (ha : (residue 1 (n : ℂ) k.1 f).1 ≠ 0) : weightedMode n hn k f ≠ 0 := by
  intro hzero
  have he := weightedMode_inner n hn k f 2
  have haez : (fun x : Space => (Real.sqrt (n ^ 2 - 1) : ℂ) *
      entireInnerE (n : ℂ) k.1 (residue 1 (n : ℂ) k.1 f).1 x 2)
      =ᵐ[volume.restrict (ball (0 : Space) 1)] fun _ => (0 : ℂ) := by
    have hz := VectorFourier.coordinateMap_preserving.quasiMeasurePreserving.ae
      (Lp.coeFn_zero VectorFourier.Vector 2 (volume : Measure VectorFourier.Frequency))
    filter_upwards [he, ae_restrict_of_ae hz] with x hx hz
    rw [hzero] at hx
    rw [hz] at hx
    simpa only [PiLp.zero_apply] using hx.symm
  have hc : Continuous (fun x : Space => (Real.sqrt (n ^ 2 - 1) : ℂ) *
      entireInnerE (n : ℂ) k.1 (residue 1 (n : ℂ) k.1 f).1 x 2) :=
    continuous_const.mul (continuous_const.mul (centerE_continuous ((n : ℂ) * k.1) 2))
  have heq := Measure.eqOn_open_of_ae_eq haez isOpen_ball hc.continuousOn
    continuous_const.continuousOn
  have hz := heq (show (0 : Space) ∈ ball (0 : Space) 1 by simp)
  have hn0 : (n : ℂ) ≠ 0 := Complex.ofReal_ne_zero.mpr (by linarith)
  have hs : (Real.sqrt (n ^ 2 - 1) : ℂ) ≠ 0 :=
    Complex.ofReal_ne_zero.mpr (ne_of_gt (Real.sqrt_pos.2 (contrast_pos n hn)))

  simpa only [entireInnerE, Pi.smul_apply, smul_eq_mul, centerE_zero,
    Matrix.cons_val_two, Matrix.cons_val_one, Matrix.cons_val_zero] using hz

theorem ode_wronskian_hasDerivAt (f df u du q : ℝ → ℂ) (r : ℝ)
    (hf : HasDerivAt f (df r) r) (hdf : HasDerivAt df (q r*f r) r)
    (hu : HasDerivAt u (du r) r) (hdu : HasDerivAt du (q r*u r) r) :
    HasDerivAt (fun t => f t*du t-u t*df t) 0 r := by
  convert (hf.mul hdu).sub (hu.mul hdf) using 1
  ring

theorem ode_two_solution_expansion (R : ℝ) (f df u du v dv q : ℝ → ℂ) (W : ℂ)
    (hW : W ≠ 0)
    (hf : ∀ r, R < r → HasDerivAt f (df r) r)
    (hdf : ∀ r, R < r → HasDerivAt df (q r*f r) r)
    (hu : ∀ r, R < r → HasDerivAt u (du r) r)
    (hdu : ∀ r, R < r → HasDerivAt du (q r*u r) r)
    (hv : ∀ r, R < r → HasDerivAt v (dv r) r)
    (hdv : ∀ r, R < r → HasDerivAt dv (q r*v r) r)
    (hwr : ∀ r, R < r → u r*dv r-v r*du r = W) :
    ∃ a b : ℂ, ∀ r, R < r → f r = a*u r+b*v r := by
  let ac : ℝ → ℂ := fun r => (f r*dv r-v r*df r)/W
  let bc : ℝ → ℂ := fun r => (u r*df r-f r*du r)/W
  have ha (r : ℝ) (hr : R < r) : HasDerivAt ac 0 r := by
    simpa only [zero_div] using
      (ode_wronskian_hasDerivAt f df v dv q r (hf r hr) (hdf r hr) (hv r hr) (hdv r hr)).div_const W
  have hb (r : ℝ) (hr : R < r) : HasDerivAt bc 0 r := by
    simpa only [zero_div] using
      (ode_wronskian_hasDerivAt u du f df q r (hu r hr) (hdu r hr) (hf r hr) (hdf r hr)).div_const W
  obtain ⟨a,hae⟩ := isOpen_Ioi.exists_is_const_of_deriv_eq_zero (convex_Ioi R).isPreconnected
    (fun r hr => (ha r hr).differentiableAt.differentiableWithinAt)
    (fun r hr => (ha r hr).deriv)
  obtain ⟨b,hbe⟩ := isOpen_Ioi.exists_is_const_of_deriv_eq_zero (convex_Ioi R).isPreconnected
    (fun r hr => (hb r hr).differentiableAt.differentiableWithinAt)
    (fun r hr => (hb r hr).deriv)
  refine ⟨a,b,?_⟩
  intro r hr
  rw [← hae r hr, ← hbe r hr]
  dsimp only [ac,bc]
  field_simp [hW]
  linear_combination -f r*(hwr r hr)

def outgoing (l : ℕ) (k : ℂ) (r : ℝ) : ℂ := xi l (k*r)
def outgoingD (l : ℕ) (k : ℂ) (r : ℝ) : ℂ := k*xiD l (k*r)
def incoming (l : ℕ) (k : ℂ) (r : ℝ) : ℂ := xiMinus l (k*r)
def incomingD (l : ℕ) (k : ℂ) (r : ℝ) : ℂ := k*xiMinusD l (k*r)
def radialCoefficient (l : ℕ) (k : ℂ) (r : ℝ) : ℂ :=
  (l : ℂ)*(l+1)/(r : ℂ)^2-k^2

def hankelCoeff (l s : ℕ) (phase : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  phase ^ s * (Nat.factorial (l + s) : ℂ) /
    (2 ^ s * (Nat.factorial s : ℂ) * (Nat.factorial (l - s) : ℂ))

def hankelPoly (l : ℕ) (phase z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  ∑ s ∈ Finset.range (l + 1), hankelCoeff l s phase * z ^ (-(s : ℤ))

def hankelPolyD (l : ℕ) (phase z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  ∑ s ∈ Finset.range (l + 1),
    hankelCoeff l s phase * ((-(s : ℤ) : ℂ) * z ^ (-(s : ℤ) - 1))

def hankelPolyDD (l : ℕ) (phase z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  ∑ s ∈ Finset.range (l + 1),
    hankelCoeff l s phase * ((-(s : ℤ) : ℂ) *
      (((-(s : ℤ) - 1 : ℤ) : ℂ) * z ^ (-(s : ℤ) - 1 - 1)))

theorem hankelPoly_hasDerivAt (l : ℕ) (phase z : ℂ) (hz : z ≠ 0) :
    HasDerivAt (hankelPoly l phase) (hankelPolyD l phase z) z := by
  apply HasDerivAt.sum
  intro s _
  simpa using (hasDerivAt_zpow (-(s : ℤ)) z (Or.inl hz)).const_mul
    (hankelCoeff l s phase)

theorem hankelPolyD_hasDerivAt (l : ℕ) (phase z : ℂ) (hz : z ≠ 0) :
    HasDerivAt (hankelPolyD l phase) (hankelPolyDD l phase z) z := by
  apply HasDerivAt.sum
  intro s _
  simpa using
    ((hasDerivAt_zpow (-(s : ℤ) - 1) z (Or.inl hz)).const_mul
      (-(s : ℤ) : ℂ)).const_mul (hankelCoeff l s phase)

def p (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ := hankelPoly l I z
def pD (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ := hankelPolyD l I z
def pMinus (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ := hankelPoly l (-I) z
def pMinusD (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ := hankelPolyD l (-I) z

def xi (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ := (-I) ^ (l + 1) * exp (I * z) * p l z
def xiD (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  (-I) ^ (l + 1) * exp (I * z) * (I * p l z + pD l z)
def xiMinus (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  I ^ (l + 1) * exp (-I * z) * pMinus l z
def xiMinusD (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  I ^ (l + 1) * exp (-I * z) * (-I * pMinus l z + pMinusD l z)

theorem xi_hasDerivAt (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) (hz : z ≠ 0) :
    HasDerivAt (xi l) (xiD l z) z := by
  have he : HasDerivAt (fun w : ℂ => exp (I * w)) (exp (I * z) * I) z :=
    by simpa using ((hasDerivAt_id z).const_mul I).cexp
  have hp := hankelPoly_hasDerivAt l I z hz
  convert (he.const_mul ((-I) ^ (l + 1))).mul hp using 1 <;>
    simp only [xi, xiD, p, pD] <;> ring

theorem xiMinus_hasDerivAt (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) (hz : z ≠ 0) :
    HasDerivAt (xiMinus l) (xiMinusD l z) z := by
  have he : HasDerivAt (fun w : ℂ => exp (-I * w)) (exp (-I * z) * (-I)) z :=
    by simpa using ((hasDerivAt_id z).const_mul (-I)).cexp
  have hp := hankelPoly_hasDerivAt l (-I) z hz
  convert (he.const_mul (I ^ (l + 1))).mul hp using 1 <;>
    simp only [xiMinus, xiMinusD, pMinus, pMinusD] <;> ring

def psi (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ := (xi l z + xiMinus l z) / 2
def psiD (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ := (xiD l z + xiMinusD l z) / 2

theorem psi_hasDerivAt (l : ℕ) (z : ℂ) (hz : z ≠ 0) :
    HasDerivAt (psi l) (psiD l z) z := by
  exact ((xi_hasDerivAt l z hz).add (xiMinus_hasDerivAt l z hz)).div_const 2

def tmDet (l : ℕ) (n z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  n * psi l (n * z) * xiD l z - xi l z * psiD l (n * z)

theorem poly_coeff_raising (l s : ℕ) (phase : ℂ) :

(A4) The original, unmodified componentwise field (1) is zero at EVERY axis point, including the origin. It is smooth on |x|>1; its rotation, reflection, and radial-zero identities hold pointwise there. No assertion of all-point interior absolute convergence is made.

(A5) For EVERY z in C and EVERY r>0, the original field omega -> H_z[rho](r omega) belongs to L^1(S^2,d sigma), and

  omega dot H_z[rho](r omega) = 0 for sigma-almost every omega.

Consequently, for every bounded measurable angular weight b,

  integral_{S^2} [omega dot H_z[rho](r omega)] b(omega) d sigma = 0.       (3)

Under (I3), in the packet's EXACT bilinear, unconjugated convention,

  for all r>0 and all p : HarmonicPolynomial.Poly,
    ComplexMagneticDebye.radialCoefficient n hn z u p r = 0.             (4)

No homogeneity or harmonicity assumption on p is needed for (4). These are radial magnetic moments, not all magnetic vector moments. Toroidal magnetic moments can be nonzero.

Neither z!=0 nor `volumeBlock c_n z 1 u = 0` is assumed in Theorem A. At z=0 the actual magnetic field is zero by its prefactor. A kernel equation becomes relevant only for subsequent homogeneous interior Maxwell reconstruction.

### 1.4 Explicit constants

The proof applies more generally to any L^2 source supported in the closed ball of radius a>0, satisfying the source symmetry laws derived below. For R>=0 and L=R+a, set

  C_z(L) = exp(|z| L) [L + |z| L^2/2],
  B_z(L) = exp(|z| L) [1+|z| L]/(4 pi).

Then

  ||H_z[rho]||_{L^2(B_R)} <= |z| C_z(R+a) ||rho||_2.                    (5)

For each r>0,

  integral_{S^2} |H_z[rho](r omega)|_2 d sigma
    <= 2 sqrt(pi) |z| exp(|z|(r+a)) [1+|z|(r+a)]
       sqrt(r+a)/r ||rho||_2.                                          (6)

For the sphere source a=1,

  ||rho||_2 <= sqrt(n^2-1) ||U||_2,
  ||rho||_1 <= sqrt(4 pi/3) sqrt(n^2-1) ||U||_2.

There is no material-frequency restriction beyond n>1 for this family. Bounds (5)-(6) are local and do not assert that the outgoing kernel or field is globally integrable at complex frequency.

## 2. Complete numbered proof

### Step 1. Measurable representatives and the actual scalar integrals

Choose a Borel representative of rho, zero outside its support ball. This is permitted for a finite-dimensional Lebesgue-measurable L^2 function: replace each set in a countable sequence of measurable simple approximants by a Borel set equal to it modulo a null set, and take the almost-everywhere limit, set to zero on the remaining null set. Finite-dimensional coordinate measurability suffices.

This choice DOES NOT CHANGE (1) AT ANY OBSERVATION POINT. For a fixed x and a fixed pair of indices, the old and new integrands differ only where the source representatives differ. Integral congruence almost everywhere therefore gives exactly the same scalar integral, whether or not it is integrable. The same statement holds for every nonnegative majorant introduced below. Fixed-Q source symmetry is preserved, since orthogonal transformations preserve null sets.

All parameter-integral functions used below can consequently be taken Borel. For clarity, if f(x,y) is one of the scalar integrands, its absolute integral is a measurable nonnegative parameter integral by Tonelli. Its totalized complex integral is measurable as well: on the measurable set where the absolute integral is finite, it is the limit of integrals of f 1_{|f|<=N} over the finite source ball; those bounded parameter integrals are measurable. On the complement the totalized scalar integral is zero. This also justifies restriction of the actual field to an individual sphere; an arbitrary volume-a.e. representative alone would not justify such a restriction.

### Step 2. Derive the source covariance from the actual electric sector

The accepted `ModalAxialRotation.action_ae`, together with `matrix_vector`, says for each fixed theta,

  U_{A_theta u}(y) = R_theta U_u(R_{-theta} y) for almost every y.        (7)

The accepted `material_invariant` identifies the material weight at y and R_{-theta}y. Multiply (7) by the scalar square-root weight in (I1), and move this scalar through the finite coordinate sum. This proves the NEW source-action identity

  rho_{A_theta u}(y) = R_theta rho_u(R_{-theta} y) for almost every y.     (8)

Membership in axialSector is precisely A_theta u=u as an L^2 vector for every theta. Equality of these L^2 objects, followed by the measure-preserving substitution y=R_theta v in (8), yields

  for every theta, rho(R_theta v)=R_theta rho(v) for almost every v.      (9)

For reflection, the accepted `ModalMeridionalStatic.source_action` gives

  rho_{A_S u}(y)=S rho_u(Sy) for almost every y.

The reflection-fixed part of electricSector gives A_S u=u. Substitution by S, or multiplication by S and use of S^2=I, proves

  rho(Sv)=S rho(v) for almost every v.                                  (10)

The null set in (9) MAY depend on theta. No common pointwise source representative has been assumed or obtained here.

By (I1), rho is L^2 and supported in the unit ball. Cauchy-Schwarz on that finite-volume ball proves rho is L^1 and gives the material-dependent bounds in Section 1.4. This verifies all analytic source assumptions, rather than postulating them for a new abstract magnetic field.

### Step 3. Kernel covariance and orientation

For any real orthogonal Q, |Qv|=|v|. The explicit radial formula therefore gives

  g_z(Qv)=Q g_z(v) for every v, including v=0.                          (11)

Real orthogonal Q is unitary for the Hermitian Euclidean norm, so |Qg|_2=|g|_2. The cross-product identity is

  (Qv) cross (Qw) = det(Q) Q(v cross w).                               (12)

One proof is to pair both sides with Qa using the bilinear dot product. The scalar triple product acquires det(Q), while the dot product is preserved by Q^T Q=I. Nondegeneracy gives (12) for real vectors, and complex bilinearity extends it to complex vectors. In particular det(R_theta)=1 and det(S)=-1. Reflection does not conjugate z or the source.

Expanding coordinates also gives

  |v cross w|_2^2 = |v|_2^2 |w|_2^2 - |sum_j v_j conjugate(w_j)|^2,

so |v cross w|_2 <= |v|_2 |w|_2. This supplies the norm inequality used below.

### Step 4. One absolute-convergence set for all complex frequencies

Define b(v)=|v|^{-2} for v!=0 and b(0)=0. Define an EXTENDED nonnegative integral

  W_rho(x) = integral b(x-y) |rho(y)|_2 dy in [0,infinity],
  D_rho = {x : W_rho(x)<infinity}.                                     (13)

This majorant does not depend on z. Nonnegative integration is used here; infinity is never silently replaced by Lean's totalized real integral zero.

For |x|<=R and |y|<=a, x-y lies in B_{R+a}. Polar integration gives

  integral_{B_L} b(v) dv = 4 pi L.

Tonelli and translation invariance, with L=R+a, imply

  integral_{B_R} W_rho(x) dx <= 4 pi (R+a) ||rho||_1 < infinity.         (14)

Thus W_rho is finite almost everywhere in each integer-radius ball, and the countable union of these balls proves volume(D_rho^c)=0. Its Borel measurability follows from Step 1 and Tonelli.

For 0<|v|<=L,

  |g_z(v)|_2 <= exp(|z|L)/(4 pi) [|z|/|v|+1/|v|^2]
              <= B_z(L) b(v).                                         (15)

At zero both sides are zero. For a fixed x choose L=|x|+a>0. It follows that, whenever x is in D_rho, all nine scalar functions

  y -> g_z,j(x-y) rho_l(y), j,l in {0,1,2},

are absolutely integrable, for EVERY z. This is the promised justification of all finite sums, differences, and vector integrals in (2).

### Step 5. Local L^2 bounds, with the limiting passages justified

Set J_z(x)=integral |g_z(x-y)|_2 |rho(y)|_2 dy as another extended nonnegative integral. Direct radial integration of the first bound in (15) gives

  integral_{B_L} |g_z(v)|_2 dv <= C_z(L).                              (16)

The needed L^1*L^2 estimate can be proved without invoking an unverified regularity theorem. If k>=0 is L^1 and f>=0 is L^2, let f_N=min(f,N). For each x, Cauchy-Schwarz with measure k(v)dv gives

  [integral k(v) f_N(x-v) dv]^2
    <= ||k||_1 integral k(v) f_N(x-v)^2 dv.

Integrate in x. Tonelli applies to the nonnegative right side and translation preserves the inner L^2 integral. Consequently

  ||k*f_N||_2^2 <= ||k||_1^2 ||f||_2^2.

Monotone convergence as N tends to infinity gives the same bound for k*f, including its almost-everywhere finiteness. This argument verifies, rather than assumes, the local convolution estimate.

Apply it with f=|rho|_2 and k=1_{B_{R+a}} |g_z|_2. On B_R the convolution is J_z, because rho vanishes outside B_a. Thus

  ||J_z||_{L^2(B_R)} <= C_z(R+a) ||rho||_2.

On D_rho, (2) and Step 3 give |H_z|_2<=|z|J_z. Its complement is null, so the same L^2 estimate holds for the actual field (1) and for H_z^sharp. This proves (5). Replacing k by 1_{B_{R+a}}b also gives ||W_rho||_{L^2(B_R)}<=4 pi(R+a)||rho||_2.

These estimates identify H_z^sharp with the SAME locally L^2 actual magnetic field. In particular, against any compactly supported smooth scalar test f, both integral H_i f and integral H_i partial_j f agree for the two representatives. The products are integrable by Cauchy-Schwarz on a containing ball. No Maxwell equation has been assumed to obtain this representative.

### Step 6. Invariance of D_rho without an uncountable intersection

Fix Q=R_theta or S. Equations (9)-(10) and Euclidean norm preservation give |rho(Qy)|_2=|rho(y)|_2 for almost every y. For EVERY fixed observation point x, change variables y=Qv in the extended nonnegative integral:

  W_rho(Qx)
    = integral b(Qx-y)|rho(y)|_2 dy
    = integral b(Q(x-v))|rho(Qv)|_2 dv
    = integral b(x-v)|rho(v)|_2 dv
    = W_rho(x).                                                        (17)

The substitution is legitimate even when the value is infinity: Q is a Borel bijection with |det Q|=1, and the integrand is nonnegative and measurable. The source null set occurs in v and can be discarded inside the integral for each x.

Thus (17) has the quantifiers FOR EVERY theta, FOR EVERY x. It is not merely a separate almost-everywhere identity in x. Hence D_rho is invariant under every rotation and S, and the SAME set works for all z. No uncountable intersection of conull observation sets is taken.

### Step 7. Covariance of the actual magnetic integral

Let x be in D_rho. By (17), Qx is also in D_rho, so every integral and finite linear combination below is absolutely integrable. Using (11), the source law, the measure-preserving change of variables, and finally (12),

  H_z[rho](Qx)
    = -i z integral g_z(Qx-y) cross rho(y) dy
    = -i z integral Qg_z(x-v) cross Qrho(v) dv
    = -i z det(Q) Q integral g_z(x-v) cross rho(v) dv
    = det(Q) Q H_z[rho](x).                                            (18)

Bounded complex-linear maps commute with these integrals because their integrands are absolutely integrable. Inserting det(R_theta)=1 and det(S)=-1 proves the actual rotation and axial-reflection laws in (A2).

The operator form is also valid. If rho_Q(y)=Qrho(Q^{-1}y), then W_{rho_Q}(Qx)=W_rho(x) and

  H_z[rho_Q](Qx)=det(Q)QH_z[rho](x) for x in D_rho.

By the source-action identity (8), this applies to the actual source of A_theta u. It supplies covariance of the magnetic source-to-field construction, not just covariance of the explicit `centerH` model.

Define H_z^sharp as in (A3). D_rho and its complement are Q-invariant. Equation (18) applies on D_rho; off D_rho both sides are zero. Therefore

  for all z, theta, x,
    H_z^sharp(R_theta x)=R_theta H_z^sharp(x),
    H_z^sharp(Sx)=-S H_z^sharp(x).                                      (19)

This is the simultaneous pointwise representative needed before choosing a rotation as a function of x.

### Step 8. Reflection plus rotation forces the radial scalar to vanish

Put s_z(x)=x dot H_z^sharp(x). Orthogonality and (19) give

  s_z(R_theta x)=s_z(x),      s_z(Sx)=-s_z(x).                          (20)

For any x off the axis, let q=sqrt(x_0^2+x_1^2)>0 and choose theta with cos(theta)=x_0/q, sin(theta)=x_1/q. The standard parametrization of the real unit circle guarantees such an angle; alternatively this rotation is the explicit 2 by 2 orthogonal matrix with those entries. Then y=R_{-theta}x=(q,0,x_2) is fixed by S. Consequently

  s_z(y)=s_z(Sy)=-s_z(y),

so s_z(y)=0 over C, and (20) gives s_z(x)=0. On the axis Sx=x already, so the same reflection argument proves the radial scalar is zero there as well.

The point-dependent angle is used only AFTER (19), whose quantifiers are all theta and all x. This is the resolution of the rotation-dependent-null-set problem; evaluating (9) at a point-dependent angle would not have been legitimate.

In fact reflection at (q,0,t) forces the 0 and 2 coordinates of H_z^sharp to be zero. Thus, for q>0,

  H_z^sharp(q cos(theta),q sin(theta),t)
    = h_z(q,t) (-sin(theta),cos(theta),0),
  h_z(q,t)=H_z^sharp(q,0,t)_1.                                         (21)

On the axis rotation by pi forces the first two coordinates to vanish, and axial reflection forces the third to vanish. Hence H_z^sharp=0 there. Away from the axis H_z^sharp may be nonzero: (21) is a cylindrical azimuthal magnetic field, not a claim that H vanishes identically.

Since H_z^sharp=H_z[rho] on D_rho and D_rho is conull, the original actual field has x dot H_z[rho](x)=0 almost everywhere. This completes the core radial-zero proof.

### Step 9. The raw field on the axis, and review of the pending reflection proof

There is a useful pointwise statement even at axis points outside D_rho. Both S=diag(1,-1,1) and R_pi=diag(-1,-1,1) are diagonal sign transformations. For Q in {S,R_pi}, write Q=diag(q_0,q_1,q_2), q_j in {-1,1}, and set

  A_jl(x)=integral rho_l(y) g_z,j(x-y) dy.

The same source a.e. congruence and substitution as before give, at EVERY x,

  A_jl(Qx)=q_j q_l A_jl(x).                                           (22)

Unlike a general rotation, this identity does not distribute an integral over any sum. Multiplication by a nonzero scalar preserves both integrability and nonintegrability; negation and a measure-preserving substitution therefore commute even with a totalized scalar integral. Thus (22) is valid without x in D_rho. Since q_next(i) q_previous(i)=det(Q)q_i, (1) implies the raw axial law H(Qx)=det(Q)QH(x) at every x for these two transformations.

At an axis point x=(0,0,t), Q=R_pi fixes x and forces H_0=H_1=0. Reflection S fixes x and its axial law forces H_2=0. Hence the original expression (1) is zero at every axis point.

This also reviews the mathematics of the PENDING `ComplexMagneticReflection` file: its `gradient_response_reflection` uses integral a.e. congruence, a measure-preserving substitution, and one coordinate sign at a time; `magnetic_reflection` then uses q_next q_previous=-q_i. Those steps are valid even for totalized integrals. The review does NOT certify that file's Lean compilation, imports, frozen object, or independent audit. Its formal status remains PENDING.

It is unsafe to extend that proof to an arbitrary rotation without an integrability argument. For example, on (0,1), f(t)=1/t and g(t)=-1/t+1 both have totalized scalar integral zero, but f+g=1 has integral one. A rotation mixing the two coordinates therefore does not in general commute with componentwise totalized integration. This is a counterexample to the unrestricted proof rule, not a claim of a counterexample to this particular magnetic field.

Nor does sector membership imply absolute convergence at every point. The source rho(y)=1_{0<|y|<1/2}|y|^(-5/4)e_2 is L^2, rotation-covariant and polar-reflection-even. Its W_rho(0)=4 pi integral_0^(1/2) t^(-5/4) dt is infinite. At z=1, |g_1(v)|_2=sqrt(1+|v|^2)/(4 pi |v|^2)>=1/(4 pi |v|^2), so J_1(0) is infinite as well; the angular factors in the nonzero coordinate products have positive absolute integral, making those scalar products nonintegrable. At the physical L^2 level, dividing by sqrt(n^2-1) on its support gives an electric-sector vector. Realizing that vector as a literal repository VectorL2 element also uses the bidirectional physical-coordinate identification. This demonstrates why an everywhere absolutely convergent interior formula cannot be assumed.

The current mathlib Bochner-integral documentation explicitly defines the integral to be zero outside L^1 and requires integrability in its addition theorem. This is background verification of the issue; it is not a check of the pinned project's API or binaries. Primary reference [E1] below.

### Step 10. Exterior pointwise covariance and smoothness

If |x|>a, put d=|x|-a>0 and L=|x|+a. For y in the source support, d<=|x-y|<=L. Hence

  W_rho(x)<=d^(-2)||rho||_1<infinity,
  J_z(x)<=exp(|z|L)/(4 pi) [|z|/d+1/d^2] ||rho||_1.                    (23)

Every exterior point belongs to D_rho, so the original field obeys all of the covariance and radial-zero identities pointwise there.

Smoothness follows directly from the actual kernel, with its differentiation-under-the-integral premise verified: on any compact set K contained in {|x|>a}, the set of differences x-y, x in K, y in the support ball, is compact and stays a positive distance from zero. Every spatial derivative of g_z is bounded on that difference set. The differentiated integrands are bounded by a constant times |rho(y)|_2, an L^1 function. Dominated convergence, applied to difference quotients on a slightly larger compact exterior neighborhood, permits each derivative under the integral. Repeating for all orders gives C^infinity exterior regularity.

No interior regularity theorem or limiting argument across the material interface is used.

### Step 11. Every positive-radius sphere meets D_rho in full area measure

Volume-a.e. zero by itself would give only an a.e.-radius result. The following additional estimate proves the stronger actual-convolution conclusion (4).

Fix r>0, and for y in R^3 define the nonnegative, possibly infinite quantity

  A_r(y)=integral_{S^2} b(r omega-y) d sigma(omega).

Let t=|y|. By rotational invariance of area, for t>0 and t!=r,

  A_r(t)=2 pi integral_{-1}^1 [r^2+t^2-2rt s]^(-1) ds
        = (2 pi/(rt)) log((r+t)/|r-t|).                               (24)

At t=0 the value is 4 pi/r^2. At t=r the integral is infinite, even though b(0)=0 at a single angular point. That exceptional sphere has three-dimensional measure zero. We treat A_r as a finite-a.e. L^2 representative when applying Cauchy-Schwarz; changing its values on that null sphere does not change an integral in y.

Polar integration in y gives

  ||A_r||_{L^2(B_a)}^2
    = (16 pi^3/r^2) integral_0^a [log((r+t)/|r-t|)]^2 dt.              (25)

Put L=r+a>0. For 0<=t<=a, t!=r,

  0 <= log((r+t)/|r-t|) <= log(L/|r-t|).

The interval for s=t-r lies in [-L,L]. Therefore

  integral_0^a [log((r+t)/|r-t|)]^2 dt
    <= 2 integral_0^L [log(L/s)]^2 ds
    = 2L integral_0^infinity v^2 exp(-v) dv
    = 4L.                                                             (26)

The substitution s=L exp(-v) proves the middle equality. Two integrations by parts give the last integral as 2; v^2 exp(-v) and v exp(-v) have zero endpoint limits at infinity, so these integrations by parts have no omitted boundary terms. Consequently

  ||A_r||_{L^2(B_a)} <= 8 pi^(3/2) sqrt(r+a)/r.                         (27)

Tonelli is applicable to b(r omega-y)|rho(y)|_2 because it is nonnegative and jointly measurable. Equations (13), (27), and Cauchy-Schwarz give

  integral_{S^2} W_rho(r omega) d sigma
    = integral_{B_a} |rho(y)|_2 A_r(y) dy
    <= 8 pi^(3/2) sqrt(r+a)/r ||rho||_2 < infinity.                     (28)

This holds for EACH fixed r>0, not just almost every r. It proves sigma({omega:r omega not in D_rho})=0 for every positive radius. The exceptional angular set in (28) depends on rho and r, but not on z or on a harmonic weight.

On this sphere, |g_z|<=B_z(r+a)b over the support differences, so (28) also gives

  integral_{S^2} J_z(r omega) d sigma
    <= 2 sqrt(pi) exp(|z|(r+a)) [1+|z|(r+a)]
       sqrt(r+a)/r ||rho||_2.

Since |H_z|_2<=|z|J_z on D_rho, this proves (6). All fixed-radius restrictions used here are measurable by Step 1, and all asserted magnetic surface integrals are genuine finite integrals, not zeros caused by totalization.

### Step 12. Actual magnetic harmonic moments and the L^2 Debye datum

Fix any r>0. Outside the angular-null set in (28), x=r omega belongs to D_rho, and Step 8 yields

  0=(r omega) dot H_z[rho](r omega)=r [omega dot H_z[rho](r omega)].

Divide by the nonzero real number r. The resulting angular radial component is zero almost everywhere, before any weight is selected. Multiplication by any bounded measurable weight and integration proves (3), simultaneously for all such weights at this radius.

In the supplied `radialCoefficient`, the weight is exactly `SphericalMomentEquation.angularPolynomial p`; the definition has no conjugation and no normalization factor. Under (I3), the same angular-null set is null for angularMeasure, so its scalar integrand is almost everywhere zero and hence integrable with integral zero. This proves (4) for all polynomials p, all r>0, and all z. It also proves vanishing against the usual conjugated spherical-harmonic weights, if those are used for a separate normalized basis. A fixed normalization only rescales the integrals and cannot affect zero.

The r=1 conclusion is an angular integral of the actual convolution evaluated on the interface sphere. It is NOT an identification with an interior or exterior trace and does not establish their equality. If a separately proved theorem supplies one-sided continuous representatives up to the interface, their radial dot products are zero by continuity from their open regions. That extra theorem is not assumed here or claimed as proved.

For the project's localized L^2 magnetic response, the ACCEPTED `ComplexMagneticResponse.response_actual`, with c=c_n, source radius 1, observation radius T=1, and `coefficient_support`, identifies each coordinate with H_z[rho] almost everywhere on the closed unit ball. Finite intersection over the three coordinates preserves this equality for the bilinear radial sum. Thus its radial scalar is zero almost everywhere there.

Under the exact clipping identity (I4), `DebyeScalarL2.datum 1 (ComplexMagneticDebye.coordinates n hn z u)` is therefore the ZERO L^2 element: it is zero almost everywhere inside its support and identically zero outside in the asserted representative. This derivation uses accepted `response_actual` plus the explicitly requested datum interface, rather than treating pending scalar theorems as independently accepted.

Alternatively, the stated PENDING `ComplexMagneticDebye.scalar_actual`, if audited and promoted, immediately gives the restricted equality to zero on the open unit ball. Its `moment_actual` then gives

  RadialMoment.moment (datum 1 coordinates) (angularPolynomial p) r = 0
    for almost every r in (0,1).                                       (29)

Do not upgrade (29) to every r for an arbitrary chosen L^2-datum representative: it could differ from zero on an entire sphere. The all-radii statement (4) is specific to the actual convolution representative and rests on (28). The usual polar null-set theorem supplies one a.e.-radius set independent of p for (29).

No magnetic Helmholtz equation or regular radial mode is needed for these conclusions. The existence statement in the pending `regular_radial_coefficient` can now use amplitude c=0 without solving an ODE. To prove that an already named amplitude c in c*regular(l,z*n,r) is zero, one additionally needs nontriviality of that regular mode on a set of positive radial measure; that interface is not supplied and is not silently invoked.

Steps 1-12 prove all claims in Theorem A, subject to the exact defining interfaces stated in Section 1.2.

## 3. Exact additional vector-harmonic reconstruction needed for TE exclusion

This section separates a weak coefficient identity that can be derived directly from Maxwell from the full reconstruction theorem still needed for the project's TE terminology. It does not declare TE classification already proved.

### 3.1 Normalization and the required reconstruction statement

Let J=(a,b) with 0<=a<b<=infinity, and Omega_J={x:|x| in J}. On this open spherical shell, suppose E,H are actual L^2_loc fields and

  curl E = i z H,       curl H = -i z epsilon E                         (30)

as distributions against every compact smooth vector test. Assume z!=0, constant epsilon!=0, and permeability normalized to 1. Take scalar spherical harmonics Y_lm orthonormal for standard area sigma, satisfying -Delta_S Y_lm=lambda_l Y_lm, lambda_l=l(l+1). For l>=1 put

  R_lm=Y_lm omega,
  Psi_lm=grad_S Y_lm/sqrt(lambda_l),
  Phi_lm=omega cross Psi_lm.

Use R_00=Y_00 omega separately. If the project instead uses a constant multiple of sigma, renormalize the Y_lm for that measure; the coefficient formulas below then retain the same form.

For F=E,H, define

  d^F_lm(r)=integral_{S^2} [r omega dot F(r omega)] conjugate(Y_lm) d sigma,
  a^F_lm(r)=d^F_lm(r)/r,
  b^F_lm(r)=integral F(r omega) dot conjugate(Psi_lm) d sigma,
  c^F_lm(r)=integral F(r omega) dot conjugate(Phi_lm) d sigma.

These coefficients are defined for almost every radius using L^2 polar slices. The conjugations in these normalized harmonic coordinates are explicit; the packet's polynomial moment definition remains the unconjugated one in (4).

REQUIRED ADDITIONAL THEOREM, NOT SUPPLIED OR FORMALLY PROVED HERE:

(V1) {R_00} together with {R_lm,Psi_lm,Phi_lm : l>=1, |m|<=l} is a complete orthonormal basis of L^2(S^2;C^3).

(V2) For every pair satisfying (30), d^E_lm,d^H_lm belong to H^1_loc(J), the degree-zero coefficients are zero, and the pair reconstructs as

  E(r omega) = sum_{l>=1,m} [
      (d^E_lm/r) R_lm
      + ((r d^E_lm)'/(sqrt(lambda_l) r)) Psi_lm
      - (i z d^H_lm/sqrt(lambda_l)) Phi_lm ],                           (31)

  H(r omega) = sum_{l>=1,m} [
      (d^H_lm/r) R_lm
      + ((r d^H_lm)'/(sqrt(lambda_l) r)) Psi_lm
      + (i z epsilon d^E_lm/sqrt(lambda_l)) Phi_lm ].                   (32)

Derivatives are weak radial derivatives. The series converge in angular L^2 for almost every r and in L^2 on every compact subannulus. This statement includes the absence of an invisible tangential remainder: that is why completeness cannot be replaced by merely naming some scalar coefficients.

The radial, surface-gradient, and surface-curl vector harmonics and their completeness are classical; a primary reference is Barrera, Estevez and Giraldo (1985), Section 3 [E2]. The exact weak-field theorem (V1)-(V2), with the stated normalization, topology, and project-field identification, remains a Lean obligation. Theorem A does not depend on it.

### 3.2 Weak radial-curl identity proved here

The coefficient relation relevant to TE exclusion can be justified directly without assuming differentiable E or H. Fix l>=1 and a smooth radial eta compactly supported in J. The vector test

  V(x)=eta(r) conjugate(Y_lm(omega)) omega, r=|x|, omega=x/r,

is smooth with compact support away from r=0 and from the shell's endpoints. Direct differentiation of this smooth test gives

  curl V = -eta(r) sqrt(lambda_l)/r conjugate(Phi_lm(omega)).           (33)

The radial derivative of eta contributes zero because its gradient is parallel to omega. The remaining angular gradient gives (33), with the displayed minus sign from grad_S Y cross omega = -omega cross grad_S Y.

By the definition of distributional curl, integral E dot curl V = integral (i z H) dot V. All these integrals are absolutely convergent: the tests and their derivatives are bounded on a compact shell, and E,H are L^2 there. Polar Fubini is consequently valid. Substituting (33) yields

  -sqrt(lambda_l) integral_J eta(r) r c^E_lm(r) dr
    = i z integral_J eta(r) r^2 a^H_lm(r) dr.                          (34)

The coefficient functions are L^2 on every compact radial interval by Cauchy-Schwarz on the sphere and polar disintegration; on such intervals r is bounded above and below. Thus the difference of the two sides' densities is L^1_loc. The fundamental lemma for distributions represented by locally integrable functions gives

  c^E_lm(r) = -i z d^H_lm(r)/sqrt(lambda_l) for almost every r.         (35)

For completeness, that fundamental lemma does not assume hidden regularity: convolving a locally integrable density which annihilates every compact smooth test with a compact mollifier gives zero on each smaller interval; the usual local L^1 convergence of mollification then gives zero almost everywhere. It applies separately to real and imaginary parts.

The same test in the second Maxwell equation gives

  c^H_lm(r) = i z epsilon d^E_lm(r)/sqrt(lambda_l).                     (36)

The signs agree with curl(c Phi)'s radial coefficient -sqrt(lambda_l)c/r. Taking divergence in (30) gives div E=div H=0 under z!=0 and epsilon!=0; weak scalar-gradient tests justify div curl=0. The radial/poloidal divergence identity is

  (r^2 a^F_lm)'=sqrt(lambda_l) r b^F_lm.                              (37)

To check (37), use the compact scalar test eta(r) conjugate(Y_lm). Its gradient is eta'(r) conjugate(Y_lm) omega + eta(r) sqrt(lambda_l)/r conjugate(Psi_lm). Distributional divergence and absolutely convergent polar integration give integral r^2 a^F_lm eta' + sqrt(lambda_l) integral r b^F_lm eta = 0, which is precisely (37). The right side of (37) is L^2 on each compact radial interval, so r^2 a^F_lm, and therefore d^F_lm=r a^F_lm, belong to H^1_loc. This identifies the poloidal coefficients in (31)-(32). A complete formal proof must implement these compact-test steps and establish (V1) separately for reconstruction. For l=0 the radial vector test has zero curl; (30) and the nonzero constants force both a^E_00 and a^H_00 to vanish. No Psi_00 or Phi_00 is defined or divided by.

Equation (35), proved in (33)-(34), plus Theorem A implies every toroidal electric coefficient is zero on each spherical annulus where E,H are L^2_loc and the first Maxwell equation holds distributionally. This forward implication itself needs neither epsilon!=0 nor z!=0. The stronger two-field reconstruction and nonstatic interpretation require the assumptions of (V1)-(V2).

After the reconstruction theorem is established, (31)-(32) with d^H=0 show that the electric field has only radial/poloidal terms and the magnetic field only toroidal terms. This is the precise TE-exclusion consequence in this convention. It does not say the magnetic field is zero or select a single harmonic degree.
