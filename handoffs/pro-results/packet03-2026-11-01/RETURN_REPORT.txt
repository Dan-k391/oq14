## 1. Exact theorems and quantifiers

### 1.1 Conventions and hypotheses

All integrals use Lebesgue measure. For a complex vector field F,
\[
 \|F\|_A^2=\int_A |F(x)|^2\,dx
 =\sum_{j=1}^3\int_A |F_j(x)|^2\,dx.
\]
The pointwise vector norm is the Euclidean Hermitian norm; dot products appearing with an explicit conjugate are complex bilinear before that conjugation.

Let Omega, U, V be open subsets of R3, with U nonempty and
\[
 \overline U\ \text{compact},\quad \overline U\subset V,\qquad
 \overline V\ \text{compact},\quad \overline V\subset\Omega.
\]
No connectedness or boundary smoothness is assumed. The empty-U case is trivial.

Fix d >= 0, set M=1+d, and define
\[
 \epsilon(x)=
 \begin{cases}1,&x_3<0,\\ M,&x_3\ge0,\end{cases}
 \qquad a=\epsilon^{-1}.
\]
Thus M >= 1 and M^{-1} <= a <= 1.

Assume E,H belong to H(curl;Omega), meaning their actual distributional curls on ALL of Omega are in L2, and assume
\[
 \operatorname{curl}E=ikH,\qquad
 \operatorname{curl}H=-ik\epsilon E
 \tag{M}
\]
as these actual weak curls. Tests may cross the interface. Separate equations on the two open half-regions, without the regional weak equations or appropriate transmission conditions, are not this hypothesis.

Set
\[
 K=\overline U,\quad
 R=\operatorname{dist}(K,\mathbb R^3\setminus V)>0,\quad
 L=4/R,\quad h_0=R/8.
 \tag{G}
\]
For physical tangential coordinates i=1,2 and real h != 0, use
\[
 \delta_{h,i}F(x)=\frac{F(x+he_i)-F(x)}h.
\]
These are Lean indices 0 and 1; the normal coordinate is 2 : Fin 3.

### 1.2 Real-frequency theorem

For EVERY choice of sets and d above, EVERY real k>0, and EVERY pair of actual fields satisfying (M), define
\[
 B=\sqrt{2k^2+4L^2}=\sqrt{2k^2+64/R^2}.
\]
Then, for BOTH tangential directions,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0<|h|<h_0}\|\delta_{h,i}\operatorname{curl}H\|_U
 \le \sqrt M\,B\bigl(kM\|E\|_V+L\|H\|_V\bigr).
 }\tag{T1}
\]
A stronger estimate is
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0<|h|<h_0}\|\delta_{h,i}\operatorname{curl}H\|_U
 \le C_H\|H\|_V,\qquad
 C_H=MB^2+\sqrt M\,BL.
 }\tag{T2}
\]
Consequently the requested bound with C(||E||_V+||H||_V) holds with C=C_H. Alternatively, (T1) gives
\[
 C_{\rm mixed}=\sqrt M\,B\max\{kM,L\}.
\]
All constants are independent of the fields, h, and the tangential direction. Only M, k, and the displayed separation R enter. No controlling patch larger than V is needed.

### 1.3 Complex-frequency theorem

For EVERY k in C minus {0}, assume (M) with that same complex k and the same positive real epsilon. Put
\[
 \lambda(k)=\max\{\operatorname{Re}(k^2),0\},\qquad
 B(k)=\sqrt{2\lambda(k)+64/R^2}.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0<|h|<h_0}\|\delta_{h,i}\operatorname{curl}H\|_U
 \le \sqrt M\,B(k)\bigl(|k|M\|E\|_V+L\|H\|_V\bigr),
 }\tag{T3}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{0<|h|<h_0}\|\delta_{h,i}\operatorname{curl}H\|_U
 \le \bigl(MB(k)^2+\sqrt M\,B(k)L\bigr)\|H\|_V.
 }\tag{T4}
\]
No sign condition on Re k, Im k, or Re(k^2) is needed. Section 3 gives the exact complex calculation and uniform family statement.

These are local results. They do not use or alter an outgoing condition, normalization, or the convention for one-sided interface values. Changing epsilon on the plane itself has no effect on the L2 assertions.

## 2. Complete numbered proof for real k>0

### Step 1. Construct cutoffs with the stated numerical constants

Let s(x)=dist(x,K), a 1-Lipschitz function. Define f_eta to be 1 for s<=R/8, linear from 1 to 0 on [R/8,3R/8], and 0 for s>=3R/8. Define f_zeta to be 1 for s<=5R/8, linear from 1 to 0 on [5R/8,7R/8], and 0 for s>=7R/8.

Both functions are compactly supported and have Lipschitz constant at most L=4/R. Convolve each with a nonnegative C-infinity mollifier of integral 1 supported in the ball of radius R/16. Call the results eta and zeta. They are real, smooth, compactly supported, and satisfy
\[
 0\le\eta,\zeta\le1,\qquad
 \|\nabla\eta\|_\infty,\|\nabla\zeta\|_\infty\le L,
\]
\[
 \eta=1\ \text{when }s\le R/16,\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}\eta\subset\{s\le7R/16\},
\]
\[
 \zeta=1\ \text{when }s\le9R/16,\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}\zeta\subset\{s\le15R/16\}\Subset V.
 \tag{1}
\]
Convolution preserves the Lipschitz bound because it is an average of translates; for the smooth result that bound controls the Euclidean gradient.

Write S=supp eta. If |h|<R/8, x is in S, and 0<=t<=1, then
\[
 s(x+the_i)\le s(x)+|h|<9R/16.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus eta=1 on U, zeta=1 on all required translated segments from S, and those segments lie in V. For
\[
 D_h=V\cap(V-he_i)=\{x\in V:x+he_i\in V\},
\]
we have S compactly contained in D_h. Both signs of h are covered.

### Step 2. State the elementary approximation facts used below

Choose a nonnegative smooth compactly supported kernel rho with integral 1 and set rho_t(x)=t^{-3}rho(x/t). For every f in L2(R3;C^m),
\[
 \rho_t*f\longrightarrow f\quad\text{in }L^2,\qquad
 \|\rho_t*f\|_2\le\|f\|_2.
 \tag{3}
\]
For f with compact support, rho_t*f is smooth with compact support. Convolution commutes with every constant-coefficient distributional derivative.

Here is why these facts apply without additional regularity. Minkowski's inequality and translation invariance give the norm bound. Strong continuity of translations in L2 gives
\[
 \|\rho_t*f-f\|_2
 \le\int\rho(y)\|f(\,\cdot-ty)-f\|_2\,dy\longrightarrow0
\]
by dominated convergence, with dominating function 2 rho(y)||f||_2. Translation continuity follows first for continuous compactly supported functions and then for L2 functions by density and the translation isometry.

All fields mollified below are L2 and have compact support, hence are L1 on a bounded set. Differentiating their convolution is justified by the bounded compactly supported derivatives of the kernel and dominated convergence. Commutation with distributional derivatives follows by Fubini and integration against smooth compact tests; the relevant integrals are absolutely integrable by local L1 integrability. Completeness of L2 is also used.

For a primary author-hosted reference to the approximation fact, see Juha Kinnunen, Real Analysis, Theorems 3.18 and 3.21:
https://math.aalto.fi/~jkkinnun/files/real_analysis.pdf
No external Maxwell or transmission regularity theorem is being invoked.

### Step 3. Derive divergence and the actual inverse-weight curl equation

Distributional derivatives commute, so div(curl E)=0: componentwise, each mixed second derivative cancels its counterpart. Applying divergence to the first equation in (M) gives
\[
 ik\,\operatorname{div}H=0.
\]
Since k is nonzero,
\[
 \operatorname{div}H=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal D'(\Omega).
 \tag{4}
\]

Define
\[
 W=-ikE.
\]
The second equation in (M), with epsilon positive, gives
\[
 W=a\,\operatorname{curl}H.
\]
Because E is a genuine H(curl) field, so is W. The first equation gives
\[
 \operatorname{curl}W=(-ik)(ikH)=k^2H.
 \tag{5}
\]
No derivative of epsilon has been taken.

For a smooth compactly supported vector test phi in Omega, the weak-curl identity, with conjugation explicitly placed on the test, is
\[
 \int_\Omega a\,\operatorname{curl}H\cdot
       \overline{\operatorname{curl}\phi}
 =k^2\int_\Omega H\cdot\overline\phi.
 \tag{6}
\]
Both products are integrable by Cauchy-Schwarz. This is the genuine regional equation.

### Step 4. Justify compact H(curl) tests and every cutoff product

We need the following precise testing lemma.

Let D be open and w,g be L2(D) fields with curl w=g in distributions. If phi belongs to H(curl;D) and has compact support inside D, then
\[
 \int_D w\cdot\overline{\operatorname{curl}\phi}
 =\int_D g\cdot\overline\phi.
 \tag{7}
\]

To prove it, extend phi by zero only after using its compact support. Its global weak curl is the zero extension of its regional curl. Indeed, a smooth cutoff equal to 1 near the support reduces any global compact test to a regional test; all cutoff-derivative terms are outside that support and vanish. Thus no boundary distribution is created.

Mollify this extension with radii smaller than its support's distance to D-complement. The approximants are smooth compact tests inside D. Equation (3), applied to both phi and its weak curl, gives convergence in the H(curl) graph norm. The two pairings converge by Cauchy-Schwarz, proving (7).

The weak product rule used here and below is
\[
 \operatorname{curl}(\chi v)
 =\chi\operatorname{curl}v+\nabla\chi\times v
 \tag{8}
\]
for smooth scalar chi and v in H(curl) locally. It follows directly by testing each component and applying the scalar distributional product rule. Likewise,
\[
 \operatorname{div}(\chi v)
 =\chi\operatorname{div}v+\nabla\chi\cdot v.
 \tag{9}
\]
All terms in the applications below are L2, since the cutoff and its derivatives are bounded with compact support.

In particular chi^2 v is an admissible test whenever chi is smooth and compactly supported in D, and
\[
 \operatorname{curl}(\chi^2v)
 =\chi^2\operatorname{curl}v+2\chi\nabla\chi\times v.
 \tag{10}
\]
These arguments work across the material plane. We never integrate separately over the two half-regions or invoke an unproved trace identity.

### Step 5. Prove the local weighted energy absorption

Let v be a genuine H(curl;D) field, let a be real with M^{-1}<=a<=1, and suppose a curl v belongs to H(curl;D) with
\[
 \operatorname{curl}(a\operatorname{curl}v)=k^2v.
\]
Take a real smooth compact cutoff chi. Step 4 makes phi=chi^2 v admissible. Define
\[
 A_\chi=\int_D a\chi^2|\operatorname{curl}v|^2,\quad
 Q_\chi=\int_D\chi^2|v|^2,
\]
\[
 T_\chi=\int_D a\chi\,\operatorname{curl}v\cdot
                      \overline{\nabla\chi\times v},\quad
 D_\chi=\int_D a|\nabla\chi\times v|^2.
\]
The exact complex equality is
\[
 A_\chi+2T_\chi=k^2Q_\chi.
 \tag{11}
\]
Here A_chi,Q_chi,D_chi are real nonnegative finite quantities; T_chi may be complex. Taking real parts, weighted Cauchy-Schwarz yields
\[
 A_\chi=k^2Q_\chi-2\operatorname{Re}T_\chi
 \le k^2Q_\chi+2\sqrt{A_\chi D_\chi}.
\]
The inequality
\[
 2\sqrt{A_\chi D_\chi}
 \le\tfrac12 A_\chi+2D_\chi
\]
is the expansion of the nonnegative square
(sqrt(A_chi)-2sqrt(D_chi))^2. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 A_\chi\le2k^2Q_\chi+4D_\chi.
 }\tag{12}
\]
The highest-derivative energy has actually been absorbed.

When 0<=chi<=1 and ||grad chi||_infinity<=L, this gives
\[
 A_\chi\le(2k^2+4L^2)\|v\|_{\operatorname{supp}\chi}^2
 =B^2\|v\|_{\operatorname{supp}\chi}^2,
 \tag{13}
\]
because |grad chi cross v|<=L|v| and a<=1. There is no unbounded quotient on the right of this lemma apart from the lower-order field v, which is controlled independently below.

Apply (12) first to the ORIGINAL H with chi=zeta in V. This uses only H(curl) regularity. Coercivity gives
\[
 \|\zeta\operatorname{curl}H\|_2
 \le\sqrt M\,B\|H\|_V.
 \tag{14}
\]

### Step 6. Prove the quantitative compact div-curl regularity lemma

Lemma: if F is a compactly supported L2(R3;C3) field and its distributional curl C and divergence D belong to L2, then F belongs to H1(R3;C3), and
\[
 \boxed{\|\nabla F\|_2^2=\|C\|_2^2+\|D\|_2^2.}
 \tag{15}
\]

For a smooth compactly supported complex vector field G, expansion gives
\[
 \|\operatorname{curl}G\|_2^2
 =\|\nabla G\|_2^2
 -\sum_{p,q}\int\partial_pG_q\,\overline{\partial_qG_p}.
\]
Two integrations by parts, with no boundary term, show
\[
 \int\partial_pG_q\,\overline{\partial_qG_p}
 =\int\partial_qG_q\,\overline{\partial_pG_p}.
\]
Summing gives ||div G||_2^2, proving the identity for G.

Now let F_n=rho_{1/n}*F. These fields are smooth and compactly supported, and
\[
 \operatorname{curl}F_n=\rho_{1/n}*C,\qquad
 \operatorname{div}F_n=\rho_{1/n}*D.
\]
Applying the smooth identity to F_n-F_m gives
\[
 \|\nabla F_n-\nabla F_m\|_2^2
 =\|\rho_{1/n}*C-\rho_{1/m}*C\|_2^2
  +\|\rho_{1/n}*D-\rho_{1/m}*D\|_2^2.
\]
The right side tends to zero by (3). Hence the gradients are Cauchy in L2. If G is their L2 limit, then F_n->F in L2 and integration against a compact smooth scalar test identifies G_{pq} as the weak derivative partial_p F_q. This proves F in H1. Passing to the limit in the smooth identity proves (15).

This proof establishes the needed regularity. It does not assume a pre-existing H1 estimate or invoke a transmission regularity theorem.

### Step 7. Apply the div-curl lemma to the valid cutoff extension

Let F=zeta H in Omega, extended by zero outside Omega. Compact support of zeta inside V justifies the global product equations
\[
 \operatorname{curl}F
 =\zeta\operatorname{curl}H+\nabla\zeta\times H,\qquad
 \operatorname{div}F=\nabla\zeta\cdot H.
 \tag{16}
\]
Their right sides are understood as the corresponding compactly supported extensions. They are L2. The terms involving grad zeta are retained; no homogeneous whole-space Maxwell equation is claimed for F.

For real p and complex z, direct expansion, or applying the real identity separately to Re z and Im z, gives
\[
 |p\times z|^2+|p\cdot z|^2=|p|^2|z|^2.
 \tag{17}
\]
By (15), followed by the triangle inequality in
L2(C3) direct-sum L2(C),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\nabla F\|_2
 &=\|(\operatorname{curl}F,\operatorname{div}F)\|\\
 &\le\|\zeta\operatorname{curl}H\|_2
       +\||\nabla\zeta|\,|H|\|_2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
\]
Consequently there are TWO independently available bounds:
\[
 \boxed{\|\nabla F\|_2\le kM\|E\|_V+L\|H\|_V,}
 \tag{19}
\]
using curl H=-ik epsilon E, and
\[
 \boxed{\|\nabla F\|_2\le(\sqrt M\,B+L)\|H\|_V,}
 \tag{20}
\]
using the original-field energy estimate (14). The cutoff constant in these formulas is 1, rather than an extra sqrt(2), because (17) treats curl and divergence together.

### Step 8. Bound quotients of H independently of quotients of its curl

For any F in H1(R3;C3) and h!=0,
\[
 \delta_{h,i}F
 =\int_0^1\partial_iF(\,\cdot+the_i\,)\,dt
 \quad\text{in }L^2,
\]
and hence
\[
 \|\delta_{h,i}F\|_2\le\|\partial_iF\|_2\le\|\nabla F\|_2.
 \tag{21}
\]
For smooth F the equality is the fundamental theorem of calculus, for either sign of h. For our compactly supported H1 field, its mollifications converge in H1 by (3). At fixed h the quotient operator is bounded on L2 with norm at most 2/|h|, so the equality passes to the limit. The Bochner integral converges because translations are L2 isometries and strongly continuous; Minkowski gives (21).

Equation (2) implies zeta=1 at both x and x+he_i when x is in S. Translation preserves Lebesgue null sets, so
\[
 \delta_{h,i}H=\delta_{h,i}F\quad\text{a.e. on }S.
\]
Thus N_h:=||delta_{h,i}H||_S satisfies
\[
 N_h\le kM\|E\|_V+L\|H\|_V,\qquad
 N_h\le(\sqrt M\,B+L)\|H\|_V.
 \tag{22}
\]
No tangential derivative of curl H has been used.

### Step 9. Derive the regional quotient equations on the translated overlap

Fix i tangential and 0<|h|<h0. On D_h put
\[
 q_h=\delta_{h,i}H,\quad
 c_h=\delta_{h,i}\operatorname{curl}H,\quad
 w_h=\delta_{h,i}W.
\]
For this fixed h all three are L2(D_h): each translated difference has norm at most 2/|h| times the corresponding L2(V) norm. This finite, nonuniform bound suffices to make the forthcoming test legal.

Regional translation of the weak-curl equation gives
\[
 \operatorname{curl}q_h=c_h,\qquad
 \operatorname{curl}w_h=k^2q_h.
 \tag{23}
\]
To see the regional translation rigorously, take phi in C_c-infinity(D_h) and use phi_h(y)=phi(y-he_i) as a test on V. Its support lies in V. The substitution y=x+he_i transforms the weak-curl pairing into the shifted pairing. Subtract the unshifted identity and divide by the real h. All pairings are absolutely integrable L2 products.

Tangential translation leaves x3 unchanged, so a(x+he_i)=a(x), including at the plane. The full product-quotient identity is
\[
 \delta_{h,i}(aC)(x)
 =a(x)\delta_{h,i}C(x)
  +\delta_{h,i}a(x)\,C(x+he_i).
\]
The second term is EXACTLY ZERO here. With C=curl H this yields
\[
 w_h=a c_h=a\operatorname{curl}q_h.
 \tag{24}
\]
Therefore q_h and w_h are genuine regional H(curl) fields on D_h and
\[
 \int_{D_h}a c_h\cdot\overline{\operatorname{curl}\phi}
 =k^2\int_{D_h}q_h\cdot\overline\phi.
 \tag{25}
\]

This argument does not apply a global homogeneous equation to zero extensions of H, W, or curl H. The equation is proved directly on the translated overlap.

### Step 10. Test the quotient equation, display its commutator, and absorb

Since S is compactly contained in D_h, Step 4 permits
\[
 \phi=\eta^2q_h,\qquad
 \operatorname{curl}\phi=\eta^2c_h+2\eta\nabla\eta\times q_h.
\]
Let
\[
 A_h=\int_{D_h}a\eta^2|c_h|^2,\qquad
 T_h=\int_{D_h}a\eta c_h\cdot\overline{\nabla\eta\times q_h}.
\]
The exact energy identity is
\[
 A_h+2T_h=k^2\|\eta q_h\|_2^2.
 \tag{26}
\]
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
 |T_h|
 \le\sqrt{A_h}
      \left(\int_{D_h}a|\nabla\eta\times q_h|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le\sqrt{A_h}\,L N_h.
\]
Consequently
\[
 A_h\le k^2N_h^2+2\sqrt{A_h}\,L N_h
 \le k^2N_h^2+\tfrac12A_h+2L^2N_h^2,
\]
so
\[
 A_h\le B^2N_h^2.
 \tag{27}
\]
Finally eta=1 on U and a>=1/M imply
\[
 \|\delta_{h,i}\operatorname{curl}H\|_U^2
 =\|c_h\|_U^2\le M A_h\le MB^2N_h^2.
 \tag{28}
\]
Substituting the two versions of (22) proves (T1) and (T2), respectively. The right sides do not contain h or any uncontrolled quotient of curl H. Taking the supremum is now justified.

The original-field test in Step 5 used only H(curl), the H1 estimate in Step 7 was derived independently, and the quotient test only used fixed-h H(curl) membership. There is no circular regularity assumption.

### Step 11. Weak tangential derivatives, if desired

The estimate also proves that partial_i(curl H) belongs to L2(U), with the same bound. This conclusion is not needed to obtain the uniform quotient estimate.

Indeed, choose h_n->0 with 0<|h_n|<h0. A bounded sequence in the Hilbert space L2(U;C3) has a weakly convergent subsequence. For any smooth compactly supported vector test phi in U, extend phi by zero; for all sufficiently small h its translated support lies inside V. Change of variables gives
\[
 \int_U\delta_{h,i}(\operatorname{curl}H)\cdot\overline\phi
 =-\int_V\operatorname{curl}H\cdot
                   \overline{\delta_{-h,i}\phi}.
\]
The test quotients converge to partial_i phi in L2(V), by smoothness and a fixed compact support bound. Passing to the weak limit identifies it as the distributional derivative. Weak lower semicontinuity preserves the norm bound.

The standard directional difference-quotient argument is also covered by Kinnunen, Sobolev Spaces, Section 2.5, Theorem 2.32:
https://math.aalto.fi/~jkkinnun/files/sobolev_spaces.pdf
The use of weak compactness here is only for this optional derivative conclusion; the div-curl regularity proof above used a Cauchy sequence instead.

## 3. Complex frequencies and uniform sequences

### 3.1 The identities that change, and those that do not

For complex k!=0, divergence still gives div H=0, and
\[
 W=-ikE=a\operatorname{curl}H,\qquad
 \operatorname{curl}W=k^2H
\]
remain exact complex identities. The norm identity becomes
\[
 a|\operatorname{curl}H|^2=|k|^2\epsilon|E|^2.
 \tag{29}
\]
Replacing |k|^2 here by k^2 is incorrect unless k is real.

In the Hermitian test calculation, the EXACT equality remains
\[
 A_\chi+2T_\chi=k^2Q_\chi.
\]
Its real part is
\[
 A_\chi=\operatorname{Re}(k^2)Q_\chi
           -2\operatorname{Re}T_\chi.
 \tag{30}
\]
There is no conjugation of k^2 in this convention: conjugation is on the test, and k^2 multiplies the un-conjugated field. If the opposite inner-product convention is used, the conjugated equation has the same real part.

Set lambda(k)=max(Re(k^2),0). Since Q_chi>=0, exactly the same absorption gives
\[
 A_\chi\le2\lambda(k)Q_\chi+4D_\chi.
 \tag{31}
\]
Both original-field and quotient energy estimates now use B(k). In the direct div-curl bound, ||curl H|| is bounded with |k|M||E||. The mollification, cutoff, translation, and coercivity arguments are unchanged because a is still positive real. This proves (T3) and (T4). It does not assert a real-frequency Poynting identity at complex frequency.

### 3.2 Uniform statement with correct quantifier order

Fix the patches, hence R,L,h0, and fix d, hence M. Let (k_j) be any complex sequence with
\[
 |k_j|\le K_0<\infty.
\]
For EACH j choose ANY actual regional pair E_j,H_j satisfying (M) with frequency k_j. Define
\[
 B_*=\sqrt{2K_0^2+64/R^2},\qquad
 C_{H,*}=MB_*^2+\sqrt M\,B_*L.
 \tag{32}
\]
Then for every j with k_j!=0, both tangential i, and all 0<|h|<h0,
\[
 \|\delta_{h,i}\operatorname{curl}H_j\|_U
 \le C_{H,*}\|H_j\|_V
 \le C_{H,*}(\|E_j\|_V+\|H_j\|_V).
 \tag{33}
\]
This follows from
\[
 \operatorname{Re}(k_j^2)
 =(\operatorname{Re}k_j)^2-(\operatorname{Im}k_j)^2
 \le |k_j|^2\le K_0^2.
\]

If k_j=0 for some index, (M) gives curl H_j=0, so (33) is trivially true there as well; div H_j=0 is not claimed for that index.

In particular, k_j->k_*!=0 supplies boundedness and a nonzero tail. No factor 1/|k_j| occurs in the constant. If additionally sup_j ||H_j||_V<=N, then the supremum over j and h in (33) is at most C_{H,*}N. A bounded frequency sequence alone does not assert bounded field norms.

If d varies with j, the same result is uniform when 0<=d_j<=D, replacing M by 1+D. The hypotheses do not give contrast-independent constants for unbounded d.

### 3.3 Why a dependence on material contrast is appropriate

For example, on fixed patches wholly in x3>0, take real k>0 and
\[
 H=e_2e^{ik\sqrt M x_1},\qquad
 E=-M^{-1/2}e_3e^{ik\sqrt M x_1}.
\]
Direct calculation verifies both Maxwell equations with epsilon=M. Yet
\[
 \|\partial_1\operatorname{curl}H\|_U=k^2M|U|^{1/2},
\]
whereas
\[
 \|E\|_V+\|H\|_V=(1+M^{-1/2})|V|^{1/2}.
\]
Thus no contrast-independent constant can hold throughout the class of local patches in the theorem. The H-only constant above grows at most linearly with M for fixed frequency and geometry. No optimal numerical constant is claimed.

theorem half_absorb
    (x y r : ℝ)
    (h : x ^ 2 ≤ r + 2 * x * y) :

    (α x y r : ℝ)

theorem complex_mass_real_bound

def WeakMaxwellC {Ω : Set Space} (k : ℂ) (ε : Space → ℝ)
    (E H : DomainCurl.HCurl Ω) : Prop :=
  (∀ᵐ x ∂DomainCurl.regionMeasure Ω, ∀ j : Fin 3,
    E.val.2 j x = Complex.I * k * H.val.1 j x) ∧
  (∀ᵐ x ∂DomainCurl.regionMeasure Ω, ∀ j : Fin 3,

  fun x j => u.val.1 j x

  fun x j => u.val.2 j x

  ∑ j : Fin 3, ∫ x in S, ‖F x j‖ ^ 2

  Real.sqrt (vectorL2Sq S F)

def difference (F : Field) (i : Fin 3) (h : ℝ) : Field :=
  fun x j => ((h : ℂ)⁻¹) * (F (x + h • basis i) j - F x j)

  openOmega : IsOpen Ω

  openV : IsOpen V
  nonemptyU : U.Nonempty

  radius_pos : 0 < r
