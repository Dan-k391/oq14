## PROVED: a flux-only dimension bound for entire propagating vacuum fields

Fix the same square `D=(-L/2,L/2)^2` in the measurement plane and real vacuum wavenumber `k>0`. Let `W` be any complex linear family of regular vacuum Maxwell fields generated by transverse `L2(S2)` Herglotz amplitudes. The Hilbert norm on `W` can be any fixed input norm; in particular no bound on the Herglotz amplitudes in terms of that norm is assumed. Suppose, for every coherent `a` in `W`, its real time-averaged normal flux density `s_a` satisfies

    P_D(a) = integral_D s_a >= p ||a||^2,     p>0,
    integral_(R2\D) |s_a| <= delta ||a||^2.

Then `dim W` has a finite bound depending only on `k,L,p,delta`. This statement does not assume passivity or bound the local electric or magnetic energy. It includes any finite family of regular vector spherical waves with arbitrary complex coefficients and arbitrarily large relative gains.

### Step 1: the planar flux has fixed bandwidth

Each physical electric and magnetic field component on the plane is the Fourier transform of a finite complex measure supported in the transverse disk of radius `k`: push forward its Herglotz measure under `d -> k d_perp`. The measure is finite because `L2(S2)` is contained in `L1(S2)`. Products with conjugate components correspond to convolutions of those measures and reflected conjugate measures. Therefore

    supp Fourier(s_a) subset K = {xi in R2: |xi| <= 2k}.

Each field is continuous, so `s_a` is integrable on the bounded square. The bypass hypothesis then implies `s_a in L1(R2)`. The claim consequently reduces to an elementary property of scalar `L1` bandlimited functions.

### Step 2: an elementary L1 observability inequality

For every fixed bounded square `D` and compact frequency disk `K`, there exists a finite constant `C=C(K,D)` such that

    ||f||_L1(R2) <= C ||f||_L1(R2\D)

for every complex `f in L1(R2)` whose Fourier transform is supported in `K`.

**Proof.** Use `fhat(xi)=integral f(x) exp(-i xi.x) dx` and inverse factor `(2pi)^(-2)`. If no such constant exists, choose `f_n` with total `L1` norm one and outside-square norm tending to zero. Their transforms are bounded in `Linf(K)` by one. Since `L1(K)` is separable, a subsequence converges weak-* to some `g in Linf(K)`. Fourier inversion gives pointwise convergence to

    f(x) = (2pi)^(-2) integral_K g(xi) exp(i xi.x) dxi.

Uniform bounds on the first derivatives, obtained by inserting `i xi` into this integral, make the convergence uniform on every compact set. For example, apply equicontinuity and a finite net to the pointwise convergence. The limit is zero on the open complement of the closed square: on every compact ball in that complement, its `L1` norm is zero by the outside-square hypothesis and local uniform convergence. The compact frequency support makes the integral defining `f` an entire function of two complex variables, so its real-analytic restriction vanishes everywhere. But uniform convergence on the closed square gives

    integral_D |f| = lim integral_D |f_n| = 1,

a contradiction. This proves a finite observability constant without a numerical optimal value.

This elementary compactness argument is all that is needed here; no quantitative uncertainty theorem is imported. The constant depends on bandwidth and the entire shape/size of the square, and is not asserted to scale like area.

### Step 3: a finite-rank point quadrature

Put `kappa=2k`. Fourier inversion gives the explicit derivative estimate

    ||grad f||_Linf <= D_kappa ||f||_L1,
    D_kappa = (2pi)^(-2) integral_(|xi|<=kappa) |xi| dxi
            = kappa^3/(6pi).

For `s_a`, Step 2 gives `||s_a||_L1 <= C delta ||a||^2`. Partition `D` into `m^2` equal squares and take their centers `x_j`. The midpoint quadrature obeys

    |integral_D s_a - (L^2/m^2) sum_j s_a(x_j)|
       <= [L^3 D_kappa C delta/(sqrt(2) m)] ||a||^2.

Choose the positive integer `m` so the bracket is strictly less than `p`; for example,

    m = 1 + floor(L^3 D_kappa C delta/(sqrt(2) p)).

At each node the normal flux depends only on the four complex tangential values `E_x,E_y,H_x,H_y`. If a finite-dimensional `W` had dimension greater than `4m^2`, the linear map taking an input to all these point values would have a nonzero kernel vector `a`. Its quadrature is zero. The error inequality would then give `P_D(a)<p||a||^2`, contradicting the assumed passage. Thus

    dim W <= 4m^2.

The argument also excludes an infinite-dimensional `W` by restriction to any finite-dimensional subspace of larger dimension. If `delta=0`, Step 2 directly implies `s_a=0` for every input, so the positive-passage space is zero dimensional.

### What this result does and does not say

The Herglotz coefficient norm does not occur in the proof. Arbitrarily large physical core amplification cannot evade the result if the entire regular vacuum continuation itself meets the global bypass condition. The result is a finiteness statement, not the conjectured area law. General local Maxwell solutions with evanescent transverse spectra do not satisfy the fixed bandwidth hypothesis; the earlier local packet construction is therefore fully compatible with it. Actual dielectric fields on a plane passing through material need not satisfy that hypothesis either.

## PROVED: obstruction to sending a fixed regular-wave target arbitrarily far from its controls

Suppose a sequence of devices is vacuum on balls of radius `R_n -> infinity`, and on a fixed finite-dimensional input space their fields converge uniformly on every compact subset of the measurement plane to a family of entire regular vacuum Herglotz fields. Suppose their actual fields satisfy the same positive passage `p` and absolute bypass `delta` for all inputs. Then the limiting family satisfies exactly the hypotheses above.

Indeed, compact convergence passes `P_D>=p||a||^2` to the limit. Fatou's lemma on the complement of `D` gives `integral |s_a| <= delta||a||^2`, since flux converges pointwise for each fixed input. Apply the bandwidth/quadrature theorem. Hence no such fixed target family of arbitrarily large dimension can meet fixed passage and bypass thresholds merely by moving increasingly accurate controls to infinity.

This conditional limiting statement does not bound arbitrary varying target fields whose coefficients have no locally convergent propagating vacuum limit. It also does not bound a device with nearby material. It is an obstruction to a specific proposed completion of remote transfer control, not a proof of the unrestricted channel conjecture.

## Preliminary check of the proposed amplified drift loop

For TE states `v=(u,u')`, the generator is `A_n + (nu/x^2)Y`, where `A_n=[[0,1],[-n^2,0]]`, `Y=[[0,0],[1,0]]`, and `nu=l(l+1)`. A common reference identity loop with prefix `U_0` has first-order interaction drift

    B_M = integral U_0(x)^(-1) Y U_0(x) dx.

For `U_0=[[a,b],[c,d]]`, determinant one gives the exact identity

    U_0^(-1) Y U_0 = [[-ab,-b^2],[a^2,ab]].

Thus the lower-left entry of `B_M` is nonnegative and its upper-right entry nonpositive. For the stack orientation with a large first-coordinate prefix, the expected dominant lower-left scale is `q^(2M)`. The opposite orientation would suppress this entry; the exact layer order is essential.

If the precise loop satisfies `B_M/q^(2M) -> bY` with `b>0`, its prefix norm is `O(q^M)`, and its length is `O(M)`, then the proposed asymptotic powers are consistent. Set `q^M ~ R^alpha`, with `1/2<alpha<1`, and repeat `K=O(R^(2-2alpha))` loops. Their total length is `O(R^(2-2alpha) log R)=o(R)`. A per-loop interaction bound `O(M q^(2M)/R^2)` yields an accumulated second-order error of order `O(M^2 R^(2alpha-2))`, tending to zero. Relative radius variation is `O(R^(1-2alpha) log R)`, also tending to zero. These estimates explain why both restrictions on `alpha` are needed. Exact constants, centering corrections, and any endpoint errors still require the author's final loop formula.

## Sign and reachability audit still needed

Positive loop length directly supplies positive-time `exp(t nu Y)`. It does not directly supply negative drift time. A plausible legitimate repair is to conjugate by a common quarter-turn to obtain `exp(-t nu X)`, then use the compact flow generated by `nu(Y-X)`. Since every `nu=l(l+1)` is an integer, that simultaneous rotation is periodic and its negative times can be represented by positive times. Product limits can then recover both signs of the separate weighted shears. This must be written as a sequence of finite approximating layer words, with accuracy fixed before taking the large-radius limit; it is not an exact inverse from reversed physical time.

Once both signs of common `sl(2)` generators and their `nu`-weighted versions are available in the closure, commutators produce polynomial powers of the distinct `nu_j`. Vandermonde interpolation separates the finite angular orders and gives the full Lie algebra `sl(2)^m`. The passage from this algebra to approximate transfer control must retain the semigroup/group justification, the connected component of `SL(2,R)^m`, and material positivity. A Lie-rank statement alone does not establish whole-plane power passage or bypass.

## Stronger final bandwidth theorem with explicit constants

The manuscript improves the compactness and quadrature proof above in two ways.

First, it supplies a self-contained explicit observation constant, avoiding a nonquantitative compactness constant or an imported uncertainty theorem. For a one-dimensional bandwidth interval `[-kappa,kappa]` and missing spatial interval of length `L`, take

    n = 2 + ceil(4 e kappa L),
    C_(kappa,L) = 2[1+2n(16e)^(n-1)].

Choose `n` nodes by `L1` averaging in separated half-bins of the adjacent outside interval. Their Lagrange coefficient sum on the missing interval is at most `(16e)^(n-1)`. The bandlimited derivative bound `||f^(n)||infty <= kappa^(n+1)||f||1/[pi(n+1)]` makes the integrated interpolation remainder at most

    ||f||1 [kappa L/(pi(n+1))] (2kappa L)^n/n! < ||f||1/2.

Absorbing this remainder proves the stated constant. Complex values use the divided-difference integral remainder, not a complex-valued mean-value theorem. Fubini and the partial Fourier support property allow the one-dimensional estimate to be applied on horizontal slices; the observed strip lies outside the square.

Second, a pointwise trace estimate replaces the quadrature count. Fourier inversion gives `|s_a(x)| <= kappa^2 C delta ||a||^2/(4pi)`. On any finite-dimensional counted input space, the point-flux Hermitian matrix factors through four complex tangential field components and therefore has rank at most four. Its positive trace is at most four times this operator-norm bound. Integrating the trace over `D` and using positive passage on an orthonormal basis gives the explicit bound

    dim W <= A kappa^2 C_(kappa,L) delta/(pi p),   kappa=2k.

This is finite, with no field-energy or Herglotz-norm bound, and no condition `delta<p`. The exponential constant means it is not an area-scaling theorem. The earlier compactness and quadrature proof remains a valid independent conceptual check.

## Stronger fixed-band finite-radius obstruction

The channel author independently observed that a fixed angular band permits a finite-radius version without assuming fixed target coefficients. The actual scalar flux function space spanned by all Hermitian cross-products of that finite regular-wave basis is finite-dimensional in `L1(R2)`. Each cross-flux is `O(r^-3)` on the plane: the leading spherical far-field electric amplitudes are transverse to the planar radial direction, and their magnetic partners are radial cross-products, so every leading `r^-2` cross-Poynting vector is radial and has zero normal component. Smoothness handles the origin.

Compactness of this function space's `L1` unit sphere gives a radius `R_J` beyond which every tail is at most `||f||1/(2C)`. The global observation estimate then absorbs that tail and bounds full `L1` by twice `C` times the actual bypass in the vacuum-core disk. The same trace argument gives

    dim W <= 2 A kappa^2 C_(kappa,L) delta/(pi p)

for any device whose vacuum core contains `B_(R_J)` and whose core fields use only the prescribed angular band. Gains and phases may vary arbitrarily. The radius threshold depends on the band, so this does not give an unrestricted statement when band and control radius change together.
