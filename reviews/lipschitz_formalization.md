The actual approximation is convolution of a scalar Lipschitz function on Euclidean three-space with the existing explicit shrinking normalized bumps. `mollify_lipschitz_partial` proves that each classical directional derivative of the convolution equals convolution of the actual line derivative. Its essential bridge is pinned mathlib's `LipschitzWith.integral_lineDeriv_mul_eq`, applied to the reflected smooth kernel. The negative reflection derivative and the negative integration-by-parts direction cancel. No weak-derivative or density property is assumed for the Lipschitz function.

The source separately proves uniform derivative bounds, a.e. derivative convergence by genuine differentiation of locally integrable functions, compact support of convolution, and eventual containment of that support in the original open region. Support control uses the actual convolution support inclusion and a compact-set thickening inside an open set. No uniform distance to a physical material boundary is required beyond compact interior support of the test.

`mollify_directional_pair_tendsto` is dominated convergence against an arbitrary actual L1 scalar field, with the explicit dominating function `C * norm(v) * norm(g(x))`. The original C1 distributional divergence identity applies to sufficiently small mollifiers. Its integral limit proves `lipschitz_weak_divergence`. Rademacher's a.e. differentiability theorem identifies the line derivatives with the actual Frechet gradient and independently establishes integrability of the final pairing.

The physical corollaries `lipschitz_maxwell_real_conservation` and `lipschitz_maxwell_cross_conservation` specialize this theorem to genuine regional weak Maxwell fields. The latter gives the single complex integral of the actual gradient dotted with the Hermitian two-input flux, with independent L1 integrability. Coefficients remain real functions and require no derivatives; no global field L2 condition, boundary trace, scattering solvability, or assumed smooth-density theorem is introduced.
