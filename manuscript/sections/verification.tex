\section{Verification, remaining lemmas, and separate verdicts}
\label{ver:section}

\subsection{What was checked}
The hotspot, channel, and counterexample tracks used disjoint source-file
ownership. A different research agent reviewed each track's key arguments,
and the coordinator checked their equations and scope. The review found and
corrected the direction of the bound on the real parts of the outgoing
Dirichlet-to-Neumann multipliers, and made explicit the derivative
asymptotic used in the radial angular-momentum obstruction. These are
recorded in the review files and Git history. This is independent checking
within an AI-assisted investigation, not independent human peer review or
machine-checked continuum mathematics.

The proofs use standard Hilbert-space spectral theory, Lax--Milgram,
Fredholm theory, Sobolev compactness and local elliptic estimates. The
scalar scattering proof supplies its own outgoing uniqueness and
material-Runge argument. Its elementary Bessel/Hankel facts can be checked
against the Wronskian identities and large-order formulas in
\cite{dlmf}. None of these background facts is a substitute for a
Maxwell-to-operator bridge in the channel problem.

The reproducible script \texttt{scripts/verify.py} checks the exact pair
model, passive reciprocal perturbations, the angular monodromy, radial
Bragg matching, the conical projection identities, and local evanescent
flux. Its fifteen diagnostic groups use fixed seed $140014$ where
random perturbations are needed.  The additional modal groups check
the exact sphere determinant against its rational exponential form,
the first asymptotic coefficient and nine high-$Q$ dipole poles,
and the constant-lifting cancellation through exponent one.
The newest groups integrate the actual TE/TM radial systems for the
growing-stack construction with step refinement, check the exact
real-frequency dynamic-kernel bound, and independently integrate the
conjugated angular generator through each layer of the identity loop.
The last calculation includes both actual TE and TM interaction matrices.
Another group computes source reaction coefficients from the actual
regular/irregular transfer matrices of a common finite stack and checks
their paired large-radius limit.
They do not certify global channel
synthesis or the continuous error bounds used in the proofs.
All matrix tests use singular values or Hermitian eigenvalues, not tests
of randomly selected coherent inputs. Random numbers choose one fully
specified perturbation matrix; its full norm and all-subspace certificate
are then evaluated. Floating-point residuals support consistency of the
analytic proofs; they are not interval-certified error estimates.

The ideal model's energy Gram matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(J_j^{-1})$
and its internal exchange-current Gram matrix is $I$; common detuning is
zero. For weighted, purely Hamiltonian perturbations of norm $\rho=0.01$,
Theorem~\ref{cx:n-model} bounds transmission-operator error by
$0.0100713$. This is below $1-\sqrt{0.9}$, so it certifies transmission
above $90\%$ in the perturbed model for every coherent vector. The
reproduced smallest powers are approximately $0.999961$, $0.999959$, and
$0.999949$. Model power-balance and full-unitarity residuals are below
$10^{-14}$ in the reported run.

The local evanescent example has an analytically exact section Gram matrix
$I_8$. Direct integration of its explicit trigonometric fields with
$33$, $65$ and $129$ transverse quadrature points gives operator errors
below $10^{-14}$. This refines quadrature of a local analytic formula; it
is not a domain, port, or material-mesh convergence study. The Bragg
diagnostic uses $q=2$, $k_0=1$, $x_0\simeq2.74370727$ and material upper
bound below $4.266$. For $M=0,2,4,8$ its core amplitude squared is
approximately $8$, $115.292$, $1824.323$, $464888.834$, while reflection
power remains one. These tests check normalization and exact matching,
not an increasing transmission count.

No numerical simulation of the analytic physical $N$-channel family in
Theorem~\ref{uc:counterexample} was performed. Therefore numerical
Maxwell transmission singular values, section-flux matrices, absolute
bypass, weakest physical coupling, and relative model-reduction errors
for $N=2,4,8$ are \emph{unavailable}, not zero. Their fields in the
machine-readable record are null. No rounded-hotspot scattering mesh was
computed; that theorem rests on the analytic existence and convergence
proof, which is nonconstructive in its illumination cost and rounding
scale. The supplied notes' claims about earlier finite-dimensional Lean
checks were not rerun. A new formalization is described below, with its
own source, pinned dependencies, successful compilation, and axiom audit.

The additional eighteen declarations in namespace \texttt{OQ14.Maxwell}
use genuine real Fr\'echet derivatives on Euclidean three-space,
complex vector fields, and Lebesgue integrals. They include the product
rule for $\operatorname{div}(E\times H)$, curl and conjugation,
the complex Poynting identity and real local conservation,
$\operatorname{curl}\nabla=0$, $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}=0$,
and the homogeneous curl--curl Maxwell equation. Compact-test integration
by parts proves that classical $C^1$ Maxwell fields satisfy the
distributional curl equations; compact support discharges integrability
rather than leaving it as an unexplained assumption.

Explicit differentiable plane waves and growing and decaying evanescent
waves satisfy the actual curl equations. Each of the latter has zero
normal real power everywhere, whereas a particular coherent pair is a
Maxwell solution with constant positive normal power $3/4$ everywhere
in normalized units. This is a local-field example: infinite transverse
extent and exponential growth preclude treating it as a finite-power
physical input. The formal source and its README list every exported
name, hypothesis and scope.

Eleven further declarations in \texttt{OQ14.WeakCurl} construct the
actual whole-space $L^2$ distributional curl graph. Bounded test
functionals are identified with Lebesgue integrals; their kernels form
a closed complex subspace, giving a complete graph norm. Compact tests
separate the $L^2$ classes, so the weak curl is unique. Simultaneous
$L^2$ limits preserve the relation, and classical $C^1$ fields with
field and curl in $L^2$ belong to the graph.

The twelve declarations in \texttt{OQ14.DomainCurl} extend this
construction to restricted Lebesgue measure and tests compactly
supported inside an open region. No global $L^2$ or zero boundary
condition is imposed. The classical bridge assumes global $C^1$
regularity but requires $L^2$ only on the region. Its final theorem
proves zero distributional divergence of a weak curl, using compact
$C^2$ tests and symmetry of actual mixed derivatives; neither field
nor curl is assumed differentiable. The inherited norms are maximum
coordinate graph norms, not electromagnetic energy normalizations.

The eleven declarations in \texttt{OQ14.CurlCutoff} prove actual
$L^2$ multiplier bounds, the distributional rule
$\operatorname{curl}(\chi E)=\chi\operatorname{curl}E+
\nabla\chi\times E$, and its graph-norm estimate for compact $C^1$
cutoffs. Isometric zero extension and its integral pairing are
constructed explicitly. When the cutoff is supported inside the open
region, its field and product-rule curl extend to the whole-space
weak-curl graph. No boundary condition on the original field or
unrestricted zero-extension theorem is assumed.

Sixteen declarations in \texttt{OQ14.WeakPoynting} construct genuine
smooth approximation in the whole-space weak-curl graph. Convolution
smoothness and curl commutation follow from the actual integral and
translated compact tests. Strong continuity of $L^2$ translations gives
convergence of their Bochner bump averages. An absolute product-space
estimate justifies Fubini and identifies these averages with the smooth
pointwise convolution. Explicit shrinking bumps then prove graph-norm
convergence, without assuming a density theorem.

Six declarations in \texttt{OQ14.WeakGreen} use that proved approximation
to establish the local bilinear Green identity
\[
 \int \chi(\operatorname{curl}E\cdot F-E\cdot\operatorname{curl}F)
       +\nabla\chi\cdot(E\times F)=0
\]
for arbitrary whole-space $H(\operatorname{curl})$ fields and compact
$C^1$ tests. Every pairing is an actual integrable $L^2$ product.
The next seventeen declarations establish conjugation of weak curl,
the actual complex Poynting identity, and its localization to arbitrary
open regions. A smooth interior cutoff equal to one near the test support
is constructed explicitly, so the original fields need no global
$L^2$ condition or boundary condition. For the actual regional weak
Maxwell equations with real variable coefficients, the reactive term
is purely imaginary. The real time-averaged flux therefore has zero
distributional divergence against every compact real $C^1$ test.
This verifies the weak conservation step in Lemma~\ref{ch:fluxlemma};
its continuous plane-slice representatives and trace evaluations remain
outside this formal coverage.

Sixteen declarations in \texttt{OQ14.FluxSlices} prove the actual
volume-preserving coordinates separating $z$ from the transverse plane,
Fubini, $L^1$ slice integrability and the compact tensor-test derivative
calculation. Applied to regional weak Maxwell fields, they establish
the one-dimensional weak derivative relation for the aperture-integrated
flux, retaining the transverse-gradient term. The slices are a.e.
integral functions; no representative or evaluation trace is assumed.

Twenty-five declarations in \texttt{OQ14.ModalAlgebra} define the finite
Riccati--Hankel Laurent expressions, prove their actual complex
derivatives, and derive the exact TM determinant factorization, rational
pole equation and multiple-zero derivative condition. The degree-one
Wronskian and coefficient nonvanishing are checked explicitly. The
general-degree identities, transcendence, pole existence and Fredholm
multiplicity identification are not thereby formalized.

Twenty-four declarations in \texttt{OQ14.RadialGreen} verify outgoing
continuation, the complex source coefficient, its strictly negative
imaginary part and gain identity, and the source-load Wronskian
cancellation. The radial ODEs give the load and state derivatives and
barrier monotonicity on a closed interval. The full partial-wave
expansion and all-angular-order convergence remain separate obligations.

Ten declarations in \texttt{OQ14.StaticVolume} prove positivity and
inverse estimates for the actual Hilbert-space block $I+S^*PS$,
including actual orthogonal projections, perturbations of norm below one,
and the polarization residual error bound. Identification of the
projection with the physical longitudinal Newton operator is still an
analytic formalization obligation.

For every audited declaration the compiler lists three axioms: propositional
extensionality (\texttt{propext}), classical choice
(\texttt{Classical.choice}), and quotient soundness (\texttt{Quot.sound}).
These are the standard logical foundations used by these mathlib proofs. No
project-specific axiom, \texttt{sorry}, or admitted proof occurs. The
formal build records the exact source hashes and pinned dependencies.
This formally verifies the listed operator, differential and integral
theorems. It does not yet formalize weak-field existence, conical or corner
analysis, scattering density, interface rounding, the analytic Fredholm
pencil, modal normalization, or the missing uniform channel approximation.
In particular the full mathematical research problem cannot be closed by
adding its missing assertions as axioms.

\subsection{Smallest remaining research bridges}
\label{ver:gaps}
For a channel proof, Theorem~\ref{ch:fulloutput} already controls the
complete physical output error after all feedback. What is missing is
a uniform estimate of the effective transmission directions with no
uncontrolled field-resource factor. A bound on $B_*$ on the counted
subspaces would suffice for finiteness in the stated collar class, but
the Bragg construction shows why external passivity cannot supply a
general input-to-field bound. It does not rule out a sharper global
argument using radiative accessibility, through-power and bypass together.
Theorem~\ref{ca:localpackets} proves that local Maxwell equations and
whole-plane coherent bypass control alone cannot supply the required
restriction. The minimal abstract target is
the uniform finite-rank approximation in Remark~\ref{ch:missing};
its area scaling is an additional requirement.
Corollary~\ref{nr:channelcount} supplies a physical uniform resource
bound when total dielectric volume is sufficiently small, without a
support-diameter bound. That additional volume restriction is absent
from the primary target. Theorem~\ref{cg:stack-count} separately gives
an area bound in any prescribed finite angular band for the growing
radial-stack construction: opposite-parity gains cancel in signed
passage, even while local field energy diverges on large physical
subspaces. Modes above the prescribed band remain outside that bound.
Theorem~\ref{cn:radial-bypass} now excludes arbitrary high angular
degeneracy in a centered homogeneous core, even with unlimited exterior
resonant amplification and both polarizations. It leaves mixed angular
orders and structured cores outside its scope.

For a disproof, Remark~\ref{cx:missing-bridge} specifies a quantitative
alternative: a power-normalized Maxwell compression close to a unitary
at one common frequency, plus the full physical flux inequality and
absolute-bypass certificate on every coherent vector. Resonant algebra
proves that weak coupling alone is not an obstruction, but it gives
neither simultaneous bounded-index synthesis nor a relative error
estimate for finite dielectric structures.
Theorem~\ref{cn:local-synthesis} does provide actual bounded-index
radial controls for small independent changes of finitely many complex
core amplitudes. Its explicitly retained local range is not a global
reachability or absolute-bypass theorem.
Theorem~\ref{cc:global-control} now supplies approximate global
reachability for every fixed finite set of TE orders, using amplified
angular perturbations in distant positive-index layers. The complex core
gains can be independently prescribed up to common phase.
Theorem~\ref{cd:paired-control} also controls the paired TM transfers,
and Theorem~\ref{cd:wall-isolation} amplifies one reflection sector while
suppressing its opposite in a fixed $C_2$-even band. It does not estimate
the response to a newly added inclusion or unselected angular orders.
Separately,
Theorem~\ref{cb:count} bounds counted dimension for any entire propagating
Herglotz family using only its absolute bypass, without a gain bound.
Corollary~\ref{cb:finitecore} transfers this obstruction to actual devices
with a sufficiently large vacuum core at each fixed finite angular band.
Its radius threshold may depend on the band. Thus neither result controls
nearby material or arbitrary simultaneous growth of angular band and
radius. Nearby material is essential to the subsequent counterexample.
Theorem~\ref{cp:rank} demonstrates why it is essential: actual
finite positive dielectrics can have arbitrary coherent passage rank
and unit total throughput when bypass is omitted. Section~\ref{ct:section}
constructs a local edge-circulation profile with equal passage and return,
and exact finite-band jets with orthonormal physical inputs. Its
Proposition~\ref{ct:completion} states the sufficient whole-plane $L^1$
Maxwell remainder. Theorem~\ref{cl:remainder} proves the self-consistent
small-inclusion estimate for the explicit limiting kernel, and
Theorem~\ref{rg:physical-limit} proves the all-order physical Green limit
and whole-plane convergence for finite radial backgrounds.
Theorem~\ref{uc:counterexample} combines them in the required order
to disprove unrestricted finiteness for every fixed $\delta>\eta>0$.
The selected output is the full scattering map; no claim is made here
for stronger bypass thresholds or a prescribed receiving hemisphere.

For hotspots, Theorem~\ref{hs:three-packing} supplies the finite-device
driven construction directly, using conical points rather than an
extrusion approximation. Its neighborhood projections lie in the
prescribed planar patch; it does not require the rounded maxima to lie
exactly in that plane. The separate modal construction now closes the
modal bridge: Theorem~\ref{ms:modal-packing} produces one high-$Q$
resonance with a finite nonzero reciprocal residue norm, all singular
coefficients nonzero, and smooth rounded peaks with actual maxima in the
plane patch. The insertion contrast may depend on $N$, within the same
fixed material bounds. The surrounding resonator's support is unrestricted.
