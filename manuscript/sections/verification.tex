\section{Verification, remaining lemmas, and separate verdicts}
\label{ver:section}

\subsection{What was checked}
The hotspot, channel, and counterexample tracks used disjoint source-file
ownership. A different research agent reviewed each track's key arguments,
and the coordinator checked their equations and scope. The review found and
corrected the direction of the bound on the real parts of the outgoing
Dirichlet-to-Neumann multipliers, and made explicit the derivative
asymptotic used in the radial angular-momentum obstruction. These are
recorded in the review files and Git history. This is independent checking
within an AI-assisted investigation, not independent human peer review or
machine-checked continuum mathematics.

The proofs use standard Hilbert-space spectral theory, Lax--Milgram,
Fredholm theory, Sobolev compactness and local elliptic estimates. The
scalar scattering proof supplies its own outgoing uniqueness and
material-Runge argument. Its elementary Bessel/Hankel facts can be checked
against the Wronskian identities and large-order formulas in
\cite{dlmf}. None of these background facts is a substitute for a
Maxwell-to-operator bridge in the channel problem.

The reproducible script \texttt{scripts/verify.py} checks the exact pair
model, passive reciprocal perturbations, the angular monodromy, radial
Bragg matching, and local evanescent flux. Its fixed seed is $140014$.
All matrix tests use singular values or Hermitian eigenvalues, not tests
of randomly selected coherent inputs. Random numbers choose one fully
specified perturbation matrix; its full norm and all-subspace certificate
are then evaluated. Floating-point residuals support consistency of the
analytic proofs; they are not interval-certified error estimates.

The ideal model's energy Gram matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(J_j^{-1})$
and its internal exchange-current Gram matrix is $I$; common detuning is
zero. For weighted, purely Hamiltonian perturbations of norm $\rho=0.01$,
Theorem~\ref{cx:n-model} bounds transmission-operator error by
$0.0100713$. This is below $1-\sqrt{0.9}$, so it certifies transmission
above $90\%$ in the perturbed model for every coherent vector. The
reproduced smallest powers are approximately $0.999961$, $0.999959$, and
$0.999949$. Model power-balance and full-unitarity residuals are below
$10^{-14}$ in the reported run.

The local evanescent example has an analytically exact section Gram matrix
$I_8$. Direct integration of its explicit trigonometric fields with
$33$, $65$ and $129$ transverse quadrature points gives operator errors
below $10^{-14}$. This refines quadrature of a local analytic formula; it
is not a domain, port, or material-mesh convergence study. The Bragg
diagnostic uses $q=2$, $k_0=1$, $x_0\simeq2.74370727$ and material upper
bound below $4.266$. For $M=0,2,4,8$ its core amplitude squared is
approximately $8$, $115.292$, $1824.323$, $464888.834$, while reflection
power remains one. These tests check normalization and exact matching,
not an increasing transmission count.

No physical $N$-channel dielectric family was completed. Therefore actual
Maxwell transmission singular values, section-flux matrices, absolute
bypass, weakest physical coupling, and relative model-reduction errors
for $N=2,4,8$ are \emph{unavailable}, not zero. Their fields in the
machine-readable record are null. No rounded-hotspot scattering mesh was
computed; that theorem rests on the analytic existence and convergence
proof, which is nonconstructive in its illumination cost and rounding
scale. No Lean formalization was performed. The supplied notes' claims
about earlier finite-dimensional Lean checks were not rerun and do not
certify any continuum result here.

\subsection{Smallest remaining research bridges}
\label{ver:gaps}
For a channel proof, Theorem~\ref{ch:fulloutput} already controls the
complete physical output error after all feedback. What is missing is
a uniform estimate of the effective transmission directions with no
uncontrolled field-resource factor. A bound on $B_*$ on the counted
subspaces would suffice for finiteness in the stated collar class, but
the Bragg construction shows why external passivity cannot supply a
general input-to-field bound. It does not rule out a sharper argument
using through-power and bypass together. The minimal abstract target is
the uniform finite-rank approximation in Remark~\ref{ch:missing};
its area scaling is an additional requirement.

For a disproof, Remark~\ref{cx:missing-bridge} specifies a quantitative
alternative: a power-normalized Maxwell compression close to a unitary
at one common frequency, plus the full physical flux inequality and
absolute-bypass certificate on every coherent vector. Resonant algebra
proves that weak coupling alone is not an obstruction, but it gives
neither simultaneous bounded-index synthesis nor a relative error
estimate for finite dielectric structures.

For hotspots, the restricted theorem has no remaining local-to-global
excitation assumption. The finite-three-dimensional and modal bridges
are those in Remark~\ref{hs:open3d}: vector scattering control under
finite-height realization and a valid mode with all required singular
coefficients and normalization. Infinite extrusion and freely chosen
illumination do not discharge these bridges.

\subsection{Build and provenance}
The canonical source is \texttt{manuscript/main.tex} and its included
sections. The documented build is \texttt{python scripts/build.py}
with Tectonic~0.17.0, followed or preceded by
\texttt{python scripts/verify.py}. Tectonic runs the required bibliography
and reference passes. The build script rejects unresolved final-pass
citations, references and overfull boxes. Rendered pages are inspected
separately; compilation never counts as mathematical verification.
The delivered \texttt{output/build-manifest.json} records the exact
source commit, clean/dirty state, engine, source hashes and PDF hash;
\texttt{output/verification.json} records the analogous diagnostic
provenance. The release record supplies the clean-checkout build result,
branch, tag, and Git bundle. No repository was published.
