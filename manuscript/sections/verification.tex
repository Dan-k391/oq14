\section{Verification, remaining lemmas, and separate verdicts}
\label{ver:section}

\subsection{What was checked}
The hotspot, channel, and counterexample tracks used disjoint source-file
ownership. A different research agent reviewed each track's key arguments,
and the coordinator checked their equations and scope. The review found and
corrected the direction of the bound on the real parts of the outgoing
Dirichlet-to-Neumann multipliers, and made explicit the derivative
asymptotic used in the radial angular-momentum obstruction. These are
recorded in the review files and Git history. This is independent checking
within an AI-assisted investigation, not independent human peer review or
machine-checked continuum mathematics.

The proofs use standard Hilbert-space spectral theory, Lax--Milgram,
Fredholm theory, Sobolev compactness and local elliptic estimates. The
scalar scattering proof supplies its own outgoing uniqueness and
material-Runge argument. Its elementary Bessel/Hankel facts can be checked
against the Wronskian identities and large-order formulas in
\cite{dlmf}. None of these background facts is a substitute for a
Maxwell-to-operator bridge in the channel problem.

The reproducible script \texttt{scripts/verify.py} checks the exact pair
model, passive reciprocal perturbations, the angular monodromy, radial
Bragg matching, the conical projection identities, and local evanescent
flux. Its fifteen diagnostic groups use fixed seed $140014$ where
random perturbations are needed.  The additional modal groups check
the exact sphere determinant against its rational exponential form,
the first asymptotic coefficient and nine high-$Q$ dipole poles,
and the constant-lifting cancellation through exponent one.
The newest groups integrate the actual TE/TM radial systems for the
growing-stack construction with step refinement, check the exact
real-frequency dynamic-kernel bound, and independently integrate the
conjugated angular generator through each layer of the identity loop.
The last calculation includes both actual TE and TM interaction matrices.
Another group computes source reaction coefficients from the actual
regular/irregular transfer matrices of a common finite stack and checks
their paired large-radius limit.
They do not certify global channel
synthesis or the continuous error bounds used in the proofs.
All matrix tests use singular values or Hermitian eigenvalues, not tests
of randomly selected coherent inputs. Random numbers choose one fully
specified perturbation matrix; its full norm and all-subspace certificate
are then evaluated. Floating-point residuals support consistency of the
analytic proofs; they are not interval-certified error estimates.

No numerical simulation of the analytic physical $N$-channel family in
Theorem~\ref{uc:counterexample} was performed. Therefore numerical
Maxwell transmission singular values, section-flux matrices, absolute
bypass, weakest physical coupling, and relative model-reduction errors
for $N=2,4,8$ are \emph{unavailable}, not zero. Their fields in the
machine-readable record are null. No rounded-hotspot scattering mesh was

proof, which is nonconstructive in its illumination cost and rounding
scale. The supplied notes' claims about earlier finite-dimensional Lean
checks were not rerun. A new formalization is described below, with its
own source, pinned dependencies, successful compilation, and axiom audit.

\subsection{Smallest remaining research bridges}
\label{ver:gaps}
The unrestricted assertion has been disproved in
Theorem~\ref{uc:counterexample} for every $\delta>\eta>0$ with full
scattering output. The following resource estimates describe stronger
restricted finiteness questions and the routes leading to that disproof.
For a restricted channel proof, Theorem~\ref{ch:fulloutput} controls the
complete physical output error after all feedback. What is missing is
a uniform estimate of the effective transmission directions with no
uncontrolled field-resource factor. A bound on $B_*$ on the counted
subspaces would suffice for finiteness in the stated collar class, but
the Bragg construction shows why external passivity cannot supply a
general input-to-field bound. It does not rule out a sharper global
argument using radiative accessibility, through-power and bypass together.
Theorem~\ref{ca:localpackets} proves that local Maxwell equations and
whole-plane coherent bypass control alone cannot supply the required
restriction. The minimal abstract target is
the uniform finite-rank approximation in Remark~\ref{ch:missing};
its area scaling is an additional requirement.
Corollary~\ref{nr:channelcount} supplies a physical uniform resource
bound when total dielectric volume is sufficiently small, without a
support-diameter bound. That additional volume restriction is absent
from the primary target. Theorem~\ref{cg:stack-count} separately gives
an area bound in any prescribed finite angular band for the growing
radial-stack construction: opposite-parity gains cancel in signed
passage, even while local field energy diverges on large physical
subspaces. Modes above the prescribed band remain outside that bound.
Theorem~\ref{cn:radial-bypass} now excludes arbitrary high angular
degeneracy in a centered homogeneous core, even with unlimited exterior
resonant amplification and both polarizations. It leaves mixed angular
orders and structured cores outside its scope.

The earlier disproof criterion in Remark~\ref{cx:missing-bridge} specifies a quantitative
alternative: a power-normalized Maxwell compression close to a unitary
at one common frequency, plus the full physical flux inequality and
absolute-bypass certificate on every coherent vector. Resonant algebra
proves that weak coupling alone is not an obstruction, but it gives
neither simultaneous bounded-index synthesis nor a relative error
estimate for finite dielectric structures.
Theorem~\ref{cn:local-synthesis} does provide actual bounded-index
radial controls for small independent changes of finitely many complex
core amplitudes. Its explicitly retained local range is not a global
reachability or absolute-bypass theorem.
Theorem~\ref{cc:global-control} now supplies approximate global
reachability for every fixed finite set of TE orders, using amplified
angular perturbations in distant positive-index layers. The complex core
gains can be independently prescribed up to common phase.
Theorem~\ref{cd:paired-control} also controls the paired TM transfers,
and Theorem~\ref{cd:wall-isolation} amplifies one reflection sector while
suppressing its opposite in a fixed $C_2$-even band. It does not estimate
the response to a newly added inclusion or unselected angular orders.
Separately,
Theorem~\ref{cb:count} bounds counted dimension for any entire propagating
Herglotz family using only its absolute bypass, without a gain bound.
Corollary~\ref{cb:finitecore} transfers this obstruction to actual devices
with a sufficiently large vacuum core at each fixed finite angular band.
Its radius threshold may depend on the band. Thus neither result controls
nearby material or arbitrary simultaneous growth of angular band and
radius. Nearby material is essential to the subsequent counterexample.
Theorem~\ref{cp:rank} demonstrates why it is essential: actual
finite positive dielectrics can have arbitrary coherent passage rank

Theorem~\ref{rg:physical-limit} proves the all-order physical Green limit
and whole-plane convergence for finite radial backgrounds.
