\section{Disproof of the unrestricted fixed-section channel bound}
\label{uc:section}

The construction in this section exploits the distinction between power
crossing a chosen square and power delivered to a receiver on its far side.
Near an edge, a circulating field can cross into the square and return
outside it.  The positive contribution inside the square counts as passage;
the return flow contributes to absolute bypass.  The definition permits this
provided both are within their prescribed thresholds.  Selecting the complete
outgoing scattering map also counts reflected radiation in the output power.

The construction makes this picture work simultaneously for any prescribed
finite number of coherent input directions.  Small dielectric spheres near
paired edges produce localized circulation.  A distant radial stack amplifies
the incident fields that drive them, compensating for their shrinking size.
The stack and spheres are ordinary finite dielectrics; their size and number
of layers may depend on the requested dimension.  A uniform estimate for all
superpositions, including interference terms and the whole measurement plane,
connects this picture to the actual Maxwell fields.

The unrestricted target quantifies over all fixed passage and bypass
thresholds in Definition~\ref{pr:count}. The following counterexample
uses a permitted range of those thresholds. It does not address the
different question obtained by requiring $\delta\leq\eta$, or by
restricting the selected outputs to a specified receiving hemisphere.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrarily large counted subspaces at fixed thresholds]
\label{uc:counterexample}
Fix $k_0>0$, $\eps_+>1$, $L>0$, $0<\tau<1$, and
$0<\eta<\delta$. For every positive integer $N$ there is a finite,
piecewise smooth, real scalar dielectric with
$1\leq\eps_r\leq\eps_+$, vacuum exterior, and no interior sources,
whose physical incoming space contains an $N$-dimensional counted
subspace through the same square
$D=(-L/2,L/2)^2\times\{0\}$. The selected outgoing map is its
complete scattering map. In particular, every vector in this subspace
has unit power throughput relative to its incident power, passage at
least $\eta$ times that power, and absolute bypass at most $\delta$
times that power. All plane fluxes are integrable.

Consequently the unrestricted finiteness assertion in
Conjecture~\ref{pr:channel-target}, and hence its stronger area bound,
is false. The frequency, material interval, square and thresholds
are common to the whole sequence of devices.
\end{theorem}
The strict inequality $\eta<\delta$ leaves room to choose an intermediate
power $p_0$: in the limiting construction the passage and absolute bypass
both approach $p_0$ for every unit input in the counted subspace.  The proof
then chooses a finite device close enough to this limit to satisfy both
inequalities.  Since the complete lossless scattering map preserves incident
power, the throughput condition holds for every $\tau<1$.  This does not
require that power leaving on a particular side be collected by a receiver.

The order of limits matters.  For each requested dimension, first choose
the independent incident data; then increase the stack gain while shrinking
the spheres; finally take the stack radius large enough to approximate the
limiting Green kernel for that particular choice.  No single device or
uniform radius is claimed to work for all dimensions at once.

\begin{proof}
Choose once and for all an index $1<q<\sqrt{\eps_+}$, a sphere
permittivity $1<\eps_p\leq\eps_+$, and constants $h>\rho>0$.
Choose $p_0\in(\eta,\delta)$ and then $t>0$ so that
$\alpha t^2/(2\pi h)=p_0$, where
$\alpha=4\pi\rho^3(\eps_p-1)/(\eps_p+2)>0$.
These choices do not depend on $N$.

Fix $N$. Lemmas~\ref{ct:jets}--\ref{ct:gram} supply $N$ distinct
right-edge points and their left-edge partners, a finite band of
regular electric-wall TE interpolants $v_j$, and $\lambda>0$ such
that $g_j=v_j/\sqrt\lambda$ are orthonormal physical incoming data
with the prescribed independent jets. Let
\[
 B_M=q^M,\qquad a_M=t\sqrt\lambda/B_M,\qquad \ell_0=k_0^{-1}.
\]
Thus $a_M\to0$ and the actual common core gain $B_M$ produces the
jet multiplier $A_M=B_M/\sqrt\lambda=t/a_M$.
Place the $2N$ radius-$\rho a_M\ell_0$ spheres at height
$h a_M\ell_0$ above the chosen edge points. For large $M$ they are
disjoint, lie strictly above the plane, and remain inside a fixed
ball. Their arrangement is invariant under rotation by $\pi$ about
the $z$ axis.

For each such $M$, Theorem~\ref{cl:remainder} proves invertibility
of the actual inclusion volume equation for the explicit limiting
background kernel with gain $B_M$. It gives, uniformly for
$c\in\mathbb C^N$ with $\|c\|=1$,
\[
 \|S_{z,M}^{\infty}(c)-F_{a_M}(c)\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^2)}
       \leq C_N a_M.
\]
The constant is independent of $M$ once $N$ and its interpolants
are fixed. This estimate includes the self-consistent polarization,
all source angular orders, all coherent cross terms, and the entire
measurement plane.

Now hold $M$ fixed. Take a common radial stack of $M$ high-low
quarter-wave pairs, with indices $q$ and one, starting at
dimensionless radius $R=2\pi n$. The physical radius is $R/k_0$.
Theorem~\ref{rg:physical-limit} proves both convergence of its
source Green kernel to that used above and convergence in whole-plane
$L^1$ of the resulting \emph{actual perturbed-device} fluxes, uniformly
on this finite input space. Inverse stability supplies the actual
dielectric polarizations, not arbitrary imposed dipole sources.
Choose a sufficiently large finite integer $n=n(M,N)$ so that
\[
 \sup_{\|c\|=1}
 \|S_{z,M}^{\rm phys}(c)-S_{z,M}^{\infty}(c)\|_1<a_M.
\]
One harmless common phase in the incident normalization does not
affect flux or orthonormality. This order of choices requires no
radius estimate uniform in $M$ or $N$.

Combining the last two estimates with Proposition~\ref{ct:model}
gives uniformly on the unit sphere of $\mathbb C^N$
\[
 P_D^{\rm phys}(c)\longrightarrow p_0,\qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R^2\setminus D}|S_{z,M}^{\rm phys}(c)|\,dA
       \longrightarrow p_0 .
\]
Here the absolute-value conclusion uses
$\bigl||f|-|g|\bigr|\leq|f-g|$, so cancellation between distinct
inputs or between distant parts of the plane is not substituted for
the bypass condition. Choose a finite $M$ large enough that both
errors are less than
$\tfrac12\min\{p_0-\eta,\delta-p_0\}$.

The resulting medium consists only of finitely many concentric
ordinary dielectric shells and the separated ordinary dielectric
spheres. It is compactly supported and piecewise smooth. The
volume-equation inverse just proved gives its well-posed response
for the prescribed illumination. Equivalently, outgoing uniqueness
for this finite positive dielectric follows from the imaginary
Green identity, outgoing Rellich theorem and interface unique
continuation; the exterior zero field propagates through the finite
smooth interfaces. The volume solution defines a bounded physical
scattering map on transverse angular $L^2$ inputs. Choose as
$\mathcal H_{\rm in}$ the closed subspace generated by the finite regular
TE inputs used here, and keep the complete angular outgoing space.
On every finite TE input, the exact lossless power identity is
$\|Sa\|^2=\|a\|^2$. Continuity extends it to the closed input space.
That space is a Hilbert space, since transversality and the chosen
subspace are closed in angular $L^2$. Thus the actual restricted
scattering map $T=S|_{\mathcal H_{\rm in}}$ satisfies $T^*T=I$ and
includes every outgoing radiation channel. No surjectivity assertion
is needed.

Set $W=\operatorname{span}\{g_1,\ldots,g_N\}$. Its dimension is
$N$ and input power is $\|c\|^2$. For unit $c$ the preceding
strict margins prove the passage and bypass inequalities, while
$\|Tc\|^2=1\geq\tau$ proves throughput. Quadratic homogeneity
extends them to every $c$, including zero. The spheres leave a
positive vacuum gap around $\overline D$ for each individual
device; the remote shells lie outside that neighborhood. The
proved plane-tail bounds give legitimate integrable fluxes even
where the plane intersects remote interfaces, whose circles have
zero plane measure. All admissibility conditions are satisfied.
Since $N$ was arbitrary with the displayed fixed parameters,
no finite device-independent count can hold there.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope and physical mechanism]\label{uc:scope}
The flux near each sphere circulates across a sharp edge of the
measurement square: its positive interior contribution is paired
with an exterior return of the same limiting magnitude. This is
allowed by the frozen definition, which does not require the
square to be a sole connection or the selected output to lie
above it. The construction uses the strict allowance $\delta>\eta$;
it does not refute a reformulated claim with smaller bypass or
additional output restrictions. In particular the resource-controlled
theorems requiring $\delta<\eta\tau$ retain their stated scope.
The total support, common core amplification, interface count and
inverse minimum feature size grow along the construction. None is
a bounded resource in Conjecture~\ref{pr:channel-target}.
The proof is analytic. Its complete physical construction and the
same-device passivity identity are formalized in the endpoint listed
in Section~\ref{ver:lean}; no finite-element simulation is claimed.
\end{remark}
