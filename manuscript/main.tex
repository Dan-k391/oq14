\begin{abstract}
How tightly can optical fields be concentrated, and how many independent
power-carrying channels can pass through a fixed section of a dielectric
device?  We study explicit versions of these questions motivated by
Miller and Monticone's OQ14. With a common positive permittivity bound,
we construct arbitrarily many simultaneous localized electric-field peaks
in dielectric scattering at a fixed driving frequency, and in a single
normalized outgoing mode of a finite smooth dielectric. The modal
construction allows a prescribed real part of the complex resonance
frequency and arbitrarily high prescribed quality factor. For channels, we show that the area of a
fixed square does not impose a uniform finite count under our unrestricted
definition: for every finite count there is a device meeting the passage
and absolute-bypass tests for every coherent input, provided the allowed
bypass threshold exceeds the required passage threshold.  The selected
output in this construction is the complete scattering map.  Stronger
bypass requirements and specified receiving-only outputs are separate
versions of the question, which we do not settle here.  Lean checks the continuum Maxwell
constructions and the stated modal-volume limits; Section~\ref{ver:lean}
describes the formal endpoints and the scope of those checks.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Reading guide.} The introduction explains the two questions
and summarizes the results before stating their precise definitions. Status
words refer to the labeled statement, not to every possible interpretation
of OQ14. Section~\ref{ver:lean} specifies the formal coverage;
Section~\ref{ver:verdicts} records the separate conclusions and limitations.
